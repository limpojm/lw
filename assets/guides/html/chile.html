Investor Entry Routes

  • InvestChile, the national investment promotion agency established under Law 20,848, connects investors with opportunities and issues foreign investor certificates online within up to 15 working days; it tracked a $65.7 billion foreign project portfolio at end-2025
  • Law 20,848 (Foreign Investment Promotion Law) guaranteeing equal, non-discriminatory treatment of foreign and domestic investors, with prior authorisation generally required only in a few areas such as hydrocarbons and nuclear energy
  • Free capital movement under Chapter XIV of the Central Bank's Compendium of Foreign Exchange Regulations (new version in force from 1 January 2026): transfers above $10,000 go through the formal exchange market and are reported, with no restriction on repatriating capital or profits
  • The 2026 pro-growth reform package approved by Congress on 4 August 2026, which lowers corporate tax gradually from 27% to 23% by 2029 and streamlines environmental permitting, with some provisions under Constitutional Court review
  • Lithium participation through partnership with state companies under CORFO contracts, as with NovaAndino Litio, the Codelco–SQM joint venture operating the Salar de Atacama until 2060
  • A network of 34 trade agreements covering more than 60 economies, alongside double taxation treaties that reduce the 35% additional tax on remitted profits

Market-Entry Observation

Chile in October 2026 offers one of Latin America's most open and institutionally stable investment environments, anchored by an A-range credit rating (Fitch A-, S&P A, Moody's A2) and an independent central bank. Exports reached a record $107 billion in 2025, up 7.9%, led by mining exports of $63.3 billion, and foreign direct investment rose 13% to $14.2 billion. The investment pipeline is strengthening: InvestChile's foreign project portfolio grew 16.8% to $65.7 billion at end-2025, led by energy, and the Capital Goods Corporation's 2026–2030 pipeline rose 32% in the first quarter of 2026 to $87.7 billion, driven by mining. President José Antonio Kast's reform package, approved by Congress in August 2026, cuts corporate tax from 27% to 23% by 2029 and speeds up permitting, and the Central Bank projects investment growth of 8.1% in 2027.

Chile's main near-term task is converting this investment pipeline into renewed growth. Activity was flat in the first half of 2026 (GDP -0.2% year-on-year in Q2), as large mining projects completed and ore grades weighed on output, and the Central Bank has set its 2026 growth range at 0.25–0.75% before a recovery to 2–3% in 2027. Inflation of 4.1% in September reflects higher fuel prices linked to the Middle East conflict, while core inflation is steady at around 3.3% and the policy rate is held at 4.5%. Chile's buffers are substantial: copper prices above $6.50 per pound support export earnings, the current account is projected in surplus in 2026, and gross public debt is projected to peak at around 44% of GDP, below the government's 45% prudence threshold. For investors, the combination of a cyclical low, lower taxes ahead and a deep project pipeline creates an attractive entry point.

Investor Risk Note

Chile's risk profile is that of a well-governed, investment-grade economy passing through a cyclical slowdown rather than a structural weakness. An independent central bank, free capital movement, non-discriminatory treatment of foreign investors under Law 20,848 and A-range sovereign ratings provide a predictable framework, and 2025 delivered record exports and a 13% rise in FDI. The 2026 slowdown reflects the completion of large mining projects and lower ore grades, while the investment pipeline has expanded sharply and the Central Bank expects growth to recover to 2–3% in 2027. The main points to plan for are the phased rollout of the 2026 reform package, with some provisions under Constitutional Court review, and US tariff measures on certain non-copper exports. Both are manageable with sound legal advice and Chile's broad trade-agreement network, and the reform package's lower corporate tax and faster permitting point to an improving environment over the medium term.

Market Access

Chile has one of the world's most extensive trade-agreement networks, with 34 agreements in force covering more than 60 economies, including a free trade agreement with the United States since 2004, the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), the Pacific Alliance and an Interim Trade Agreement with the European Union that entered into force in 2025. It is a founding member of the WTO. China is the main export destination, particularly for copper and fruit, followed by the United States and the European Union. Mining accounted for 59% of goods exports in 2025, with copper concentrates alone reaching $36.3 billion, while fresh cherries ($3.4 billion) lead a record fruit sector. Chile's Pacific ports provide direct links to Asian markets, positioning it as a natural gateway between South America and the Asia-Pacific.

Current International Business Channels

The British Chamber of Commerce in Chile (Britcham), the American Chamber of Commerce in Chile (AmCham Chile) and the Confederation of Production and Commerce (CPC), the leading national business federation, are the main channels for business liaison, while the National Mining Society (Sonami) and the Capital Goods Corporation (CBC) provide sector intelligence on the mining and project pipeline. InvestChile runs investor outreach and sector briefings, and ProChile supports export-oriented partnerships. The World Bank, the Inter-American Development Bank and IDB Invest are active in infrastructure, energy transition and private-sector development, and Chile's deep capital markets and investment-grade sovereign provide a strong base for project finance.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Chile suits long-term strategic investors seeking a stable, rules-based gateway to South America and the Asia-Pacific: mining and mining-services companies targeting copper, productivity technology and processing; energy investors in solar, wind, battery storage and green hydrogen, which lead InvestChile's portfolio; lithium and battery-supply-chain investors able to partner with state companies; infrastructure investors drawn to concessions and port and transmission projects; and global services and technology firms using Santiago as a regional hub. Agri-food and aquaculture investors benefit from Chile's counter-seasonal position and wide trade-agreement network.
