The economy today

Guinea in October 2026 is one of Africa's fastest-growing economies, as Simandou iron ore exports ramp up alongside record bauxite output. GDP grew by around 7% in 2025, and the World Bank projects 8.8% growth in 2026 and 11.6% in 2027. Foreign direct investment rose more than fivefold to about $8 billion in 2025, according to UNCTAD, and bauxite exports hit a record 114.8 million tonnes in H1 2026. Macroeconomic buffers are strengthening: reserves rose to $4.5 billion (4.8 months of imports) in Q1 2026, inflation was 5.9% in September, and the BCRG cut its policy rate to 9.5% in March. S&P moved its B+ rating to a positive outlook in March 2026, constitutional order was restored through the 2025–2026 elections, ECOWAS lifted all remaining sanctions, and an IMF staff-level agreement on a $425 million, 41-month programme was reached in August 2026.

Guinea's central structural challenge is to turn its mineral wealth into broad-based, diversified growth while giving investors a predictable framework. Around 95% of exports are mining products, and since 2024 the government has pursued a more assertive mining policy: it revoked 129 expired exploration permits in 2025 and transferred the GAC bauxite concession to the state-backed Nimba Mining Company, with some disputes, including an ICSID claim by Axis Minerals, still pending. The direction of travel is constructive: Guinea reached an amicable settlement with Emirates Global Aluminium in May 2026, the IMF programme supports mining revenue transparency and governance reforms, and a sovereign fund is planned to save part of the mineral windfall. For investors, the government's priority on local processing, power generation and agriculture creates substantial opportunity in alumina refining, energy, logistics and agri-food value chains.

Where are the opportunities?

Conakry

Capital and principal commercial port city; seat of government, the central bank and the investment agency, and within the Investment Code's Zone A

Financial and business services, port logistics, construction, consumer markets

Boké / Kamsar (northwest)

Heart of the world-class bauxite industry, home to the leading exporters and the Kamsar port, with a proposed LNG terminal under consideration

Bauxite mining, alumina refining, port and marine services, energy

Simandou range (southeast)

Site of the Simandou iron ore project, which began exporting in late 2025 and is ramping up towards 60 million tonnes a year for the Rio Tinto SimFer blocks by H2 2028

Iron ore, mining services, supply chains, local content

A new 600-km main railway, a 70-km mine spur and coastal port facilities linking the interior to global markets

How do I get in?

Agence de Promotion des Investissements Privés (APIP), the national investment agency, operating the one-stop shop (Guichet Unique) for company registration, Investment Code benefits and review of large projects

Investment Code (Law L/2015/008/AN of 25 May 2015), open to foreign investors in most sectors, with duty exemptions and two- to three-year tax holidays varying between Zone A (Conakry and western prefectures) and Zone B (rest of the country)

Mining Code (2011, amended by Law L/2013/053) and negotiated mining conventions, administered by the Ministry of Mines and Geology, with government priority on domestic processing of bauxite into alumina

Investor protection through ICSID membership (since 1968) and bilateral investment treaties, such as the Guinea–Germany treaty in force since 2014

Banque Centrale de la République de Guinée (BCRG) management of the Guinean franc; mining and gold exporters must repatriate a share of export proceeds (60% for gold), and the new IMF-supported programme promotes greater exchange-rate flexibility

Simandou 2040 programme of 122 megaprojects and 36 reforms, targeting over $200 billion of investment by 2040, with a planned Sovereign Fund of Guinea designed in line with the Santiago Principles

Energy and hydrocarbons entry through independent power projects (including plans for up to 500 MW of solar) and a licensing round covering 22 onshore and offshore oil blocks

Trade and market access

Guinea is a member of ECOWAS, which lifted all remaining sanctions and fully reintegrated the country in January 2026 and of the Mano River Union with Sierra Leone and Liberia. It is a WTO member and participates in the African Continental Free Trade Area. China is the dominant export market, taking a record 74% of bauxite shipments in 2025 and all Simandou iron ore, while India and the UAE are also major export destinations. The new Simandou railway and ports, alongside Kamsar and the Port of Conakry, are expanding export capacity, and the World Bank encourages using the Simandou corridor to connect farmers and producers to domestic and regional markets. The Guinea–Mali power interconnection within the West African Power Pool will further strengthen regional links.

The overall risk picture

Guinea's risk profile reflects a resource-rich economy moving rapidly into a higher-growth phase, with strengthening institutions and external buffers. The restoration of constitutional order, full reintegration into ECOWAS and the African Union, a positive S&P outlook and a new IMF-supported programme together mark a substantial improvement in the investment environment since 2024. The principal consideration for investors is regulatory: the state has taken a firmer approach to mining licences and domestic processing, and some disputes remain before international tribunals, although the May 2026 settlement with EGA shows a willingness to resolve differences amicably. The political environment remains centred on the presidency, with limited opposition participation in the 2026 legislative vote, so sound compliance and stakeholder engagement are advisable. Investors who align with national priorities in processing, energy and diversification, structure projects with clear conventions and ICSID protection, and plan prudently for currency and logistics conditions are well placed to share in one of Africa's strongest growth stories.

Who should I talk to first?

Presidency of the Republic (Mamadi Doumbouya)

Sets national strategy, including the Simandou 2040 programme, mining policy and domestic-processing priorities

Primary reference point for strategic direction and large-scale project alignment

Agence de Promotion des Investissements Privés (APIP)

National investment agency running the one-stop shop and Investment Code approvals

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentive guidance

Banque Centrale de la République de Guinée (BCRG)

Monetary authority managing the Guinean franc, reserves and export-proceed repatriation rules

Relevant for currency, banking and treasury planning

Ministry of Mines and Geology (Bouna Sylla)

Grants and reviews mining titles and negotiates mining conventions, including refining commitments

Essential counterpart for any mining or mining-services investment

Ministry of Economy, Finance and Budget (Mariama Ciré Sylla)

Leads the IMF programme, fiscal policy and the design of the Sovereign Fund of Guinea

Key for tax, fiscal-stability and co-investment discussions

Ministry of Plan and International Cooperation (Ismaël Nabé)

Coordinates development partners and the Simandou 2040 project pipeline

Useful for PPPs and DFI-backed infrastructure projects

Simandou partners (Rio Tinto SimFer and Winning Consortium Simandou)

Operators of Guinea's largest mining, rail and port development, with major procurement needs

Key counterparts for suppliers, logistics and services investors

Business networks

APIP is the main facilitation channel for foreign investors and has hosted successive editions of the Guinea Investment Forum in Conakry. Guinea's ministers also promote the country at international events, such as the Invest in African Energy Forum in Paris in 2026. The World Bank Group approved a new partnership framework and $293 million of financing in June 2026, and the IMF programme anchors wider donor engagement, creating co-financing openings with development finance institutions. Major mining operators, including Rio Tinto, Chalco, Société Minière de Boké and CBG, maintain large in-country teams and are a valuable source of practical intelligence on logistics, procurement and local content.

Who this market suits

Guinea suits investors with a long-term horizon and an appetite for frontier growth: mining and metals companies and their suppliers, particularly those able to support bauxite refining and Simandou's ramp-up; infrastructure and energy investors in hydropower, solar, transmission, rail and ports; agribusiness and food-processing investors building on agriculture's large share of output and jobs; and DFIs and co-investors partnering with the state on the Simandou 2040 programme. Investors should be comfortable with negotiated conventions, active government policy in natural resources and a market that is still building its financial depth.
