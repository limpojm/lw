The economy today

Singapore in October 2026 is delivering growth well above its long-run potential on the back of strong global demand for AI-related electronics. GDP grew 5.0% in 2025 and accelerated through 2026, rising 6.3% in Q1, 6.2% in Q2 and 5.4% in Q3 (advance estimate), led by manufacturing (+7.5%), construction (+8.8%) and trade-related services. The government raised its 2026 growth forecast to 4.5–5.5% in August, while MAS Core Inflation is projected at a moderate 1.5–2.0% for the year, allowing MAS to keep its exchange-rate policy unchanged in October. Foreign direct investment inflows reached about $151 billion in 2025 according to UNCTAD data, the largest in Southeast Asia, and EDB secured S$14.2 billion of fixed asset investment commitments, mostly in manufacturing. Budget 2026 reinforced this momentum with a national AI push, S$37 billion for research and innovation under RIE2030 and enhanced enterprise support.

As one of the world's most open economies, with annual trade of about S$2.5 trillion, Singapore's main structural consideration is its exposure to global trade policy and imported energy. Since 24 July 2026 the US has applied a 12.5% Section 301 tariff to about one-third of Singapore's US-bound exports, worth around S$9.5 billion, although semiconductors, pharmaceuticals, energy products and selected electronics and aerospace goods are exempt. The government is engaging Washington carefully while protecting Singapore's wider trade relationships, and the economy continues to grow strongly. Singapore's buffers are exceptional: official foreign reserves reached S$553.6 billion in September 2026, the highest since early 2022, and the budget remains in surplus. Its network of free trade agreements, its role as a regional hub and progress on low-carbon electricity imports create opportunities for investors in supply-chain diversification, energy transition and regional headquarters operations.

How do I get in?

Economic Development Board (EDB) as the lead agency for foreign investors, offering facilitation, site selection and incentive packages for manufacturing, headquarters, R&D and services projects

Enterprise Singapore for trade, SME partnerships and market-access support, with Budget 2026 raising Market Readiness Assistance support to 70% until March 2029

Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) licensing for banking, capital markets, payments and fund management, including the Variable Capital Company (VCC) fund structure

Open capital account with no exchange controls; profits, dividends and capital can be repatriated freely and the Singapore dollar is managed against a trade-weighted basket

Fast company incorporation through ACRA, an extensive double-taxation treaty network and an internationally recognised commercial court and arbitration ecosystem (SICC, SIAC)

JTC Corporation industrial land and purpose-built estates (Jurong Island, Tuas, one-north, Seletar) for manufacturing, logistics and R&D investors

Talent pathways through the Employment Pass, ONE Pass and Tech.Pass, noting updated Employment Pass and S Pass salary thresholds for new applicants from January 2027

Trade and market access

Singapore is a founding member of ASEAN and the ASEAN Economic Community, a WTO member, and a party to both the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) and the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP). It also has bilateral free trade agreements with major partners including the US, the EU, the UK, China, Japan and India, as well as pioneering digital economy agreements. Its main export markets are in Asia, notably China, Malaysia, Hong Kong and Indonesia, together with the US and Europe. As a leading global transhipment and bunkering hub, and with Changi Airport connecting it across Asia and beyond, Singapore provides investors with unmatched access to the fast-growing ASEAN market and wider Asia.

The overall risk picture

Singapore offers one of the lowest-risk investment environments in the world, combining political stability, strong rule of law, an open capital account and exceptional financial buffers, with official foreign reserves of S$553.6 billion and a fiscal surplus. Growth has been strong through 2026, supported by AI-related manufacturing, and inflation remains moderate. The main considerations are external: the 12.5% US tariff on part of Singapore's exports, US-China technology competition, elevated energy prices and high domestic costs. Each is manageable through product-level tariff review, robust export-control compliance, energy-efficient operations and a hub-and-spoke model that pairs Singapore with lower-cost regional locations such as the JS-SEZ. For most investors, Singapore's predictability and connectivity remain its defining advantages.

Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry (formerly MTI)

Relevant for trade policy, tariff developments and energy-transition opportunities

Useful partner for joint ventures with local firms and regional expansion

JTC Corporation

Key counterpart for manufacturing, logistics and R&D site requirements

Temasek and GIC

Major state investment companies with significant domestic and global portfolios

Potential co-investors and partners for large-scale projects

Business networks

The Singapore Business Federation is the apex business chamber, alongside the British Chamber of Commerce Singapore, the American Chamber of Commerce in Singapore, the European Chamber of Commerce and other national chambers that provide practical market intelligence and advocacy. EDB and Enterprise Singapore run regular sector briefings and investor programmes, and joint investment forums with Malaysia support entry into the JS-SEZ. Major events such as Singapore FinTech Festival and Singapore International Energy Week offer efficient access to policymakers and partners. Singapore also hosts the regional headquarters of many development finance institutions, multilateral banks and global investors, making it a natural base for deal origination across Southeast Asia.

Who this market suits

Singapore suits investors seeking a stable, well-regulated base for Asian operations: financial-services, asset and wealth managers using MAS licensing and VCC structures; technology, AI and semiconductor companies drawn by strong demand and government support; advanced manufacturing, biomedical and specialty chemicals investors; and maritime, aviation and logistics firms building on Singapore's hub status. It is also well suited to multinationals establishing regional headquarters, and to energy-transition investors targeting low-carbon fuels and electricity imports. Investors with cost-sensitive operations can combine a Singapore headquarters with production in the JS-SEZ or wider ASEAN.
