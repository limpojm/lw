Investor Entry Routes

  • Agence de Promotion des Investissements en Mauritanie (APIM), the national investment promotion agency, operating a digitised one-stop shop (Guichet Unique) for company registration and work permits
  • Investment Code (Law No. 2025-006 of 19 February 2025), strengthening protection against expropriation, guaranteeing foreign-currency availability and capital transfers, and offering tiered incentives such as reduced import duties on equipment and training tax credits
  • Green Hydrogen Code (Law No. 2024-037), under which large projects sign a global convention setting long-term fiscal and contractual terms; the AMAN convention signed in September 2026 was the first
  • Nouadhibou Free Zone, operating under its own legal and customs framework for port, fisheries and industrial investment
  • Banque Centrale de Mauritanie oversight of the ouguiya (MRU) and an expanding interbank foreign-exchange market, with transactions up 36% to $1.76 billion in 2025
  • Partnerships with state companies SMH (hydrocarbons) and SNIM (iron ore) for participation in the gas and mining value chains

Market-Entry Observation

Mauritania in October 2026 combines steady growth, sound macroeconomic management and a new generation of energy projects. Real GDP grew 4.0% in 2025 after 6.3% in 2024, with the non-extractive economy expanding 5.5%, and the IMF projects 4.7% growth in 2026. Exports rose 12.3% to almost $4.3 billion in 2025, helped by the first LNG shipments from the Greater Tortue Ahmeyim project ($237 million), narrowing the trade deficit to $23 million, while foreign-exchange reserves rose 12.6% to $2.21 billion and external debt eased to 37% of GDP. FDI totalled $634 million in 2025 as GTA's first-phase construction was completed, taking the inward stock to $8.2 billion. A new Investment Code in force since 2025, a dedicated Green Hydrogen Code, and a new 42-month IMF programme approved in June 2026 after all end-2025 targets were met give investors a clear and supportive framework.

Mauritania's main structural challenge is its reliance on extractive exports: gold, iron ore and fish account for around 90% of goods exports, so activity and revenues follow commodity prices and mine output, and extractive GDP dipped 1.2% in 2025 as planned lower gold grades at Tasiast took effect. The country is broadening this base in practical ways: GTA LNG reached nameplate capacity in December 2025, SNIM is targeting 15.5 million tonnes in 2026 on the way to 45 million tonnes of capacity by 2031, and the AMAN green hydrogen convention opens a new export industry. Policy buffers are solid, with a fiscal rule now enshrined in law, a budget deficit of only 0.3% of GDP in 2025 and a central bank that acted early, raising its policy rate to 6.5% in May 2026 as inflation rose to 4.8% in June. Each of these shifts creates openings for investors in gas services, mining logistics, renewables and food production.

Investor Risk Note

Mauritania's risk profile is that of a stable, well-managed economy with improving buffers and a growing pipeline of energy investment. Reserves rose to $2.21 billion at end-2025, the budget is close to balance, around 70% of external debt is owed to multilateral lenders, and the country met all end-2025 IMF targets before securing a new 42-month programme in June 2026. The 2025 Investment Code and the Green Hydrogen Code provide clear legal footing, while the start of LNG exports and record iron ore output show that large projects can be delivered. The main considerations are commodity concentration, the recent rise in inflation and the security situation in neighbouring Mali; Mauritania's record of internal stability, its prompt monetary response and strong international partnerships help mitigate each of these. Investors should apply standard commodity-price and security planning, particularly for projects near the eastern border.

Market Access

Mauritania has been a WTO member since 1995 and was among the first countries to ratify the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), opening tariff preferences across the continent. It is a member of the Arab Maghreb Union, signed an association agreement with ECOWAS in 2017 to ease trade with West Africa, and as a least-developed country enjoys duty-free access to the EU under Everything But Arms. The EU is its main partner, and principal export destinations include Canada, China, Europe, Algeria and Switzerland. Iron ore leaves through Nouadhibou, LNG is loaded offshore at the GTA hub, and the Rosso crossing links the country by road to Senegal, giving Mauritania a natural position as a bridge between the Maghreb and West Africa.

Current International Business Channels

The National Union of Mauritanian Employers (UNPM) is the main private-sector body and co-organised a US business mission to Nouakchott in October 2026 covering energy, mining, infrastructure, agriculture, fisheries and the digital economy, while the Chamber of Commerce, Industry and Agriculture of Mauritania provides domestic business liaison. The Mauritanides mining and energy conference and the Nouakchott National Energy Forum are useful meeting points for the resources sector. The European Union, Mauritania's main partner, supports renewable energy, green hydrogen and vocational training, and the IMF, World Bank, IFC and African Development Bank maintain active programmes; the IFC supported the design of the 2025 Investment Code. Operators such as bp, Kosmos Energy and Kinross offer practical insight into operating conditions in the gas and mining sectors.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Mauritania best suits investors with a long-term view and sector expertise: energy and oilfield-services companies positioned for GTA operations and future phases; mining, rail and port-logistics investors partnering with SNIM and international gold producers; renewable-energy and green-hydrogen developers drawn by world-class solar and wind resources and the Green Hydrogen Code; and fisheries, agribusiness and food-processing investors serving domestic and West African demand. Development finance institutions and impact investors will find strong openings in power, water and infrastructure, while all investors should build standard commodity-price and regional security planning into their projects.
