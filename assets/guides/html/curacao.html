Investor Entry Routes

  • Curaçao Investment and Export Promotion Agency (CINEX) acts as the first point of contact for investors and runs targeted outreach such as the Curaçao Investment Roundtable in Miami in April 2026
  • Economic zones (e-zones) taxing qualifying profits at 2% for companies with real presence that trade or serve clients abroad, with local sales capped at 25% of turnover and subject to approval
  • Curinde, 85% government-owned, manages the Harbour and Airport Free Economic Zones and Brievengat Industrial Park, offering duty-free storage, assembly and re-export
  • Caribbean guilder (Cg), introduced on 31 March 2025 and fixed at Cg 1.79 = $1.00, managed by the Centrale Bank van Curaçao en Sint Maarten (CBCS), which also licenses and supervises financial institutions
  • Energy-logistics entry through 2BAYS and its subsidiary Curaçao Refinery Utilities (CRU), which operate the Bullenbaai terminal; any Venezuela-linked activity must comply with US OFAC General License 46 conditions
  • Online gaming licences issued solely by the Curaçao Gaming Authority under the National Ordinance on Games of Chance (LOK), in force since 24 December 2024, replacing the former master-licence system
  • Listing and capital-raising through the Dutch Caribbean Securities Exchange (DCSX), which signed a co-promotion agreement with CINEX in March 2025

Market-Entry Observation

Curaçao enters late 2026 with solid momentum. Preliminary official figures show real GDP growth of 5.1% in 2025, led by accommodation and food services (+10.8%), financial services (+9.8%) and construction (+9.4%), and the government's mid-2026 projections, based on CBCS forecasts, point to further growth of 2.7% in 2026 with inflation of about 2.4%. Stay-over arrivals reached a record 788,427 in 2025 (+13%) and rose a further 9% in the first half of 2026, while cruise passengers totalled 881,665 in 2025. Public finances have improved markedly: consecutive budget surpluses have reduced central government debt from 90.3% of GDP in 2021 to 64.7% in 2024, with the IMF projecting around 60% for 2025, and tax receipts in the first half of 2026 were about Cg 98 million above the previous year. The external position has strengthened too, with the monetary union's current account deficit narrowing from 17% to 8% of GDP in 2025 and reserves covering 5.5 months of imports in May 2026.

Curaçao's main structural challenge is diversifying beyond tourism after the closure of the Isla refinery in 2019, which for a century anchored the island's industry. The government is turning this into an opportunity by shifting toward a service-oriented energy model based on terminals, storage and logistics: the Bullenbaai terminal has received multiple crude tankers since the start of 2026 and loaded close to 2 million barrels for China, India and Europe in May 2026. US OFAC General License 46, issued in January 2026, creates a lawful but tightly conditioned channel for Venezuelan-origin crude logistics. Beyond energy, the IMF points to yachting, maritime services, data centres, renewable energy and new South American markets as diversification routes, supported by the dollar peg, a fiscal rule, rising reserves and Curaçao's place within the Kingdom of the Netherlands, each creating openings for early movers.

Investor Risk Note

Curaçao's risk profile is that of a stable island economy with a currency fixed to the US dollar, improving public finances and the institutional anchor of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Debt has fallen by roughly a third relative to GDP since 2021, budgets are guided by a fiscal rule, reserves have reached their strongest level in years and the banking sector is broadly sound. The main considerations for investors are reliance on tourism, the transition from refining to an energy-services model and continued work on the AML/CFT framework following the 2025 CFATF evaluation. Investors in oil logistics should note plainly that dealings involving Venezuelan-origin crude are lawful only within the strict conditions of US OFAC General License 46, and should take specialist sanctions advice before committing. With record visitor numbers, a revived Bullenbaai terminal and a clear diversification agenda, the outlook remains constructive.

Market Access

Curaçao is an autonomous constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands; it is not part of the EU but is associated with it as an Overseas Country and Territory (OCT), giving its goods preferential access to the EU market, and its nationals hold Dutch and therefore EU citizenship. It participates in the WTO through the Kingdom of the Netherlands and shares a monetary union and central bank with Sint Maarten. The United States and the Netherlands are the main suppliers of imports, while goods exports go mainly to Bonaire, the Netherlands, Aruba and the United States. Curaçao's principal export is services, led by tourism, financial services and port activity. Its deep-water harbour, free zones and proximity to the Venezuelan coast make it a natural hub between North America, South America and Europe for logistics and energy storage.

Current International Business Channels

CINEX is the main organiser of investor engagement, hosting closed-door roundtables abroad such as the April 2026 Miami event with Banco Sabadell and partnering with the Curaçao Tourist Board on the CHICOS hotel investment conference held in Curaçao in November 2025. The Curaçao Chamber of Commerce and Industry is the business registry and liaison body, and the Dutch Caribbean Securities Exchange co-promotes the island's financial sector with CINEX. Kingdom channels, including the Dutch government and the Kingdom's financial-supervision framework, are an important route into larger public projects, while the island's OCT status links it to EU cooperation programmes. The IMF's regular Article IV reviews provide an independent reference on policy direction.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Curaçao suits investors seeking a dollar-pegged jurisdiction within the Kingdom of the Netherlands with a growing services economy: hotel, resort and residential-tourism developers serving record visitor demand from Europe and the Americas; energy-logistics, storage and marine-services companies able to work within US sanctions conditions at Bullenbaai; distribution and e-commerce businesses using the 2% e-zone regime and Curinde's free zones; and renewable-energy, storage and data-centre investors aligned with the IMF's diversification agenda. Financial-services, fintech and licensed gaming operators with strong compliance frameworks, and maritime and yachting businesses, can also find attractive openings.
