Investor Entry Routes

  • Seychelles Investment Board (SIB), established in 2004 as the national single gateway for investment promotion and facilitation, helping investors obtain permits, licences and incentives
  • Seychelles Investment Act 2010 and the Seychelles Investment (Economic Activities) Regulations 2022, which list activities open to foreign investors, those requiring a Seychellois partner with equity caps, and an economic needs test for others
  • Seychelles International Trade Zone (SITZ), under the SITZ Act 1995, offering full foreign ownership, customs duty exemptions on certain capital equipment and certain tax exemptions
  • Financial Services Authority (FSA) licensing for international business companies, funds, fund administrators and SITZ activities
  • An open foreign-exchange regime with no foreign exchange restrictions, no capital gains or withholding tax, and investor protection through the ICSID and New York Conventions and an Investment Appeal Panel
  • Tourism-sector entry through SIB, with hotels of more than 25 rooms open to foreign investment and smaller hotels and restaurants subject to foreign equity caps
  • Blue-economy channels, building on the world's first sovereign blue bond (2018) and a debt-for-nature swap that funded marine protection

Market-Entry Observation

Seychelles has the highest GNI per capita in Africa and a strong record of reform. Real GDP grew by 5.8% in 2025, according to the World Bank and IMF staff, driven by record tourist arrivals. Public debt fell to 53.6% of GDP in 2025, towards a medium-term anchor of 50%, and Fitch upgraded the sovereign to BB with a stable outlook in September 2025, citing tourism performance. In May 2026 the IMF Executive Board completed the final reviews of Seychelles' Extended Fund Facility and Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Inflation was 0.9% through August 2026, the Central Bank held its policy rate at 1.75% throughout 2025 and into the second quarter of 2026, and reserves cover about four months of imports. President Patrick Herminie, sworn in on 26 October 2025, leads a government focused on economic diversification.

Seychelles' main structural challenge is its reliance on tourism and on long-haul air connections: the IMF notes that about 60% of arrivals come via Doha, Dubai or Abu Dhabi. The 2026 Middle East conflict disrupted these hubs, and March arrivals fell by 37.2% year on year, so IMF staff now expect growth of about 1% in 2026. The response has been measured: the rupee and the external position have stayed broadly stable, financial-sector stress appears limited, and IMF staff report a nascent tourism recovery. The episode strengthens the case for diversification into fisheries processing, port services, blue finance and renewable energy, all areas with established institutions and development-bank support.

Investor Risk Note

Seychelles' risk profile is that of a well-managed, high-income small island state facing an external tourism shock. The 2026 slowdown came from outside the economy, as the Middle East conflict disrupted the Gulf air hubs through which most visitors travel, and it followed a record 2025 with growth of 5.8%. The policy response has been steady: the policy rate has been held at 1.75%, reserves cover about four months of imports, debt has fallen to 53.6% of GDP and the IMF programmes were completed in May 2026, with a Post-Financing Assessment due in December 2026. A nascent tourism recovery is under way and the shock has sharpened the focus on diversifying air links and the wider economy. Tourism investors should plan for route and source-market variability, while fisheries, port, renewable-energy and blue-finance investors can benefit from the diversification drive.

Market Access

Seychelles is a member of the Southern African Development Community (SADC), the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA), the Indian Ocean Commission and the WTO, a party to the Tripartite Free Trade Area and to other preferential trade agreements, and has bilateral investment treaties with several countries. Its main export destinations include France, the UAE and the UK. Europe is by far the largest source of visitors, accounting for about 77% of arrivals in early 2026, with Asia and Africa next. The deepening of Port Victoria to 16 metres is designed to cut shipping costs, reduce transit stops and strengthen its position in the Indian Ocean tuna industry.

Current International Business Channels

The Seychelles Investment Board is the main channel for investor outreach and facilitation, working alongside local business associations. The World Bank, the European Investment Bank and the French Development Agency (AFD) are active financiers, including of the Port Victoria project, and the Global Environment Facility and World Bank backed the blue bond, making development finance a useful source of co-investment. Bilateral channels with the EU and France, the UK and the main tourism source markets are useful entry points, and the blue-economy community built around marine protection and blue-finance instruments offers a network for sustainable-investment partners.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Seychelles suits investors seeking a stable, high-income base with premium positioning: hospitality groups and resort developers able to plan for source-market variability; fisheries and seafood-processing investors building on the tuna value chain; port, logistics and marine-services investors aligned with Port Victoria's expansion; renewable-energy and climate-resilience investors working alongside development banks; and financial-services firms using the FSA-regulated framework with strong compliance standards.
