The economy today

Bermuda in November 2026 combines one of the world's highest income levels, a globally significant financial-services cluster and a markedly stronger public balance sheet. Real GDP grew 1.9% in 2024 to a nominal $9.19 billion (GDP per capita of $143,878), growth for 2025 is estimated at 2.5–3.0%, and the economy expanded a further 0.5% in Q1 2026, with inflation at 2.5% in April 2026 and unemployment at 1.4%, its lowest since 1970. The 15% Corporate Income Tax, introduced from 2025 to align with OECD global minimum tax rules, raised $279 million in 2025/26 and is expected to yield $753.2 million in 2026/27, underpinning a projected surplus of $472.7 million, full repayment of $605 million of debt in January 2027 and new stabilisation, sinking and sovereign wealth funds. Moody's upgraded Bermuda to A1 in May 2026, while S&P and KBRA affirmed A+ with positive outlooks, and Bermuda ranked first in Latin America and the Caribbean in the Global Financial Centres Index in April 2026.

Bermuda's main structural consideration is its concentration on international (re)insurance and high-value tourism within a small, high-cost island economy. This concentration is also its core strength: the market's scale, capital depth and regulatory standing have proved resilient through major catastrophe years, and international business employment reached a record 5,112 in 2025. Government policy is directing CIT revenues toward debt reduction and buffer funds, while the BDA is broadening the base into asset management, private wealth, digital finance, climate-risk solutions and infrastructure. Tourism is being refreshed through the $550 million Fairmont Southampton redevelopment and rising cruise and superyacht activity, creating opportunities for investors in hospitality, real estate, energy and services alongside the established financial sector.

How do I get in?

Bermuda Business Development Agency (BDA), the official investment promotion agency, offering concierge-level support across insurance, asset management, private wealth, digital finance and infrastructure

Exempted companies formed by non-Bermudians to conduct business outside Bermuda; local companies follow a 60/40 Bermudian ownership and directorship rule, with Minister of Finance licences available for exceptions

Corporate Income Tax Act regime: 15% CIT since 1 January 2025 applies only to groups with annual revenue of €750 million or more, supported by the Tax Credits Act 2025; other businesses remain untaxed on profits, dividends and capital gains

Bermuda Monetary Authority (BMA) licensing for insurers, banks, funds and digital-asset businesses, including tailored routes for special purpose insurers, collateralised reinsurers and innovative insurers

Digital Asset Business Act framework positioning Bermuda as a regulated hub for digital assets, linked to the government's on-chain economy initiative

Bermudian dollar pegged 1:1 to the US dollar, which circulates on equal terms; conversion takes place through banks and carries a 1.25% foreign currency purchase tax

Tourism Investment Act incentives for hotel and tourism projects, and special consents for land acquisition by companies with a physical presence

Trade and market access

As a British Overseas Territory, Bermuda operates its own customs, tax and regulatory regime under English common law, with defence and foreign affairs the responsibility of the United Kingdom. Its market access rests above all on financial-services links: the United States accounted for around 62% of the $1.34 trillion in claims incurred by Bermuda commercial reinsurers in 2016–2025, Europe including the UK for about 15% and the rest of the world for 23%. The island lies about two hours by air from New York and 7.5 hours from London, and the US dollar circulates freely alongside the pegged Bermudian dollar. Bermuda's regulatory standing in the US and EU insurance markets, its position outside the EU's tax lists and its leading role in insurance-linked securities give investors direct access to North American and European capital and risk markets.

The overall risk picture

Bermuda's risk profile is that of a mature, well-regulated and highly rated international financial centre with rapidly improving public finances. Moody's upgrade to A1 and positive outlooks from S&P and KBRA in May 2026 reflect sustained surpluses, falling debt and a debt burden expected to decline to 27% of GDP in 2026/27 from 49% in 2020. The introduction of the 15% Corporate Income Tax has been absorbed without loss of the island's standing, with record international business employment and continued new insurer formation. The principal considerations are economic concentration, exposure to Atlantic hurricanes, high costs and land and ownership rules that favour local control; all are well understood and manageable with early planning, local partners and appropriate regulatory structures. Investors can take comfort from Bermuda's English-common-law framework, its stable currency peg and the government's commitment to channel at least 70% of net CIT revenue into debt reduction and financial buffers.

Business networks

The Bermuda Business Development Agency is the central business channel, hosting the annual Bermuda Risk Summit (its fifth edition in April 2026), sponsoring the Bermuda presence at the Consensus digital-asset conference since 2018 and publishing an annual review of investment activity. The Bermuda Chamber of Commerce represents local business and comments regularly on economic data, while the island's major law firms and corporate service providers are a practical source of guidance on licensing, land consents and tax. Industry gatherings such as the Bermuda International Life & Annuity Conference bring together insurers, asset managers and regulators, and the Premier's regular overseas delegations offer direct access to government decision-makers.

Who this market suits

Bermuda suits investors seeking a stable, highly rated, common-law jurisdiction with deep risk-capital markets: reinsurers, life and annuity platforms, captives and insurance-linked securities managers benefiting from the BMA's established framework; asset managers and family offices drawn by the private wealth and residency offer; and digital-finance firms working within the Digital Asset Business Act. Hospitality, real estate and infrastructure investors can engage through the Tourism Investment Act, special land consents and former base lands, building on the Fairmont Southampton redevelopment. Large multinational groups should factor in the 15% Corporate Income Tax, while smaller firms continue to benefit from the absence of tax on profits, dividends and capital gains.
