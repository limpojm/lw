Investor Entry Routes

  • REDIEX (Investment and Export Network, under the Ministry of Industry and Commerce) guides foreign investors on incentive regimes and channels applications to the relevant authorities
  • Investment Law guaranteeing national treatment for foreign investors and full repatriation of capital and profits, with new 2025 incentives (Law 7548/25) offering a 10-year dividend exemption for approved investments of at least $13 million
  • Maquila regime (Law 7547/2025) for export manufacturing: a single 1% tax on value added in Paraguay, with duty-free temporary import of inputs and machinery
  • Free-trade zones (Law 523/95) with a 0.5% income-tax rate and dividend exemption, now also used by large projects such as Paracel
  • Assembly regime (Law 7546/25) and a simple tax system with 10% VAT and 10% corporate income tax
  • SUACE single window for company formation, and an investor permanent-residence route requiring a $70,000 investment and five formal jobs
  • Free-floating guaraní managed by the Banco Central del Paraguay, with no restrictions on profit or capital repatriation

Market-Entry Observation

Paraguay in October 2026 is one of South America's fastest-growing and most stable economies. GDP grew 6.6% in 2025 and 5.7% in the first half of 2026, and the central bank has raised its 2026 forecast to 4.5% on the back of a record soybean harvest. Inflation was 1.7% in September 2026, below the 3.5% target, with the policy rate held at 5.50%. Public debt stands at about 38% of GDP, and net international reserves were about $11 billion at end-June 2026. The country now holds two investment-grade ratings, Moody's Baa3 since July 2024 and S&P BBB- since December 2025. Foreign direct investment exceeded $1 billion for a third consecutive year in 2025, reaching $1.18 billion, while announced projects rose 65% to $911 million, led by data centres, food and renewable energy. A modernised incentive framework, including new maquila, assembly and investment laws in 2025, and the EU–Mercosur interim trade agreement applied from 1 May 2026 are broadening the investment case.

Paraguay's main structural challenge is diversifying an economy closely tied to agriculture and to hydropower from the binational Itaipu and Yacyretá dams. Harvests vary with the weather and the energy model is in transition. The current Itaipu tariff arrangement runs until the end of 2026, and Brazil and Paraguay are negotiating a revised Annex C, expected to take effect in 2027 and needing approval by both parliaments. At the same time, the government is clearing expenditure arrears, which lifts the 2026 fiscal deficit to a projected 3.5% of GDP, and plans to return to the Fiscal Responsibility Law ceiling by 2028. Each of these areas carries investment opportunity. Electricity demand rose 13% in the second quarter of 2026, led by electricity-intensive users, and abundant clean power underpins green hydrogen, data centres and pulp. The Paracel, Villeta and Bioceanic Corridor projects are steadily widening the export base beyond grains and beef.

Investor Risk Note

Paraguay's risk profile is that of a stable, steadily upgrading economy. Two investment-grade ratings, low inflation, a moderate debt burden and reserves of about $11 billion give it strong buffers, and the IMF's 2026 Article IV highlighted the resilience of its macroeconomic framework. The main areas to watch are known and actively managed. They include the Itaipu Annex C revision, the weather sensitivity of agriculture, fiscal consolidation after arrears clearance, and the pace of institutional and judicial reform. Investors in energy-intensive sectors should model tariff scenarios for 2027 onwards. All investors should rely on sound legal structuring and international arbitration provisions. With these steps, Paraguay offers predictable macroeconomic conditions, competitive taxes and abundant clean energy.

Market Access

Paraguay is a founding member of Mercosur, giving preferential access to Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay, and a WTO member. The EU–Mercosur interim trade agreement, which Paraguay ratified in March 2026, has applied provisionally since 1 May 2026, removing tariffs on 91% of traded products and giving Paraguay dedicated quotas for products such as organic sugar, biofuels and pork. Argentina, Brazil and Chile were the largest export destinations in 2025, followed by Bolivia, the United States and Taiwan, with which Paraguay maintains diplomatic relations. Maquila exports reached a record $1.31 billion in 2025, 82% of them to Mercosur. Goods move mainly by barge on the Paraguay–Paraná waterway to Atlantic ports, and the Bioceanic Road Corridor will add a direct route to Chile's Pacific ports for Asian markets.

Current International Business Channels

The Paraguayan-American Chamber of Commerce (AmCham Paraguay) and European bilateral chambers are the main foreign business associations, alongside the Unión Industrial Paraguaya and sector bodies such as the cereals and oilseed exporters' chamber (CAPECO) for domestic liaison. REDIEX and the Ministry of Industry and Commerce run investor outreach and trade missions. The Inter-American Development Bank, IDB Invest, the World Bank, CAF and the European Investment Bank are active in infrastructure, energy and private-sector finance. IDB Invest and the EIB have both supported the Villeta green-hydrogen project. Large investors such as Paracel are a useful source of practical intelligence on logistics and permitting.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Paraguay suits investors seeking a low-cost, low-tax and macroeconomically stable base in the heart of Mercosur. Manufacturers and assemblers can use the 1% maquila regime to serve Brazil and Argentina. Agribusiness and food-processing investors can build on world-scale soybean and beef production and new EU–Mercosur quotas. Energy-intensive industries, including data centres, green hydrogen and pulp, are drawn by abundant renewable hydropower. Logistics and infrastructure investors can position for the Bioceanic Corridor and river-port expansion. Energy-intensive investors should factor the 2027 Itaipu tariff revision into project economics.
