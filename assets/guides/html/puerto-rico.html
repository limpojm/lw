The economy today

Puerto Rico in October 2026 offers investors a US jurisdiction with a deep manufacturing base and improving business momentum. Real gross product grew 0.4% in fiscal year 2025 (to June) after 1.6% in FY2024, with GDP of $129.4 billion and manufacturing contributing around 44% of output. Gross domestic fixed investment reached $18.0 billion in FY2025, and leading indicators remain constructive: the manufacturing PMI stood at 51.9 in Q2 2026 with new orders at 54.8, unemployment was 5.7%, and commercial, industrial and agricultural lending rose to $6.5 billion in Q4 2025 from $5.0 billion a year earlier. Tourism is strengthening, with cruise passenger movements up 53% to 747,568 in Q1 2026. Biopharma reshoring is a major driver, led by Eli Lilly's $1.2 billion Carolina project and Amgen's nearly $1 billion of commitments in Juncos, while Act 60 incentives have been extended to 2055 for new beneficiaries.

Puerto Rico's main structural challenge is energy reliability, and it is also one of its largest investment opportunities. Two island-wide blackouts occurred within four months in 2024–2025, and the restructuring of the Puerto Rico Electric Power Authority (PREPA), in Title III proceedings since 2017, is still under way. Progress is visible: in March 2026 the federal court rejected bondholder claims of at least $3.7 billion for priority treatment, and in June 2026 the Oversight Board offered non-settling bondholders $3 billion, which would lift their recovery to about 35% and bring total non-pension creditor recovery to $4 billion. In parallel, grid operator LUMA has announced nearly 1 GW of renewables and over 700 MW of storage backed by more than $4 billion of private investment. For investors, on-site generation and storage mitigate supply risk, and the energy transition itself creates opportunities in solar, storage, grid services and engineering.

How do I get in?

Invest Puerto Rico (non-profit investment promotion agency) provides confidential site selection, incentive guidance and introductions for new investors

Department of Economic Development and Commerce (DDEC) administers incentive decrees under Act 60 (the Incentives Code), extended in 2026 to 2055 for new beneficiaries, with a 4% rate on capital gains, interest and dividends for future resident-investor beneficiaries

Puerto Rico Industrial Development Company (PRIDCO) offers industrial buildings and land for manufacturing and export-services projects

Operation within the US legal, regulatory and customs system: US dollar currency, US federal courts, FDA oversight and no currency or repatriation restrictions

US federal programmes, including Opportunity Zones and SBA-guaranteed lending, apply alongside local incentives

Public-private partnerships through the Public-Private Partnerships Authority for energy, ports, roads and other infrastructure

Energy-sector entry via renewable generation, storage and grid projects, including interconnection points for nearly 1 GW of planned renewables

Trade and market access

Puerto Rico is an unincorporated territory of the United States and lies within the US customs territory, so goods move to and from the US mainland duty-free and benefit from US trade agreements and US membership of the WTO. The US mainland is the dominant export market, especially for pharmaceuticals, biologics and medical devices, and exports totalled $13.6 billion in Q1 2026. Puerto Rico's location also makes it a natural link between the US, the Caribbean and Latin America. The Port of San Juan handles most container traffic, with the Port of the Americas in Ponce and airports at San Juan and Aguadilla providing more capacity. Shipping between US ports falls under the Jones Act, while international routes and Foreign-Trade Zone procedures support re-export and regional distribution.

The overall risk picture

Puerto Rico's risk profile is that of a US jurisdiction working through the final stages of a long fiscal recovery. Investors benefit from US federal law and courts, the US dollar, FDA regulation and full access to the US market, which together provide a level of legal and currency certainty unusual in the Caribbean. The central government's debt has already been restructured under PROMESA, and the remaining PREPA case is moving towards resolution, helped by the March 2026 court ruling and the Oversight Board's improved settlement offer. The main practical considerations are electricity reliability, shipping costs and labour supply in specialised fields, all of which can be managed through on-site energy solutions, careful logistics planning and workforce partnerships. Hurricane exposure is a known factor, best addressed through resilient design and appropriate insurance. Recent multi-billion-dollar commitments by global pharmaceutical companies show strong confidence in Puerto Rico as a long-term production base.

Business networks

The Puerto Rico Chamber of Commerce, the Puerto Rico Manufacturers Association and the Puerto Rico Products Association are the leading local business networks, and the island also has active bioscience and technology industry groups. Invest Puerto Rico and DDEC run investor missions and take part in international events such as the BIO International Convention. US federal channels, including SelectUSA and the Small Business Administration, are available to investors. Global pharmaceutical and medical-device manufacturers with long-established plants are a useful source of practical intelligence on operating conditions and supply chains.

Who this market suits

Puerto Rico suits investors who want US market access, US legal protection and a proven advanced-manufacturing ecosystem: pharmaceutical, biologics and medical-device companies reshoring or expanding production; clean-energy developers and engineering firms targeting grid, solar and storage opportunities; aerospace and technology firms drawn by a bilingual workforce and Act 60 incentives; and tourism, hospitality and real estate investors building on the recovery in visitor numbers. Infrastructure investors with PPP experience can also find opportunities as energy and port projects progress.
