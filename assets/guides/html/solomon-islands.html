The economy today

Solomon Islands in October 2026 is a growing economy undergoing a rapid shift in its export base. GDP expanded an estimated 3.5% in 2025, and the Central Bank forecasts 3.0% growth in 2026 and 3.7% in 2027, with mining, infrastructure investment and services leading. Foreign direct investment more than doubled to $67 million in 2025, according to UNCTAD, making the country one of the drivers of investment growth among Pacific small island states. Minerals rose to 53% of goods exports in 2025 from 4% in 2019, helping deliver a current account surplus for the first time since 2012, while public debt remains moderate at 29.5% of GDP. Reform momentum includes new Special Economic Zones legislation, a Mineral Resources Bill before Parliament, the introduction of a 1.5% central bank policy rate in February 2026, and preparations for a value-added tax.

The country's main structural task is managing the transition from a logging-based economy to a broader, mining-led one. Logs fell from 70% of exports in 2019 to 16% in 2025 as forest resources are depleted, and gold is rapidly filling the revenue gap, led by the Gold Ridge expansion, which the government expects to generate around SI$2 billion a year in public revenue at full operation. The economy has also shown resilience to the 2026 global fuel-price shock and Tropical Cyclone Maila: inflation reached 4.6% in June 2026, driven largely by betel nut and fuel prices, before easing to 3.7% in August, while gross reserves of $862 million cover 12.5 months of imports. The World Bank's guidance that mining revenues should finance diversification points to the opportunity: renewable energy to replace imported diesel, which supplies about 98% of electricity, alongside fisheries, agro-processing and tourism.

How do I get in?

InvestSolomons (Ministry of Commerce, Industries, Labour and Immigration), the government's investment promotion agency, which registers foreign investors, offers online applications and provides aftercare

Foreign Investment Act 2005: registration with the Registrar of Foreign Investment for all non-reserved activities, review by an Investment Facilitation Committee and reasonable compensation for any compulsory acquisition

Special Economic Zones legislation passed in May 2025, with incentive schedules for zone infrastructure, production for the domestic market and production for export

Central Bank of Solomon Islands oversight of the Solomon dollar under a fixed exchange-rate regime managed against a currency basket; confirm profit and capital transfer procedures with the central bank and commercial banks

Mining entry through licensing by the Ministry of Mines, Energy and Rural Electrification, with a modernising Mineral Resources Bill before Parliament

Fisheries and processing partnerships, building on the Noro tuna hub and joint-venture models with state and provincial shareholders

Infrastructure and energy participation through donor and DFI-backed programmes, including the Tina River Hydropower project

Trade and market access

Solomon Islands is a WTO member and benefits from a set of preferential arrangements that support its exports. It acceded to the EU–Pacific interim Economic Partnership Agreement in 2020, giving duty-free access to the EU, and is a party to PACER Plus, the regional trade and development agreement with Australia, New Zealand and other Pacific states, in force since December 2020. Graduation from Least Developed Country status has been rescheduled to December 2027, and the government's Smooth Transition Strategy notes that duty-free, quota-free access to major markets including China, the EU and Australia is expected to be retained. Exports are led by gold, logs and processed tuna, shipped through Honiara and Noro, the country's two international ports.

The overall risk picture

Solomon Islands offers a distinctive combination of rising mineral wealth, strong external buffers and moderate public debt. Reserves of $862 million, equal to 12.5 months of imports, a current account that moved into surplus in 2025 and FDI that more than doubled to $67 million give the economy a solid foundation, while new SEZ legislation, the Mineral Resources Bill and planned tax reform show a clear reform direction. The main considerations are the pace of the transition from logging to mining, frequent changes of government through parliamentary votes, high fuel-based energy costs and exposure to cyclones. Each has a practical mitigation: early engagement with landowners and provincial authorities, relationships across government, renewable-energy investment and resilient project design. Investors should also note that inflation rose to 4.6% in mid-2026 on fuel and food prices, eased to 3.7% in August and is expected to ease further in 2027, and that the IMF has encouraged continued progress on fiscal and governance reform.

Business networks

The Solomon Islands Chamber of Commerce and Industry (SICCI) is the leading private-sector body, hosting the annual SICCI Business Forum and participating in the government's Private Sector Advisory Committee, which provides a direct channel for business views on reform. InvestSolomons supports investor facilitation and aftercare. The World Bank, Asian Development Bank and Green Climate Fund are active financiers of energy and infrastructure, including the Tina River Hydropower project, while Australia and other partners provide substantial development support. Established investors such as SolTuna and the Gold Ridge operator are valuable sources of practical insight into operating conditions, landowner engagement and logistics.

Who this market suits

Solomon Islands best suits investors with a long-term horizon and experience of resource-rich, small-island markets: mining and mining-services companies positioned for the expansion of gold and nickel; renewable-energy developers and DFI-backed infrastructure investors addressing the shift away from diesel power; fisheries and food-processing investors building on the Noro tuna hub and duty-free EU access; and agro-processing and export manufacturers able to use the new SEZ incentives. Investors that prioritise community engagement and partner with established local and state shareholders are well placed to succeed.
