Investor Entry Routes

  • APIEX (Investment and Export Promotion Agency) is the single agency for registering and facilitating private investment projects and promoting exports
  • Private Investment Law No. 8/2023 and its regulation (Decree No. 8/2024) guarantee fair and non-discriminatory treatment of national and foreign investors, property protection and the transfer of funds abroad, with a registration-only route for projects up to MZN 32 billion
  • Special Economic Zones and Industrial Free Zones under Decree No. 12/2026, offering customs-duty suspension; free-zone firms export at least 70% of output, and strategic projects of at least MZN 3.25 billion with 150 local jobs can obtain isolated free-zone status
  • Banco de Moçambique oversees the foreign-exchange regime; profits and dividends may be transferred abroad once tax and exchange rules are met, while exporters currently convert 50% of export receipts into meticais (regime extended for six months from October 2026)
  • Partnership with the state hydrocarbons company ENH, which holds stakes alongside international operators in the Rovuma Basin LNG developments
  • Concessions and public-private partnerships in ports, rail and industry, including the Maputo port concession and a recently launched PPP process for vehicle assembly
  • Bilateral investment treaties, including the China–Mozambique BIT in force since its 2001 signature, alongside preferential EU and UK market access through the SADC EPA and UK–SACUM EPA

Market-Entry Observation

Mozambique in October 2026 is moving from recovery into a new investment cycle. GDP grew 1.7% year-on-year in Q2 2026, the third consecutive quarter of growth following the post-election slowdown, led by household consumption (+8.4%) and services, with hotels and restaurants up 14.5%. The IMF projects 0.5% growth for 2026 and the African Development Bank 2.1%, rising to 3.5% in 2027. Foreign direct investment reached a record $5.7 billion in 2025, up 60.2%, and the government expects $5.9 billion in 2026 as LNG construction accelerates: TotalEnergies relaunched Mozambique LNG on 1 January 2026, Eni's Coral Norte has moved into construction, and ExxonMobil is targeting a final investment decision on Rovuma LNG before end-2026. The reform record is strengthening, with removal from the FATF grey list in October 2025 and the EU high-risk list in December 2025, a new SEZ regulation in April 2026, and a policy rate of 9.25%, the lowest since December 2015, with inflation at 6.5% in August.

Mozambique's main near-term challenge is fiscal and foreign-exchange liquidity ahead of the LNG revenue era. Public debt stood at 74.7% of GDP in 2025, domestic borrowing rose 16.6%, and the IMF notes constrained access to foreign currency. The authorities are responding: Mozambique repaid its outstanding IMF loans early in March 2026, is negotiating a new Extended Credit Facility programme targeted for Board approval by end-2026, is pursuing a fiscal consolidation package, and has prioritised foreign currency for fuel, food and medicines. Net international reserves reached a record $4.26 billion in February 2026 and stood at about $3.5 billion in May after the IMF repayment. For investors, this favours export-earning and foreign-currency-generating projects, which also stand to benefit most as gas revenues build towards the end of the decade.

Investor Risk Note

Mozambique's risk profile is that of a frontier economy at the start of a major gas-led investment cycle. Its strengths are significant: world-scale LNG resources now moving into construction across three projects, record FDI of $5.7 billion in 2025, a strategic coastline serving landlocked neighbours, and improving financial-integrity credentials following removal from the FATF and EU high-risk lists. The near-term constraints are fiscal and foreign-exchange liquidity, with public debt at 74.7% of GDP and limited foreign-currency availability; the authorities are addressing these through a planned IMF Extended Credit Facility programme, fiscal consolidation and foreign-exchange prioritisation, and the early repayment of IMF loans in March 2026 signals a commitment to credibility. Security in Cabo Delgado has improved sufficiently for LNG operators to lift force majeure, and climate exposure is a known factor that can be managed through resilient design and insurance. Investors who generate foreign currency, partner with established operators and plan for local-currency and payment timing will be best placed to benefit.

Market Access

Mozambique is a member of the Southern African Development Community (SADC), giving preferential access to a regional market of more than 250 million people, and of the African Continental Free Trade Area, covering a market of 1.4 billion people; it is also a WTO member. The EU–SADC Economic Partnership Agreement, applied since 2018, provides duty-free, quota-free access to the EU, and the UK–SACUM EPA gives equivalent access to the UK. Exports reached $7.8 billion in 2025, led by India (20.6%), South Africa (12.6%), the United Kingdom (8.3%) and China, with coal, natural gas, aluminium, heavy mineral sands, electricity and agricultural products such as cashew nuts the main products. With more than 2,700 km of Indian Ocean coastline and borders with six countries, Mozambique's Maputo, Beira and Nacala corridors make it the natural sea gateway for much of southern and central Africa.

Current International Business Channels

The Confederation of Business Associations of Mozambique (CTA) is the main private-sector body and the government's principal dialogue partner, and the annual Maputo International Fair (FACIM), whose 60th edition took place in 2025, is the leading trade and investment showcase. APIEX runs investor outreach and sector promotion, including events aimed at specific partner countries. The major LNG operators, TotalEnergies, ExxonMobil and Eni, and their contractors maintain substantial in-country presence and local-content programmes and are a valuable source of supply-chain intelligence. The IMF, World Bank and African Development Bank are actively engaged in Mozambique, and the EU has provided funding for security support in Cabo Delgado.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Mozambique best suits investors with a long-term horizon and the ability to generate or access foreign currency: LNG supply-chain, engineering, logistics and services companies positioned for the build-out of Mozambique LNG, Rovuma LNG and Coral Norte; port, rail and corridor investors serving landlocked neighbours; mining and mineral-processing investors in coal, heavy sands and graphite; and agro-processing, light manufacturing and vehicle-assembly investors drawn by the new SEZ regime and duty-free access to EU and UK markets. Development finance institutions, export-credit agencies and investors comfortable with frontier-market structuring are particularly well placed, given the support that risk-mitigation instruments can provide during the transition to the LNG revenue era.
