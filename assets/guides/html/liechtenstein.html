The economy today

Liechtenstein in November 2026 offers investors a rare combination: AAA-rated public finances, the Swiss franc and the Swiss customs area, and full access to the EU single market through the EEA. The government has no outstanding debt, ran a surplus of 2.8% of GDP in 2025 according to the IMF, and holds net financial assets the IMF estimates at more than 140% of GDP; S&P affirmed its AAA rating with a stable outlook in June 2026. The financial centre continues to grow: client assets of the 11 Liechtenstein banks rose 6.8% to CHF 538 billion at the end of 2025 and their result from ordinary activities rose 19.4%. Industry generates about 42% of value added, led by globally active groups such as Hilti, Ivoclar and Oerlikon Balzers, and the economy creates more jobs than it has residents, with about 43,600 people employed and a population of about 40,900.

Liechtenstein's main structural challenge is the exposure of its export-led industry to global trade conditions and a strong currency. GDP is estimated to have fallen by about 3% in 2025, a year marked by US tariffs, and goods exports outside Switzerland fell 4.9% to CHF 3.05 billion. The response is already working: a November 2025 agreement with the United States and Switzerland cut the tariff on Liechtenstein's goods to 15%, inflation of 0.3% in March 2026 keeps costs stable, and the IMF projects a return to growth of 0.5% in 2026 and 1.5% by 2028. Large fiscal reserves give the government room to support growth, invest in infrastructure and skills, and pursue its review of deeper European integration, while low unemployment of about 2% and a deep cross-border talent pool support investors in precision manufacturing, medical technology and financial services.

Trade and market access

Liechtenstein combines membership of the European Economic Area, which it joined in 1995, with a customs and currency union with Switzerland dating from 1923. This gives companies access both to the EU single market of goods, services and capital, including passporting for financial services, and to the Swiss market without customs barriers, while EFTA's free trade agreements extend access to partners worldwide. Liechtenstein has been a WTO member since 1995. Its main export markets are Germany and Austria, which together took about 45% of goods exports in 2024, followed by the United States (about 14%), China and France. Located in the Rhine Valley between Switzerland and Austria, it relies on road and rail links through its two neighbours and on nearby Swiss and Austrian airports.

The overall risk picture

Liechtenstein's risk profile is among the lowest in Europe: a stable constitutional monarchy with a coalition government in office since April 2025, AAA ratings, no national debt, net public financial assets of more than 140% of GDP and a well-capitalised banking sector. It is a member of the EEA, the UN and the WTO, an associate member of Schengen since 2011 and a member of the IMF since 2024, and it is not subject to sanctions or on the FATF grey list. The main issues are economic rather than political: an estimated 3% contraction in 2025 linked to US tariffs, the risk of renewed Swiss franc strength and the IMF's call for stronger oversight of non-bank financial firms and trustees. These are well understood and manageable, and the government's fiscal reserves give it unusual capacity to respond. Investors in financial services should expect thorough know-your-customer and licensing requirements, which underpin the centre's reputation.

Business networks

The Liechtenstein Chamber of Commerce and Industry and the Liechtenstein Bankers Association are the main business associations, representing the country's industrial exporters and its banks, and both work closely with government on location and regulatory policy. The Liechtenstein Business portal provides information on company formation, the business location and economic news, and the Financial Market Authority publishes guidance for applicants in financial services and digital assets. Many investors access Liechtenstein through the wider Rhine Valley economy, using Swiss and Austrian chambers, professional advisers and research partners across the border. Liechtenstein's membership of EFTA, the EEA and, since 2024, the IMF provides regular international reviews and a network of free trade agreements, supporting commercial relations with partners worldwide.

Who this market suits

Liechtenstein suits investors seeking a highly stable, low-tax, AAA-rated base with access to both the EU and Swiss markets: private banks, asset managers and fund promoters using EEA passporting and a financial centre with CHF 538 billion in client assets; precision engineering, construction-technology and medical-technology manufacturers serving European and global supply chains; fintech and tokenisation firms using the TVTG and MiCA frameworks; and family offices and holding structures that value long-term political continuity. Investors should be prepared for rigorous licensing, compliance and substance requirements and limited land and residence permits.
