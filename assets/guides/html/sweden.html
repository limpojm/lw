The economy today

Sweden in November 2026 offers investors one of the EU's most innovative and well-governed economies, with low public debt, a 20.6% corporate tax and a strong record in technology, engineering and clean industry. Growth has picked up: GDP rose 1.6% quarter-on-quarter in the second quarter of 2026, the fastest since early 2024, helped by investment in machinery, equipment and defence, and grew 2.8% year-on-year in the third quarter. Inflation is low, with CPIF inflation at 1.5% in September and a flash 1.1% in October, partly reflecting lower food prices. Business Sweden reports that FDI inflows reached SEK 287 billion in 2025, up 37%, and the stock of inward investment exceeds 70% of GDP, with US companies the largest single investor group. With the policy rate raised to 2% in November to keep inflation on target, the Riksbank is signalling confidence in the recovery.

Sweden's main near-term challenge is political: the September 2026 election left the centre-left opposition with 176 of 349 seats, but its four parties disagree on the shape of a government. Parliament rejected Magdalena Andersson as prime minister on 15 October and Centre Party leader Elisabeth Thand Ringqvist on 3 November, and Ulf Kristersson leads a caretaker government, which is presenting a 2027 budget that largely carries over current spending. Unemployment, at 8.6% in September, is also high by Nordic standards. The fundamentals cushion these issues: general government debt was 35.1% of GDP in 2025, all parties support continued defence rearmament, and constitutional rules require a new election within three months if four prime-ministerial votes fail. Strong public finances leave room for whichever government forms to support investment in energy, defence and industry.

How do I get in?

Business Sweden, the government's trade and investment agency, provides free, confidential support to international companies investing and expanding in Sweden

A flat 20.6% corporate income tax with no municipal or local corporate taxes, within a transparent, rules-based tax system

The FDI Act (2023:560), in force since 1 December 2023, requires notification to the Inspectorate of Strategic Products for stakes in protected activities such as energy, defence, critical raw materials and data; Phase I review takes 25 working days

EU single-market and Schengen membership, giving free movement of goods, services, capital and people and access to all EU trade agreements

Swedish krona with free capital movement; the Riksbank sets monetary policy independently and Sweden is not in the euro area

State-backed nuclear new-build framework offering government loans, two-way contracts for difference and risk-sharing, starting with Videberg Kraft at Ringhals

Large green-industry and electrification projects in northern Sweden, such as Stegra and Lyten, creating supply-chain and partnership routes

Trade and market access

As an EU member, Sweden gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Schengen membership allows passport-free travel across most of the EU, and the Öresund bridge links southern Sweden with Copenhagen and continental Europe. Sweden's west-coast and Baltic ports connect it with global markets, while its electricity grid is integrated with Nordic and European markets. Sweden keeps the krona and remains outside the euro area, so trade with euro-area partners carries currency costs that most exporters hedge. Its membership of NATO and close Nordic cooperation reinforce its role as a secure base for defence, energy and industrial supply chains in Northern Europe.

The overall risk picture

Sweden's risk profile is that of a stable, high-income EU democracy with low public debt, strong institutions and an independent central bank. The main issue for investors is the period of government formation after the September 2026 election: a caretaker government under Ulf Kristersson remains in office, two prime-ministerial votes have failed, and an extraordinary election must be held within three months if four votes fail. This affects the timing of new policies rather than the legal or regulatory framework, and all parties support higher defence spending. Investors in sensitive sectors should note the FDI screening regime run by the Inspectorate of Strategic Products, which can attach conditions to or block deals and should be addressed early with legal advice. Sweden is a FATF member since 1990 and its anti-money-laundering framework was rated as strong in its 2017 evaluation. Krona exposure and the start of monetary tightening, with the policy rate at 2%, are manageable through standard hedging.

Business networks

Business Sweden supports international investors and works with regional investment promotion bodies across the country. The American Chamber of Commerce in Sweden, the British-Swedish Chamber of Commerce, the German-Swedish Chamber of Commerce and the Stockholm Chamber of Commerce are active business associations, while the Confederation of Swedish Enterprise represents domestic employers. Family-controlled investment groups, including the Wallenberg sphere, are influential partners, as the Stegra financing round showed. The European Investment Bank and the Nordic Investment Bank are active in energy, industry and infrastructure finance, and embassies of major partners maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Sweden suits investors seeking a stable, innovative EU base with excellent governance: technology, fintech and life-sciences companies drawing on Stockholm's ecosystem; energy investors and suppliers targeting the nuclear new-build programme, renewables and grid expansion; green-industry and battery investors in northern Sweden around Stegra and Lyten's Skellefteå hub; defence and dual-use firms benefiting from rising defence spending; and data-centre developers using clean, competitive power. Investors in protected sectors should plan for FDI screening from the outset.
