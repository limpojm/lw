The economy today

Mauritius in October 2026 combines an investment-grade credit rating, high income per capita and deep financial buffers with a well-established role as a gateway between Africa, Asia and Europe. Statistics Mauritius estimates that GDP grew 3.5% in 2025 and projects 3.0% growth in 2026, with finance and insurance, trade and transport leading. Foreign direct investment (excluding global business companies) rose 45.6% to Rs 48.0 billion in 2025, led by real estate and a sharp rise in financial-sector inflows, mainly from the UK and France. Tourism reached a record 1.44 million visitors, occupation permit applications have nearly tripled since 2018 to 10,550, and the extension of AGOA to the end of 2028 has restored duty-free US access for exporters. The 2026–27 Budget added an AI City Scheme and a golden visa, and the EDB is targeting Rs 38–40 billion of FDI in the current fiscal year.

Mauritius's main structural task is to rebuild fiscal space while sustaining growth. Public debt stood at about 87.8% of GDP in mid-2026, above the authorities' 80% anchor, but consolidation is clearly under way: the deficit fell from 9.3% of GDP in FY2024/25 to about 6.0% in FY2025/26 and is targeted at 3.7% in FY2026/27, progress that Moody's has acknowledged while keeping a Baa3 rating with a negative outlook. Imported energy costs lifted inflation to 5.7% in September 2026, and the Bank of Mauritius has responded early, raising its Key Rate to 4.75% in May. Gross official reserves of $10.2 billion, equivalent to 14.4 months of imports, a large positive net international investment position and a well-capitalised banking sector give the economy a substantial cushion. The government's plans to double capital spending, partner with the private sector on the port and expand renewable power create direct openings for investors.

How do I get in?

Economic Development Board (EDB), the single government gateway for investment promotion, permits and incentives under the EDB Act 2017, including the Premium Investor Certificate for projects of at least Rs 500 million

Open company regime permitting 100% foreign ownership with no minimum capital, a corporate tax rate of 15% or lower, 46 investment promotion and protection agreements (30 in force) and arbitration through the Mauritius International Arbitration Centre

Financial Services Commission licensing for global business, funds and wealth management; Mauritius left the FATF grey list in October 2021 and the EU high-risk list in January 2022

Freeport regime offering a 3% corporate tax rate, an eight-year income tax holiday for qualifying investments and duty and VAT exemptions for re-export activity

No exchange controls: profits, dividends and capital can be freely repatriated, with no tax on dividends or capital gains, under the Bank of Mauritius's IMF Article VIII commitments

Property and residency routes, including EDB property schemes from $375,000 and a new golden visa granting 20-year permanent residence for investments of at least $1 million

Sector-entry through competitive tenders and strategic partnerships, such as the 220 MW solar-and-storage programme and port modernisation

Trade and market access

Mauritius is a member of the Southern African Development Community (SADC) and the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA), a signatory to the COMESA–EAC–SADC Tripartite Free Trade Area and a participant in the African Continental Free Trade Area since January 2021, and a founding member of the WTO. It has free trade agreements with China (in force January 2021) and India (CECPA, April 2021), an interim Economic Partnership Agreement with the EU and an Economic Partnership Agreement with the UK, and AGOA preferences for US-bound goods now run to the end of 2028. The United States takes about 11% of goods exports, and Europe supplied 64% of tourist arrivals in early 2026. With preferential access to markets covering 68% of the world's population and an expanding regional outreach to East Africa, Mauritius serves as a platform for structuring and serving investment across Africa and the Indian Ocean.

The overall risk picture

Mauritius's risk profile is that of a stable, well-governed democracy with strong institutions, high data transparency and an investment-grade rating, working through a period of fiscal consolidation. The sharp narrowing of the deficit since FY2024/25, early action by the Bank of Mauritius on inflation and reserves equal to more than a year of imports demonstrate policy credibility and resilience to external shocks. The main points to monitor are progress towards the 80% debt anchor, the pace of energy-driven inflation, and the timing of UK ratification of the Chagos treaty, which affects projected public revenue but not the commercial operating environment. The open capital account, full foreign ownership, investment treaty protection and established arbitration options give investors a predictable framework. Investors should allow for adjustments to tax and permit rules as the government balances revenue needs with competitiveness, and should use the EDB as a first point of contact.

Business networks

The Mauritius Chamber of Commerce and Industry (MCCI) and Business Mauritius are the main private-sector bodies, alongside sector groups such as the Mauritius Export Association. The EDB leads investor outreach, including business missions to Ethiopia, Tanzania and Kenya in September 2026, participation in the US-Africa Business Summit in December 2026 and preparations for the 2027 EU-Africa Summit, and is launching an E-Diaspora Platform to connect Mauritian professionals abroad. The IMF, World Bank and African Development Bank remain engaged on fiscal reform, infrastructure and climate resilience, while the Ebène financial community of banks, management companies and law firms is a ready source of practical market intelligence on Africa-focused structuring.

Who this market suits

Mauritius suits investors seeking a stable, well-regulated base for regional and Africa-focused business: financial-services, fund and wealth-management firms using the FSC framework and treaty network; real estate and hospitality investors building on record tourism and EDB property schemes; technology and AI investors attracted by the new AI City Scheme and golden visa; renewable energy developers bidding into the solar-and-storage pipeline; and logistics, Blue Economy and high-tech manufacturing investors using the Freeport and renewed AGOA access. Healthcare, life sciences and agri-business are further EDB priorities with active project facilitation.
