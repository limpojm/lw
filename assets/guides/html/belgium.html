The economy today

Belgium in November 2026 offers investors a highly open, productive economy at the heart of the EU, with direct access to the EU institutions, one of Europe's largest ports and a strong research and life-sciences base. GDP grew 1.0% in 2025 and accelerated to 0.3% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, the fastest pace since early 2025, led by services. Foreign investment remains robust: Flanders attracted 285 foreign projects worth €3.79 billion in 2025, creating 5,870 jobs, with the United States the largest investor, while Wallonia drew €1.3 billion, up 34%, and its exports reached a record €62.4 billion. Generous innovation incentives, including an effective 3.75% tax rate on qualifying innovation income, and the unlimited carry-forward of the investment deduction from 2026 add to the appeal.

Belgium's main structural challenge is restoring public finances while managing high energy costs. The European Commission projects a deficit of 5.2% of GDP in 2025 and 2026 and debt rising from 107.9% to 110.5% of GDP, and Belgium is under an EU excessive deficit procedure. Inflation reached 5.26% in October 2026, driven by energy prices, and in early November Prime Minister Bart De Wever offered his government's resignation after coalition talks on about €10 billion of savings by 2029 failed. Belgium's fundamentals nonetheless remain strong: high productivity, a skilled multilingual workforce, deep trade links and wage indexation that supports demand. A credible budget agreement would strengthen the outlook, while energy-transition, port, defence and life-sciences projects continue to create opportunities.

How do I get in?

Regional investment agencies handle foreign investment: Flanders Investment & Trade (FIT), hub.brussels and the Wallonia Export and Investment Agency (AWEX), each offering site selection, incentives guidance and aftercare

A 25% corporate income tax, with an innovation income deduction of 85% of net innovation income, giving an effective rate of 3.75% on qualifying income

An 80% partial exemption from wage withholding tax for qualifying R&D staff, and a reformed investment deduction including a 40% targeted track for SMEs

An improved expatriate tax regime from 2026, with the tax-free allowance raised to 35% of gross salary and a lower minimum salary of €70,000

Euro-area membership: the euro is the national currency, with free movement of capital and no exchange controls

EU single-market and Schengen membership as a founding member of the EU, giving free movement of goods, services, capital and people

Port of Antwerp-Bruges and inland waterway, rail and road links offering access to the EU's core industrial markets

Trade and market access

As a founding member of the EU and the euro area, Belgium gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and all of the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Its location between France, Germany, the Netherlands and the UK places much of the EU's purchasing power within easy reach. Port of Antwerp-Bruges handled 266.5 million tonnes in 2025, with container volumes stable, and is connected to the European hinterland by inland waterways, rail and motorways. Brussels hosts the main EU institutions and NATO, making Belgium the natural centre for EU regulatory engagement.

The overall risk picture

Belgium's risk profile is that of a wealthy founding member of the EU and the euro area, with strong legal certainty, an open economy and a central role in European institutions. The near-term issues are fiscal and political: debt above 100% of GDP and deficits of about 5% are under an EU excessive deficit procedure, and on 2 November 2026 Prime Minister Bart De Wever offered the resignation of his five-party coalition after budget talks failed, with the King holding it under consideration and consulting party leaders. Belgium has a long record of maintaining stable administration through government transitions, and EU fiscal rules anchor consolidation. Inflation of 5.26% in October 2026 reflects energy prices, and ECB policy, with the deposit rate at 2.5%, is directed at containing it. Investors in finance should note that most immobilised Russian central bank assets are held at Euroclear in Brussels; EU sanctions apply to all Russia-related dealings and specialist advice is recommended.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the Prime Minister (Bart De Wever)

Leads the five-party federal coalition, which offered its resignation in November 2026 over the multi-year budget

Primary reference point for federal economic, fiscal and tax policy

Regional investment agencies (FIT, hub.brussels, AWEX)

Lead foreign investment promotion and incentives in Flanders, Brussels and Wallonia respectively

Standard first points of contact for market entry, sites and grants

National Bank of Belgium

National central bank within the Eurosystem; its governor sits on the ECB Governing Council, and it supervises banks and insurers

Relevant for banking, payments and financial-sector authorisations

Federal Public Service Finance

Administers federal taxes, including innovation and investment deductions and rulings

Key counterpart for tax rulings and incentives

Port of Antwerp-Bruges

Publicly owned port authority managing one of Europe's leading ports and chemical clusters

Partner for logistics, energy-transition and industrial-site investment

Euroclear

Brussels-based international securities depository holding most immobilised Russian central bank assets

Relevant for capital-markets activity and sanctions-related risk assessment

European Union institutions in Brussels

The Commission, Council and Parliament set the regulatory framework for the single market

Essential for regulatory engagement and EU funding programmes

Business networks

The Federation of Enterprises in Belgium (FEB/VBO), the regional employers' bodies VOKA in Flanders and the Union Wallonne des Entreprises, and the American Chamber of Commerce in Belgium are the main business associations. FIT, hub.brussels and AWEX run investor events and international missions, and sector clusters support partnerships in chemicals, life sciences and logistics. The European Investment Bank, headquartered in neighbouring Luxembourg, and EU programmes provide co-financing for qualifying projects. Brussels' concentration of EU institutions, NATO, trade associations and embassies gives investors unmatched access to policy networks and events.

Who this market suits

Belgium suits investors seeking a central, high-skill base inside the EU and the euro area: life-sciences, biotech and research-intensive companies using the innovation income deduction and R&D wage incentives; chemicals, energy-transition and logistics investors around Port of Antwerp-Bruges and Ghent; companies establishing EU headquarters or regulatory-affairs teams in Brussels; and defence and technology firms serving rising European defence budgets. Financial-sector investors should take sanctions advice on Russia-related exposures.
