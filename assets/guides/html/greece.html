The economy today

Greece in November 2026 is one of the euro area's strongest turnaround stories, combining steady growth, large budget surpluses and record foreign investment. GDP grew 2.1% in 2025 and 1.9% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, and the 2027 draft budget forecasts 2.0% growth this year and 2.3% next, with investment rising 7.9%. Foreign direct investment reached a record €12.6 billion in 2025 and €7.6 billion in January–July 2026, almost double the same period of 2025, led by banking, energy and real estate. The primary surplus was 4.9% of GDP in 2025, and early repayment of bailout loans is cutting debt rapidly. Scope raised Greece to BBB+ and Moody's assigned a positive outlook in September 2026, and Chevron's entry into offshore exploration in February 2026 signals new energy potential.

Greece's main structural challenge is its high public debt, together with inflation that has risen above the euro-area average. Debt was 146.1% of GDP in 2025, still the highest in the EU, and inflation reached 5.1% in September 2026, driven by accommodation, air fares and energy. The trend is firmly positive: the government projects debt falling to 136.8% of GDP in 2026 and 128.8% in 2027, a cumulative fall of 67 percentage points since 2020, supported by primary surpluses and early loan repayments. Euro membership and ECB policy, with the deposit rate at 2.5%, anchor financing conditions, and the final Recovery Plan payment requests submitted in September 2026 will unlock up to €6.75 billion, sustaining opportunities in energy, infrastructure, digital and tourism.

How do I get in?

Enterprise Greece, the national investment and trade promotion agency, offers investor facilitation and aftercare

Strategic Investments procedure, now handled by the Ministry of Development's General Secretariat for Private Investments under Law 5297/2026, offering fast-track licensing and support for large projects

Development and EU state-aid incentives, including the framework under which the Metlen gallium project receives €118 million in grants and tax incentives

Corporate income tax of 22%, with a tonnage tax regime for shipping

Euro-area membership: the euro is the national currency and the Bank of Greece is part of the Eurosystem, with free capital movement under EU rules

Residence by investment (golden visa) for real estate from €800,000 in high-demand areas, €400,000 elsewhere and €250,000 for conversions and listed-building restoration

EU co-financing through the €35.95 billion Greece 2.0 Recovery and Resilience Plan, the largest relative to GDP in the EU, and cohesion funds

Trade and market access

As a member of the EU, Greece gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. The euro removes exchange-rate costs for trade with euro-area partners, and Schengen membership allows passport-free travel across most of the EU. Greece sits at the crossroads of Europe, Asia and Africa: Piraeus is a major Mediterranean port and the centre of Greek shipping, and Thessaloniki serves the Balkan hinterland. In energy, Greek LNG terminals feed gas northwards to Bulgaria and beyond through the Vertical Gas Corridor, and offshore exploration south of Crete could add domestic supply, positioning Greece as a regional energy gateway.

The overall risk picture

Greece's risk profile is that of a euro-area member that has completed a strong fiscal and economic recovery, with large primary surpluses, record foreign investment and steadily improving credit ratings. Scope's upgrade to BBB+ and Moody's positive outlook in September 2026, and S&P's affirmation of BBB in October 2026, reflect fiscal discipline and falling debt. The main issues are high public debt, which remains the highest in the EU although falling rapidly, and inflation of 5.1% in September 2026, both being addressed within the EU and euro-area framework. The government led by Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis has ruled out an early election, with a vote expected in spring 2027. No sanctions or legal restrictions materially limit investment in Greece, and investors benefit from the legal certainty of EU law and the convenience of the euro.

Sets economic, fiscal and reform policy; the New Democracy government has ruled out an early election, with a vote expected in spring 2027

Primary reference point for Greece's strategic direction and major projects

Enterprise Greece

National investment and trade promotion agency supporting foreign investors and exporters

Standard first point of contact for market entry

Ministry of Development (General Secretariat for Private Investments)

Evaluates Strategic Investments applications and administers development incentives

Key counterpart for large projects seeking fast-track status and state aid

Bank of Greece

Member of the Eurosystem whose governor sits on the ECB Governing Council; supervises banks within the Single Supervisory Mechanism

Relevant for banking, payments and financial-sector matters

Ministry of National Economy and Finance

Leads the budget, tax policy, debt management and the Recovery and Resilience Plan

Key counterpart on tax, fiscal and EU-funding matters

HELLENiQ ENERGY

Leading Greek refiner and Chevron's 30% partner in four offshore exploration blocks

Partner for upstream, refining and energy-transition engagement

Public Power Corporation (PPC)

Greece's largest power utility, which raised €4.25 billion in new capital in 2026 with participation from CVC Capital Partners

Essential partner for power, renewables and grid-related investment

Business networks

The American-Hellenic Chamber of Commerce, the British Hellenic Chamber of Commerce, the German-Hellenic Chamber of Industry and Commerce and the Athens Chamber of Commerce and Industry are the main business associations, while the Hellenic Federation of Enterprises (SEV) represents domestic industry. Enterprise Greece holds investor events and promotes Greece at international forums, and the annual Thessaloniki International Fair is a key platform for government economic announcements. The European Investment Bank, the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development and EU-funded programmes managed by national ministries offer co-financing for energy, infrastructure and private-sector projects. Embassies of major EU partners, the UK and the US maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Greece suits investors seeking a growing euro-area market with improving credit fundamentals: real-estate and hospitality investors in Athens, Ellinikon and the islands; energy investors in renewables, grids, LNG and offshore exploration; shipping and logistics companies using Piraeus and Thessaloniki; financial investors following the record flows into Greek banks and utilities; manufacturers in metals and critical raw materials using the Strategic Investments procedure; and technology and services companies drawing on Athens' talent pool.
