Investor Entry Routes

  • ANPI-Gabon (National Agency for the Promotion of Investments), established in 2014, offers a single window covering more than 20 agencies, with company registration in around 48 hours
  • 1998 investment code granting foreign companies the same rights as domestic firms, alongside sector codes with customs and tax incentives for mining, forestry, petroleum, agriculture and tourism
  • Special Economic Zones operated by GSEZ (Nkok, Ikolo and Mpassa-Lebombi), offering tax-free operation for 25 years, duty-free equipment imports and full repatriation of funds
  • CFA franc (XAF) pegged to the euro at €1 = 655.957 FCFA under the BEAC; foreign-exchange transactions above set thresholds require BEAC approval and export proceeds must be repatriated within 150 days
  • Production-sharing and partnership routes in petroleum, where the State may hold up to 20% and Gabon Oil Company up to 15% of contracts
  • 2019 Mining Code with state participation of 35% in large mining companies, and the 2020 tourism code offering eight-year tax exemptions for tourism investors
  • Public-private partnerships for infrastructure, managed through ANPI-Gabon and aligned with the 2026–2030 National Growth and Development Plan (PNCD)

Market-Entry Observation

Gabon in October 2026 has completed its political transition and is pursuing an ambitious investment-led growth agenda. President Brice Clotaire Oligui Nguema was elected in April 2025 under a new Constitution approved by referendum in November 2024, legislative and local elections completed the process in autumn 2025, and the African Union restored Gabon's full participation in April 2025. GDP grew an estimated 3.2% in 2025, and the government's draft 2027 budget projects growth of 4% in 2026 and 4.5% in 2027, driven by the non-oil economy, the start of iron ore production and local processing of wood and manganese. Inflation is low at around 1% (May 2026), and the BEAC cut its policy rate to 4.5% in June 2026. Gabon is the world's second-largest manganese producer, and the 2026–2030 National Growth and Development Plan is backed by a 2026 capital budget of 3,300 billion CFA francs, creating opportunities in infrastructure, mining, energy and agro-industry.

Gabon's main structural task is to put public finances on a sustainable footing while funding this investment programme. Debt and payment arrears have risen, rating agencies have the sovereign in the CCC/Caa range, and the IMF's April 2026 projections put debt at around 86% of GDP in 2026. The authorities are responding: a debt audit completed in September 2026 consolidated the debt stock at 9,525 billion CFA francs, or 68.9% of 2025 GDP on the government's figures, subject to IMF review; a new integrated public financial management system went live in January 2026; and Gabon formally requested an IMF programme in March 2026, with technical talks continuing in September. Gabon has also maintained market access and repaid a $605 million Eurobond maturity in May 2025. An IMF-backed programme would anchor fiscal reform and unlock budget support targeted at 600 billion CFA francs for 2027, improving payment discipline and creating room for private and DFI-backed partnerships in the PNCD's projects.

Investor Risk Note

Gabon's risk profile combines strong natural-resource endowments, low inflation, a euro-pegged currency and a political transition that has been completed on schedule with fiscal pressures that the government is actively addressing. The sovereign's CCC-range ratings and accumulated arrears are the key considerations for any investor exposed to government payments, and the IMF has urged that higher oil revenues be used to clear arrears and rebuild buffers. The September 2026 debt audit, the January 2026 public financial management system and ongoing IMF negotiations point to a reform path that would improve payment discipline and unlock concessional finance. CEMAC exchange regulations are a compliance matter rather than a barrier, and are best handled with experienced local banks and counsel. Investors with private revenue streams, export earnings or DFI backing are well placed, while contractors to the State should use secure payment structures until an IMF programme is in place.

Market Access

Gabon is a member of the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC), sharing the BEAC and the euro-pegged CFA franc with five neighbours, and of the Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS), which is headquartered in Libreville. It has been a WTO member since 1995, rejoined the African Union's full activities in April 2025, and has bilateral investment treaties with Belgium-Luxembourg, China, Germany, Italy, South Korea, Morocco, Romania and Spain. Oil, manganese and timber account for around 97% of exports, with China the leading destination. Goods move through Owendo port, fed by the Trans-Gabon Railway, and through Port-Gentil for oil and gas, and the coming iron ore and LNG projects will broaden the export base and the markets it serves.

Current International Business Channels

ANPI-Gabon brings together more than 20 public and private bodies, including the Chamber of Commerce, and the High Council for Investment, created in 2017, provides a formal platform for public-private dialogue. GSEZ and its partner Arise IIP host a sizeable international investor community at Nkok and are a practical source of insight on operating conditions. The World Bank, the African Development Bank and the IMF are closely engaged on public finances, governance and growth, and their programmes are an important route to co-financing. Major operators such as Eramet, Perenco and Gabon Oil Company provide useful intelligence on the extractive and energy sectors, and the national budget cycle and PNCD project pipeline are the main reference points for upcoming opportunities.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Gabon suits investors with a long-term view of natural-resource value chains and the patience to work within a reforming fiscal framework: mining and metals investors in manganese, iron ore and downstream processing; energy investors in oil, gas and the new LNG segment; timber, agro-industry and manufacturing investors using the GSEZ zones' tax-free status and full repatriation; and infrastructure investors in rail, port, road and power projects under the PNCD, ideally alongside development finance institutions. Investors relying on government payments should structure contracts with secure payment mechanisms, while those with export revenues benefit from the stability of the euro-pegged currency.
