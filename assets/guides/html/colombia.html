The economy today

Colombia in October 2026 is regaining growth momentum under a new, explicitly pro-investment government. GDP grew 2.6% in 2025 and accelerated to 3.5% year-on-year in Q2 2026 (1.3% quarter-on-quarter), with fixed investment up 7.0%, taking first-half growth to 2.9%. Foreign direct investment totalled $11.5 billion in 2025 (about 2.5% of GDP), and balance-of-payments data showed a strong start to 2026, with Q1 inflows of $3.8 billion, up 34.4% year-on-year and led by non-extractive sectors, although monthly flow data point to a softer first half. President Abelardo De La Espriella, inaugurated on 7 August 2026, has pledged to restart oil and gas exploration, permit fracking, broaden the tax base and strengthen security, and Colombian bonds strengthened after his election. The peso appreciated around 17% in the first seven months of 2026, prompting Banco de la República to begin accumulating up to $4 billion in additional reserves.

Colombia's principal structural challenge is fiscal consolidation. The 2026 deficit target was raised to 5.3% of GDP, with some private estimates higher, net debt is projected at 58.9% of GDP against a legal ceiling of 71%, and S&P (BB-), Fitch (BB) and Moody's (Baa3) all lowered their ratings between June 2025 and April 2026. Inflation reached 6.29% in September 2026, and the central bank raised its policy rate to 12.25% to bring it back toward its 2–4% target range. The new administration has made consolidation an early priority, with Finance Minister Miguel Gómez Martínez engaging international banks and multilateral lenders to extend maturities and lower borrowing costs. For investors, this combination of a reform-minded government, a credible and independent central bank, a strong currency and a deep PPP pipeline creates an attractive entry point ahead of any improvement in ratings and financing conditions.

How do I get in?

ProColombia (Invest in Colombia), the national investment promotion agency, provides facilitation, sector intelligence and its 2026 Legal Guide for Doing Business in Colombia

General Foreign Investment Regime: registering foreign capital with Banco de la República secures access to the foreign exchange market to remit profits and divestment proceeds, with no tax or fee on the capital transfer itself

Free trade zones offering a preferential 20% income tax rate (against the 35% general rate) for industrial users that meet the export targets in an agreed internationalisation plan

Established concession and public–private partnership (PPP) framework for transport, ports, energy transmission and social infrastructure, which the new administration is expected to accelerate

Hydrocarbons participation through exploration and production contracts and partnership with majority state-owned Ecopetrol, as the new government restarts oil and gas exploration

Free-floating peso managed by Banco de la República, which launched a reserve accumulation programme of up to $4 billion in August 2026 after strong peso appreciation

Treaty-based protection through investment chapters in Colombia's trade agreements, including the 2012 US–Colombia Trade Promotion Agreement, and continued ICSID membership

Trade and market access

Colombia is a WTO member, has been an OECD member since 2020, and holds one of the region's broadest trade-agreement networks, including the 2012 US–Colombia Trade Promotion Agreement, a trade agreement with the European Union, the UK–Andean agreement, and membership of the Pacific Alliance and the Andean Community. Exports reached $50.2 billion in 2025, up 1.3%, with the United States taking 29.6%, the European Union 12.4% and Panama 7.3%; agricultural and food exports grew 33.2%, led by coffee, while non-monetary gold also rose. With ports on both the Caribbean and the Pacific, Colombia is uniquely positioned to serve Atlantic and Asia–Pacific markets, and its growing EU trade provides a valuable diversification channel alongside the US relationship.

The overall risk picture

Colombia's risk profile combines one of Latin America's most diversified economies, a long tradition of macroeconomic institutions and a new government strongly committed to private investment. Growth is accelerating, investment is rising, and the peso has strengthened markedly in 2026, giving foreign investors favourable entry conditions. The main issues are well understood: a fiscal deficit that the new administration has made a priority to reduce, inflation that an independent central bank is actively addressing, regional security challenges that the government is confronting directly, and post-earthquake reconstruction in the Coffee Region. Treaty protection under Colombia's trade agreements, the free-trade-zone regime and multilateral co-financing provide practical mitigation. Investors should build fiscal and interest-rate scenarios into project economics, conduct location-specific security due diligence and track early reform delivery, which would offer meaningful upside if consolidation lifts the sovereign's ratings.

Regulator that awards and administers oil and gas exploration and production contracts

Relevant for upstream entry as exploration is restarted

Agencia Nacional de Infraestructura (ANI)

Key counterpart for road, port, airport and rail infrastructure opportunities

Business networks

Foreign investors can engage through ProColombia, which runs investor services and sector outreach, and through city-level agencies such as Invest in Bogotá. The Colombo-British Chamber of Commerce, AmCham Colombia and the National Business Association of Colombia (ANDI) provide practical business intelligence and access to established local partners. The Inter-American Development Bank, the World Bank Group including IFC, and CAF (the Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean) maintain active programmes in infrastructure, energy and private-sector development, and are natural co-financing partners as the new government seeks to refinance debt and expand concessions. Ecopetrol and the major international energy companies operating in Colombia are useful sources of insight on the upstream reopening.

Who this market suits

Colombia suits a broad range of investors seeking exposure to a large, diversified Latin American market with a new pro-investment government: oil and gas investors positioned for the restart of exploration and offshore gas development; infrastructure and PPP investors, particularly those with multilateral co-financing; construction and building-materials investors supporting reconstruction in the Coffee Region; manufacturing and logistics investors using free-trade-zone incentives and Caribbean and Pacific port access; and technology, business-services and agribusiness investors building on Bogotá's and Medellín's talent base and strong coffee and agricultural exports. Investors with hard-currency funding are well placed to benefit from the stronger peso.
