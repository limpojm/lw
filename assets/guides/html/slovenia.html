The economy today

Slovenia in November 2026 offers investors a high-income, AA-rated euro-area economy at the crossroads of Central Europe, the Adriatic and the Western Balkans, and it is currently one of the fastest-growing economies in the euro area. GDP grew 4.8% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, the strongest rate since early 2022, and 4.1% in the first half, against 1.0% for the euro area; fixed investment rose 13.2%, construction 15.9% and manufacturing 4.3%. Unemployment was just 3.8% in June 2026. Real growth in 2025 has been revised up to 1.5%. The stock of inward FDI reached €24.0 billion at the end of 2025, up 4.0%, led by Austria, Luxembourg and Switzerland, with manufacturing taking 34.1%. The new government of Prime Minister Janez Janša, confirmed in June 2026, has made the JEK2 nuclear project an absolute priority.

Slovenia's main structural challenge is keeping public finances on a sound footing while funding defence, an ageing population and a large energy investment programme. The deficit rose to 2.5% of GDP in 2025 and the revised 2026 budget, adopted on 1 October 2026, targets 2.9%, including extra spending that lifts defence to 2.03% of GDP; the 2027 and 2028 budgets aim to keep the deficit below 3%. Inflation was 3.3% in October 2026. The starting position is strong: public debt fell to 65.1% of GDP in 2025 and is estimated at 64.6% in 2026, and S&P (AA) and Fitch (A+) affirmed their ratings with stable outlooks in September 2026. As Recovery Plan funding ends in 2027, EU cohesion funds and national financing are expected to keep public investment high, creating continued opportunities in energy, transport and digital infrastructure.

How do I get in?

SPIRIT Slovenia, the public business development agency, supports foreign investors with site selection, information and access to incentives

Investment grants for qualifying projects, with strategic-investment status for manufacturing and service projects of at least €40 million and 200 new jobs, or R&D projects of at least €20 million and 100 jobs

Corporate income tax of 22% for 2024–2028 (19% plus a temporary post-flood surcharge), offset by a 100% R&D allowance and a 40% allowance for equipment and intangibles

Euro-area membership: the euro is the national currency and Banka Slovenije is part of the Eurosystem, with free capital movement under EU rules

EU single-market and Schengen membership, giving free movement of goods, services, capital and people and passport-free travel to Italy, Austria and Hungary

EU co-financing through the Recovery and Resilience Plan and cohesion programmes, which keep public investment at about 5.5% of GDP in 2026

Trade and market access

As a member of the EU, Slovenia gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. The euro removes exchange-rate costs for trade with euro-area partners, and Schengen membership allows border-free movement to its EU neighbours. The Port of Koper is a gateway for Central European trade with Asia and the Mediterranean, and the new Divača–Koper second track, operational since March 2026, adds rail capacity on the route to Austria, Hungary and beyond. Slovenia's position and language links also make it a natural base for serving the Western Balkans, and Austria, Switzerland and Germany are among its leading investors.

The overall risk picture

Slovenia's risk profile is that of a stable, high-income EU and euro-area member with strong credit ratings, low unemployment and falling public debt. The economy has accelerated in 2026 on the back of investment and construction, and the country is not subject to sanctions or FATF listing. The main near-term considerations are domestic: a temporary 22% corporate tax rate to 2028, a deficit close to the EU's 3% reference value, rising labour costs and the outcome of the 6 December 2026 referendum on tax and contribution changes. These are manageable within a clear EU fiscal framework and are offset by generous R&D and investment allowances. Investors benefit from the legal certainty of EU law, the convenience of the euro and Schengen access to neighbouring Italy, Austria, Hungary and Croatia.

Government of Slovenia (Prime Minister Janez Janša)

Five-party coalition confirmed on 4 June 2026; sets fiscal, energy and reform policy

Primary reference point for strategic direction and major projects

SPIRIT Slovenia

Public business development agency supporting foreign investors, grants and internationalisation

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentives

Banka Slovenije

National central bank within the Eurosystem; its governor sits on the ECB Governing Council, which held the deposit rate at 2.50% on 29 October 2026

Relevant for banking, payments and financial-sector matters

Ministry of Finance

Leads the budget, tax policy, debt management and EU fiscal compliance

Key counterpart on tax, fiscal and public-finance matters

Ministry of Infrastructure and Energy (Minister Jernej Vrtovec)

Oversees transport, energy and the JEK2 project, which the minister calls an absolute priority

Relevant for energy, rail and port investment

GEN energija

State energy group, co-owner of the Krško plant and investor in JEK2, refining the project's financing model

Essential partner for nuclear and power-sector engagement

Ministry responsible for the economy

Oversees industrial policy, SPIRIT Slovenia and investment incentives

Relevant for grants, strategic-investment status and manufacturing

Business networks

The Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Slovenia, the American Chamber of Commerce Slovenia, the British-Slovenian Chamber of Commerce and the Slovenian-German Chamber of Commerce are the main business associations, with regular dialogue with government. SPIRIT Slovenia and its regional network help investors find partners and incentives, while municipalities negotiate local support such as sites and utility connections case by case. The European Investment Bank and the EU's cohesion and recovery programmes co-finance infrastructure and innovation projects, and GEN energija's JEK2 programme offers a channel for engineering and supply-chain firms. Embassies of major EU partners and the United States maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Slovenia suits investors seeking a high-skill, well-governed base inside the EU and the euro area: manufacturers in automotive, pharmaceuticals and machinery serving Central European supply chains; logistics companies using the Port of Koper and the new Divača–Koper rail link; R&D-intensive and ICT firms able to use the 100% R&D allowance; energy and engineering investors targeting the JEK2 nuclear project, grid and renewables; and companies using Slovenia as a regional headquarters for the Western Balkans.
