The economy today

Bulgaria in November 2026 offers investors a combination that is rare in the EU: euro-area membership and full access to the single market, alongside a 10% flat corporate tax and one of the lowest public-debt levels in the bloc. The euro became the national currency on 1 January 2026, with the European Commission describing the changeover as smooth and efficient, and the Bulgarian National Bank is now part of the Eurosystem and the Single Supervisory Mechanism. GDP grew 3.1% in 2025 and 2.7% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, led by an 8% rise in fixed investment and 7.6% growth in consumption. Foreign direct investment reached €3.26 billion in 2025, about 2.8% of GDP, led by investors from the Netherlands, Greece and Italy. Removal from the FATF grey list at the October 2026 plenary, full Schengen membership and the April 2026 election of a single-party parliamentary majority, the first since 1997, further strengthen the outlook.

Bulgaria's main structural challenge is restoring fiscal discipline and containing inflation while keeping EU-funded investment on track. The 2025 budget deficit reached 3.5% of GDP, the 2026 budget was only adopted in mid-year with a deficit target of 5.7% of GDP, and the EU Council opened an excessive deficit procedure in July 2026 with a 2029 deadline for correction. Consumer price inflation rose to 6.1% in October 2026, in part reflecting higher global energy prices. The starting position is nonetheless strong: public debt was just 29.9% of GDP in 2025, euro membership removes currency risk and lowers funding costs, and the EU framework provides a clear consolidation path. Recovery Plan payments continue, including €896 million received on 31 July 2026, and the government's anti-corruption and reform agenda is aimed at unlocking further EU funds, creating sustained opportunities in energy, transport and digital infrastructure.

How do I get in?

InvestBulgaria Agency (under the Ministry of Economy and Industry) provides free facilitation, site selection and incentive guidance for foreign investors

Investment Promotion Act certification (Class A, Class B and priority projects) giving faster administrative service and access to incentives; the agency issued 25 Class A and B certificates in 2024

National Company Industrial Zones parks and private parks such as the Trakia Economic Zone offering serviced land for manufacturing and logistics

A 10% flat corporate income tax and 10% flat personal income tax, among the lowest rates in the EU

Euro-area membership since 1 January 2026: the euro is the national currency, with no currency risk against the euro and free capital movement under EU rules

EU single-market and Schengen membership, giving goods, services, capital and people free movement across the EU and passport-free travel since January 2025

EU co-financing through the €6.17 billion Recovery and Resilience Plan and EU cohesion programmes for energy, transport, digital and innovation projects

Trade and market access

As a member of the EU, Bulgaria gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Euro adoption in January 2026 removed exchange-rate costs for trade and investment with euro-area partners, and full Schengen membership since January 2025 has ended border checks on land routes to Romania and Greece, cutting freight times. Bulgaria sits on the land route between Central Europe, Turkey and the Middle East, and its Black Sea ports at Burgas and Varna and its Danube ports connect it to wider European and global markets. In energy, Bulgaria is building its section of the Vertical Gas Corridor, linking Greek LNG terminals with Romania, Moldova and Ukraine, with key works due by the end of 2026, positioning the country as a regional energy transit hub.

The overall risk picture

Bulgaria's risk profile is that of a stable EU and euro-area member with low public debt, a competitive tax regime and improving institutional credentials. Euro adoption in January 2026, removal from the FATF grey list in October 2026 and full Schengen membership are lasting gains, and the April 2026 election delivered the first single-party majority since 1997, ending a long period of repeated elections. The main near-term issues are the fiscal deficit, now under an EU excessive deficit procedure, and inflation of 6.1% in October 2026; both are being addressed within a clear EU framework, and debt of 29.9% of GDP leaves ample room for manoeuvre. Investors should note that Lukoil and its Bulgarian subsidiaries, including the Burgas refinery, are subject to US sanctions; activity is permitted only within the limits of US licences currently valid to April 2027, and any dealings require specialist sanctions advice. Outside this specific case, Bulgaria offers investors the legal certainty of EU law and the convenience of the euro.

Who should I talk to first?

Council of Ministers (Prime Minister Rumen Radev)

Sets economic, fiscal and reform policy; the government elected in May 2026 has prioritised anti-corruption and unlocking EU funds

Primary reference point for Bulgaria's strategic direction and major projects

InvestBulgaria Agency

National investment promotion agency; issues Investment Promotion Act certificates and supports investors through the process

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentives

Bulgarian National Bank (BNB)

Member of the Eurosystem since January 2026; its governor sits on the ECB Governing Council, and it supervises banks within the Single Supervisory Mechanism

Relevant for banking, payments and financial-sector matters

Ministry of Finance (Deputy Prime Minister Galab Donev)

Leads the budget, tax policy and the response to the EU excessive deficit procedure

Key counterpart on tax, fiscal and public-finance matters

Ministry of Economy and Industry

Oversees industrial policy, the InvestBulgaria Agency and the National Company Industrial Zones

Relevant for incentives, industrial parks and manufacturing investment

Bulgarian Energy Holding

State energy group whose interests include gas transmission through Bulgartransgaz and a 10% stake in the Han Asparuh Black Sea exploration block

Essential partner for gas, power and energy-infrastructure engagement

Kozloduy NPP – New Builds

State company developing the two new nuclear units with Westinghouse

Counterpart for suppliers and investors in the nuclear programme

Business networks

The American Chamber of Commerce in Bulgaria, the British Bulgarian Chamber of Commerce, the German-Bulgarian Industrial and Trade Chamber and the Bulgarian Chamber of Commerce and Industry are the main business associations, with active sector committees and regular dialogue with government. The InvestBulgaria Agency holds investor events and promotes the country at international forums, while the Bulgarian Industrial Association represents domestic manufacturers. The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development, the European Investment Bank and the World Bank are active in energy, transport and private-sector finance, and EU-funded programmes managed by national ministries offer co-financing for qualifying projects. Embassies of major EU partners and the United States maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Bulgaria suits investors seeking a low-cost, low-tax base inside the EU and the euro area: manufacturers serving European supply chains from industrial zones around Sofia and Plovdiv; IT, software and business-services companies drawing on Sofia's talent pool and the 10% corporate tax; energy and infrastructure investors targeting the nuclear new-build programme, the Vertical Gas Corridor, renewables and EU-funded transport projects; and logistics investors using Black Sea and Danube ports within the Schengen area. Investors in the oil and fuel sector should take sanctions advice on the Lukoil-related assets before any engagement.
