Investor Entry Routes

  • Burkinabè Investment Agency (ABI), the national investment promotion agency, which replaced the former API-BF in 2018
  • Investment Code (Law No. 038-2018/AN) offering privileged tax and customs regimes by investment size, with foreign investors in principle treated equally with domestic investors
  • 2024 Mining Code providing a 15% free-carried state interest and a state option on a further paid stake of at least 30%, with additional equity open to the state and local investors on commercial terms
  • WAEMU membership and the euro-pegged CFA franc under the BCEAO, with exchange controls set by WAEMU rules and transfers made through authorised financial intermediaries
  • OHADA uniform business law, with Commercial Courts in Ouagadougou and Bobo-Dioulasso, ICSID membership since 1966 and the New York Convention on foreign arbitral awards
  • Centre de Formalités des Entreprises (CEFORE) providing one-stop company registration

Market-Entry Observation

Burkina Faso enters late 2026 with markedly stronger macroeconomic foundations. Real GDP grew 5.3% in 2025, up from 4.8% in 2024, and inflation averaged -0.5%. Exports rose about 63% on higher gold prices, turning the current account from a deficit of 3.5% of GDP to a surplus of 6.3%, while the fiscal deficit narrowed from 5.8% to 1.8% of GDP and public debt stood at 53.4% of GDP. In June 2026 the IMF completed its fifth review of the Extended Credit Facility and approved an augmentation taking total access to about $395 million, alongside a Resilience and Sustainability Facility of about $124 million. The IMF expects growth of 3.6% in 2026 and 4.8% in 2027, and government borrowing costs have fallen, with securities yields as low as 7.1% in April 2026.

Security is the defining structural challenge. Burkina Faso remains among the countries most affected by terrorism, around 2.1 million people are internally displaced, and the US advises against all travel. The economy has nonetheless grown by around 5% a year. Policy is also shifting towards greater state participation, notably in gold, where two operating mines and three exploration licences were transferred to the state miner SOPAMIB in June 2025, and the country left ECOWAS in January 2025 to build the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) while remaining in WAEMU and the CFA franc zone. For investors, this points to an opportunity built on partnership: projects aligned with national priorities in power, agro-processing, local mining supply chains and the PND 2026–2030, structured with local partners and backed by robust security and contractual planning.

Investor Risk Note

Burkina Faso combines improving macroeconomic fundamentals — 5.3% growth, a narrowing fiscal deficit, an external surplus and sustained IMF programme support — with a security and political environment that investors must assess plainly. The country is led by military transitional authorities under Captain Ibrahim Traoré; it is not subject to broad international economic sanctions, but the US Level 4 travel advisory and Burkina Faso's suspension of visas for US citizens since 30 December 2025 affect mobility and due diligence. The state's expanding role in mining means project terms, ownership and local participation should be agreed carefully and documented. With a stable CFA franc anchor within WAEMU, IMF engagement and a clear national development plan, investors who use local legal counsel, security advisers and political-risk insurance can position for sectors where national priorities and private capital meet.

Market Access

Burkina Faso is a member of the eight-country West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU), sharing the euro-pegged CFA franc and a regional government securities market, and of the OHADA business-law area. Following its exit from ECOWAS on 29 January 2025, it is a founding member of the Alliance of Sahel States with Mali and Niger, whose Confederal Investment and Development Bank (BCID-AES) was activated in December 2025 to finance transport, energy and mining infrastructure. Burkina Faso has been a WTO member since 1995. Gold dominates exports, going mostly to Switzerland, and trade moves through road and rail corridors to coastal ports in neighbouring states.

Current International Business Channels

The Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Burkina Faso is the principal channel to domestic business, supporting feasibility studies and business links, and CEFORE streamlines company set-up. The Burkinabè Investment Agency is the entry point for Investment Code incentives. The IMF, through its Extended Credit Facility and Resilience and Sustainability Facility, and the World Bank, through its country programme and regular Economic Updates, remain active and are a useful source of macroeconomic and policy insight. The WAEMU regional securities market, the 2026 diaspora bond seeking CFAF 125 billion and the BCID-AES are emerging channels for co-financing infrastructure.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Burkina Faso best suits experienced frontier-market investors with a high tolerance for security risk and a long-term, partnership-based approach: renewable energy and power developers responding to the plan to almost quadruple installed capacity by 2030; agribusiness and agro-processing investors serving domestic and regional markets; mining services, equipment and processing firms supplying a sector with strong state-participation objectives; and telecoms, fintech and financial-services investors serving a growing population. Investors should be comfortable partnering with state entities and planning for security and regional-policy developments.
