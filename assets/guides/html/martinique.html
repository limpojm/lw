Investor Entry Routes

  • Collectivité Territoriale de Martinique (CTM), the single authority exercising regional and departmental powers, leads economic development, business aid and the management of EU funds
  • Tax-free business zones (ZFANG): a 50% profit abatement (capped at €150,000), rising to 80% (capped at €300,000) in priority sectors including tourism, renewable energy, agro-nutrition, R&D, ICT, industry and naval repair, for SMEs with fewer than 250 staff
  • National overseas investment incentives, including productive-investment tax aid under article 199 undecies B of the General Tax Code and LODEOM social-charge exemptions, both maintained in the 2027 draft finance bill
  • Full French and EU legal framework: the euro is the currency, IEDOM acts as the Banque de France's local arm, and there are no capital controls or repatriation restrictions
  • Public co-financing from Bpifrance, Banque des Territoires, AFD and EU regional funds, which support business development and port modernisation
  • Renewable-energy grid connection under a 175 MW target capacity set by the Prefect in March 2026, with geothermal exploration permits and an offshore wind consultation under way
  • Public procurement in housing and infrastructure, including a €300 million recovery plan for social housing and the Grand Port's 2025–2029 strategic programme

Market-Entry Observation

Martinique offers investors a euro-denominated, French and EU jurisdiction in the Eastern Caribbean with the highest income level of the French Antilles: GDP of €11.1 billion in 2025 and GDP per capita of €31,000. Growth was flat at 0.1% in volume in 2025, but household consumption rose 1.0%, inflation was contained at 1.1% (2.0% in June 2026) and bank credit outstanding exceeded €13 billion. IEDOM's business-climate indicator averaged 102.4 in 2025, five points up on 2024, and stood near its long-term average at 99.9 in the second quarter of 2026. Tourism is a clear strength: visitor numbers passed one million in 2025, cruise passengers rose 20%, and hotel stays grew 11.7% year on year in the second quarter of 2026, with foreign guests up 31.5%. Separate FDI statistics are not published, but port modernisation, the maintenance of national overseas tax incentives and a growing renewable-energy pipeline point to continued investment opportunity.

Martinique's main structural challenge is sustaining activity while its population declines and ages: residents numbered 360,630 in 2023, falling about 0.5% a year as young people move to mainland France, and investment fell 6% in volume in 2025, with unemployment at 12.8% in mid-2026. Mitigation is under way on several fronts: a cost-of-living protocol signed in October 2024 has cut average prices by 10.5% on 54 product families, a €300 million social-housing recovery plan has been announced, housing permits rose 7.5% over the year to mid-2026, and a framework agreement signed with the State on 1 July 2026 opens the way to local normative powers and gradual fiscal autonomy within the Constitution. For investors, the opportunities lie in upscale and cruise tourism, renewable energy, housing and construction, port logistics, and health and care services for an ageing population.

Investor Risk Note

Martinique's risk profile is that of a French and EU jurisdiction: legal, currency and transfer risks are low, contracts are enforced under French law, and the euro removes exchange-rate exposure for euro-area investors. Inflation is moderate, household consumption is holding up, business sentiment is close to its long-term average and tourism, particularly cruise and foreign visitors, is growing. The main considerations are structural: a declining and ageing population, weak investment in 2025, unemployment above the national level and continued reliance on imported energy. Cost-of-living tensions led to protests in 2024; the October 2024 protocol has since delivered measurable price reductions. The institutional-evolution process launched with the July 2026 framework agreement is a gradual political process within the French Constitution, and national overseas incentives are being maintained in the 2027 draft finance bill. Investors should take local tax advice, model incentives conservatively, and engage early with the Prefecture and the CTM, while Martinique's high incomes, EU standards and strategic Caribbean position offer a sound base for long-term investment.

Market Access

As a French single territorial collectivity exercising the powers of an overseas department and region, Martinique is an outermost region of the European Union, fully within the EU single market and customs union and using the euro. Through France and the EU it is covered by WTO membership and EU trade agreements, including the EU–CARIFORUM Economic Partnership Agreement with its Caribbean neighbours, and it is an associate member of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States. Its main exports are refined petroleum products, which rose 23% in volume in 2025, bananas and rum, with trade oriented mainly towards mainland France and the neighbouring French territories. The Grand Port Maritime handled 3.2 million tonnes of cargo and 184,705 TEU in 2025, with strengthening ro-ro links to Guadeloupe, and Aimé Césaire airport, with 1.9 million passengers in 2025, connects Martinique with Europe, North America and the Caribbean.

Current International Business Channels

The Collectivité Territoriale de Martinique, working with Bpifrance and Banque des Territoires, is the main channel for business support, co-financing and EU funds, and the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Martinique provides local business liaison. AFD and Banque des Territoires are the principal public financiers for infrastructure, energy and local-authority projects, and IEDOM and Insee publish regular, detailed economic intelligence. The Grand Port Maritime and EDF Martinique are practical sources of insight into logistics and the energy transition, while CTM consultations on offshore wind and public-building solar, and the State–CTM talks on institutional evolution, are useful forums for understanding local priorities. Operators such as Albioma are a source of practical intelligence on biomass and geothermal development.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Martinique suits investors seeking a secure, euro-denominated EU base in the Caribbean with the highest incomes in the French Antilles: hotel, leisure and cruise-related businesses targeting a tourism market that passed one million visitors in 2025; renewable-energy, geothermal and storage investors supporting the shift away from imported fuel; housing and construction firms positioned for the social-housing recovery plan; logistics and distribution firms using a modernising port; and health, care and services providers serving an ageing population. Agri-food and rum producers can use enhanced 80% ZFANG relief to expand local processing and exports.
