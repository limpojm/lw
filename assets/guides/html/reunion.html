The economy today

Réunion offers investors a stable, euro-denominated platform with full French and EU legal protection at the gateway between Europe, Africa and the Indian Ocean. Growth strengthened to 1.1% in 2025 from 0.5% in 2024, outpacing mainland France (0.8%), while household disposable income rose 1.5% in real terms and GDP per capita reached about €28,300. Inflation is subdued at 1.4% in 2025 and 1.4% in August 2026, and private-sector employment grew 0.7% in the year to June 2026. Capacity is expanding: Port Réunion handled a record 6.1 million tonnes in 2025, the airport a record 2.76 million passengers, and equipment loans grew 4.2%. Réunion does not publish separate FDI statistics, but policy momentum is clear: the 2026 Finance Law extended enhanced ZFANG tax relief to disadvantaged communes, and in September 2026 the Région launched an attractiveness strategy under its "La Nouvelle Économie" plan, with Team France Invest support and the CEO Summit 2027 to be hosted on the island.

Réunion's main structural challenge is building a broader private, export-capable economy on a small, distant island. Unemployment was about 19% in mid-2026 (8.3% nationally), although well below the 24% of 2018, and the island imported €7.1 billion of goods in 2024 against €413 million of exports. Exposure to cyclones is real — Cyclone Garance in 2025 caused an estimated €400 million of damage — but it is met by French building and insurance frameworks, dedicated climate-adaptation financing and rapid reconstruction support. Mitigation is substantial: ZFANG and LODEOM lower tax and labour costs, EU and French public finance underpins infrastructure, and renewables already supply around 93% of electricity needs, with full energy autonomy targeted for 2030. For investors, the young and available workforce, a 911,000-strong consumer market and the scope to substitute imports with local production are clear opportunities.

How do I get in?

La Réunion Développement (the regional development agency formerly known as Nexa) offers economic intelligence, project structuring, EU-funding engineering and internationalisation support, working with Team France Invest on investor reception

Tax-free business zones (ZFANG, article 44 quaterdecies CGI): profit abatements of 50% (capped at €150,000) or 80% for priority sectors (capped at €300,000), plus CFE and property-tax relief, for firms with fewer than 250 staff and turnover under €50 million

LODEOM exemptions from employer social-security contributions, used by around 50,000 businesses across the overseas departments; a proposed 2026 tightening was rejected by the National Assembly in November 2025

Overseas productive-investment tax aid (article 199 undecies B CGI) and EU cohesion funding of about €1.4 billion for 2021–2027 (ERDF and ESF+), with the Région as managing authority

Full French and EU legal framework: the euro is the currency, the IEDOM acts as the Banque de France's local arm, and there are no capital controls or repatriation restrictions

Public financing partners: AFD and Banque des Territoires have provided more than €3.8 billion of financing in Réunion over five years for housing, water, mobility and the energy transition

Octroi de mer import-tax regime, a key element of local fiscal autonomy; the European Commission proposed in September 2026 to extend its EU authorisation from end-2027 to 2030

Trade and market access

As a French overseas department and region, Réunion is an outermost region of the European Union, fully within the EU single market and customs union and using the euro, while benefiting from specific EU derogations such as the octroi de mer. Through France, it is covered by WTO membership and EU trade agreements, including the Economic Partnership Agreements with neighbouring Indian Ocean states, and it participates in the Indian Ocean Commission and Interreg Océan Indien cooperation with Mauritius, Madagascar, Seychelles and Comoros. In 2024, exports went to mainland France (37%), Asia (24%), the EU and UK (13%), North America (11%) and Indian Ocean islands (10%), led by fish (29%), sugar (15%) and rum (4%). Port Réunion provides a container and transhipment hub, and air links connect to mainland France (1.63 million passengers in 2025), Mauritius, Mayotte, Madagascar and South Africa, with a new Toulouse route from June 2026.

The overall risk picture

Réunion's risk profile is that of a French and EU jurisdiction: political, legal, currency and transfer risks are low, contracts are enforced under French law, and the euro removes exchange-rate exposure for euro-area investors. Growth is modest but steady — 1.1% in 2025, above the national rate — and inflation is low. The main considerations are structural and operational rather than sovereign: high unemployment, a small and distant market, cyclone exposure, and the periodic review of overseas incentive schemes such as LODEOM, the octroi de mer and ZFANG. Each is well mitigated: incentive reforms have been moderated by Parliament and the EU, French and EU public finance supports infrastructure and reconstruction, and renewables already cover around 93% of electricity needs. Investors should take local tax advice, insure against cyclone risk, and treat incentives as an upside rather than the sole basis of project economics, while the island's stability, EU standards and regional reach offer a dependable base for long-term investment.

Business networks

La Réunion Développement acts as the hub for business networks, running internationalisation missions, sector masterclasses and the Marque La Réunion, and the Région's Comité stratégique de l'internationalisation des entreprises (CoSIE) brings together public and private economic actors. Business France and Team France Invest support investor reception and export promotion in partnership with the Région, while the Chamber of Commerce and Industry (CCI Réunion) and employer federations provide local business liaison. AFD, Banque des Territoires and Bpifrance are the principal public financiers, and the Indian Ocean Commission and Interreg Océan Indien programme open channels to Mauritius, Madagascar, Seychelles and Comoros. The CEO Summit 2027, to be hosted in Réunion, offers a timely platform for networking and deal origination.

Who this market suits

Réunion suits investors seeking a secure, euro-denominated EU base with access to the Indian Ocean and East Africa: renewable-energy and circular-economy investors building on the island's 2030 energy-autonomy goal and waste-to-energy projects; logistics, port-services and ship-repair investors leveraging Port Réunion's transhipment role; tourism and hospitality investors benefiting from rising visitor stays and record air traffic; and agro-food, ICT, video-game and light-industry investors able to use enhanced ZFANG relief. Construction, housing and climate-adaptation specialists will find a strong public-investment pipeline, and firms targeting regional markets can use Réunion as a French-law platform for wider Indian Ocean operations.
