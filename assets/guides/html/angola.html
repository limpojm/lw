Investor Entry Routes

  • AIPEX (Agency for Private Investment and Export Promotion), the sole investment regulatory and promotion body, operating a Single Investment Window for incorporation, licences, visas and site identification
  • Private Investment Law (Law 10/18, amended by Law 10/21), with Prior Declaration, Special and Contractual regimes; approvals not decided on time are deemed tacitly granted
  • Contractual Regime for projects of at least $10 million creating 50 or more local jobs, with tax reductions of up to 15 years and customs exemptions negotiated with AIPEX under the Tax Benefits Code (Law 8/22)
  • Development zones A to D offering location-based incentives, alongside the Luanda-Bengo Special Economic Zone and the Barra do Dande free zone
  • Banco Nacional de Angola oversight of foreign exchange; the investment law guarantees the transfer abroad of dividends, liquidation proceeds and royalties, with the 2021 amendments removing the requirement to prove full project implementation first
  • Oil and gas entry through licensing by ANPG, the national concessionaire, and partnerships with Sonangol
  • PROPRIV privatisation programme, including planned partial privatisation of Sonangol and divestment of non-core state assets

Market-Entry Observation

Angola in October 2026 is enjoying its strongest growth momentum in years, led by the non-oil economy. GDP expanded 7.15% in the first half of 2026, following 3.1% in 2025, with second-quarter growth of 8.74% year-on-year and the non-oil sector up 9.24%; information and communication (+19.7%), transport and storage (+14.2%) and manufacturing (+9.7%) led the first-half gains. Inflation has fallen from 13.35% in February to 8.78% in August, allowing the Banco Nacional de Angola to cut its policy rate from 17.5% to 14.75% between March and September. Non-oil foreign direct investment reached $959.4 million in 2025, up 171% and the largest jump in 13 years, while a $2.5 billion Eurobond in March 2026 drew around $5.2 billion of demand. A modernised Private Investment Law, a Single Investment Window and a new Tax Benefits Code have simplified entry.

Angola's main structural challenge is its reliance on oil, which accounts for more than 90% of exports; production of about 1.03 million barrels a day in 2025 was lower than in previous years, and the IMF has highlighted the need for diversification. The country is addressing this on two fronts. In energy, new projects such as the $6 billion Kaminho development aim to keep output above 1 million barrels a day, and Angola's exit from OPEC in December 2023 gives it greater freedom over production. Beyond oil, diamond exports rose 21% in 2025, the Longonjo rare-earths project is due to start commercial production in 2027, and the Lobito Corridor reached a $786 million financial close in 2026 to expand mineral and agricultural freight. Buffers remain sound: reserves stood at $15.32 billion in August 2026, covering 6.6 months of imports, and the trade surplus reached $13.55 billion in the first eight months of the year.

Investor Risk Note

Angola's risk profile is that of a resource-rich economy in the middle of a determined diversification drive, with improving macroeconomic indicators. Inflation and interest rates are falling, non-oil growth is outpacing the oil sector, and renewed market access, shown by strong demand for the March 2026 Eurobond, signals growing investor confidence. Investors should note two practical considerations: Angola's inclusion on the FATF list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring requires robust know-your-customer and beneficial-ownership documentation and careful choice of banking partners, and foreign-exchange liquidity can affect the timing of remittances. Both are being addressed through AML legislative reform and IMF-recommended exchange-rate measures. With reserves covering more than six months of imports, a sizeable trade surplus and a supportive investment law that guarantees profit repatriation, Angola offers well-structured investors access to one of Southern Africa's largest economies at an early stage of its non-oil expansion.

Market Access

Angola is a member of the Southern African Development Community (SADC) and has recently acceded to the SADC Free Trade Area, while developing its national strategy under the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA). It is a WTO member and, having left OPEC in December 2023, sets its own oil production policy. China takes about 47% of exports, followed by India (11.8%) and Indonesia (6.4%), with oil the dominant export and diamonds a growing second. The Lobito Corridor, linking the Port of Lobito by rail to the Copperbelts of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Zambia and supported by US, South African and African Development Bank financing, is positioning Angola as an Atlantic gateway for regional mineral and agricultural trade. Bilateral agreements continue to expand, including a double-taxation treaty and investment-protection agreement signed with Serbia in 2026.

Current International Business Channels

AIPEX organises investor promotion and forums and operates the Single Investment Window, while the Ministry of Industry and Commerce leads Angola's AfCFTA implementation work. Bilateral business bodies such as the US–Angola Chamber of Commerce support trade and investment links, and the EU and Angola maintain an active economic dialogue. Development finance institutions are highly active: the US International Development Finance Corporation and the Development Bank of Southern Africa financed the Lobito Atlantic Railway, the African Development Bank is funding the Zambian rail link to Lobito, and the Africa Finance Corporation advises on corridor development. The IMF's regular Article IV engagement and major international energy companies with long-standing Angolan operations are useful sources of practical market intelligence.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Angola suits investors with a medium- to long-term horizon seeking early positions in a large, diversifying economy: logistics, rail and mining-services investors drawn by the Lobito Corridor; agribusiness and food-processing investors building on abundant arable land and import-substitution potential estimated at $3 billion; critical-minerals and diamond investors; and ICT and telecoms investors benefiting from the fastest-growing sector of 2026. Oil and gas companies benefit from a well-established upstream industry and ANPG licensing, while privatisation offers entry points for strategic and financial investors. All investors should plan for robust compliance processes and foreign-exchange management.
