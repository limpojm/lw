The economy today

China in November 2026 remains the world's largest manufacturing base, with growth that continues to outpace most major economies. GDP grew 5.0% in 2025, surpassing 140 trillion yuan, and expanded 4.6% in the first three quarters of 2026 (5.0% in Q1, 4.3% in Q2 and 4.5% in Q3) against a 2026 target of 4.5–5%. Innovation is a clear driver: high-tech manufacturing value added rose 13.3% and equipment manufacturing 9.3% in H1 2026, while goods exports rose 13.4% in yuan terms. Foreign investors are still establishing new businesses at pace: 70,392 foreign-invested enterprises were set up in 2025 (+19.1%) and 37,711 in January–July 2026 (+4.4%). Actual use of foreign investment was 747.69 billion yuan ($106.92 billion) in 2025, down 9.5%, and 438.33 billion yuan in January–July 2026, down 6.2%, but high-tech industries' share has risen to 41.6%. Liberalisation continues through a shorter national negative list, 23 pilot FTZs and the launch of Hainan's island-wide customs operations in December 2025.

China's main structural challenge is twofold: rebalancing growth towards household demand, and operating within an increasingly regulated technology and trade relationship with the United States. Consumer prices rose 0.9% in September 2026 and retail sales 2.7% in H1, while fixed-asset investment fell 5.7% amid the continuing property adjustment. Policy remains supportive, with the one-year and five-year loan prime rates at 3.0% and 3.5% and the seven-day reverse repo rate at 1.4%, and foreign-exchange reserves of $3.40 trillion provide a deep external buffer. On the external side, the US–China trade truce agreed in late 2025 has been extended to 10 January 2027, and leaders met in Beijing in May and Washington in September 2026. For investors, the result is a market where resilience comes from scale and supply-chain depth, mitigation comes from rigorous dual-jurisdiction compliance, and the opportunity lies in consumption, services opening and advanced manufacturing for the domestic market.

How do I get in?

Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) and its Invest in China investment promotion services, alongside provincial and municipal commerce bureaus that lead day-to-day investor facilitation

Foreign Investment Law framework with national treatment outside the 2024 national negative list, which has been cut to 29 restricted or prohibited items

23 pilot Free Trade Zones operating a shorter 27-item negative list, plus the Hainan Free Trade Port with island-wide special customs operations since December 2025

Preferential tax regimes, including a 15% corporate income tax rate for qualifying firms in Hainan and in Shanghai's Lingang New Area

People's Bank of China and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) oversight of a managed renminbi; profit repatriation operates through banks on documented transactions within China's capital-account framework

Cross-border data routes under the Personal Information Protection Law (CAC security assessment, standard contract, or certification, the latter in force since 1 January 2026)

Joint ventures and partnerships with Chinese companies and local governments for sectors that remain on the negative list

Trade and market access

China has been a WTO member since 2001 and is a founding party to the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), the world's largest free trade agreement, alongside a broad network of bilateral FTAs including with ASEAN. It has applied to join the CPTPP and the Digital Economy Partnership Agreement. Total goods trade exceeded 45 trillion yuan ($6.4 trillion) in 2025, with a record $1.2 trillion surplus. Trade is diversifying quickly: exports to ASEAN rose 13.4%, to the EU 8.4% and to Africa 25.8% in 2025, while exports to the US fell 20%. ASEAN, the EU and the US are the main export markets, connected by the Belt and Road Initiative, China–Europe rail freight and world-leading container ports. For investors, China offers both a vast domestic market and a regional production platform with preferential access across Asia.

The overall risk picture

China combines unmatched scale, deep supply chains and strong innovation momentum with a legal environment that investors need to manage carefully. The material compliance considerations are clear and should be addressed at the outset. US export controls and Entity List designations restrict dealings in advanced technology and with listed companies. US outbound investment rules prohibit or require notification of certain US-person investments in Chinese semiconductors, quantum and AI, and the COINS Act extends this to further sectors. Supply chains linked to Xinjiang are subject to the US Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act. China's Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law, Unreliable Entity List, blocking rules and the April 2026 extraterritorial-jurisdiction and supply-chain regulations can expose firms that apply foreign restrictions to Chinese counter-measures, and data and export-control laws govern cross-border data and critical minerals. These rules do not prevent investment in China's large consumer, services, healthcare and general manufacturing sectors, and the extension of the US–China truce to January 2027 has reduced near-term volatility. Investors should obtain sanctions and export-control advice in both jurisdictions, screen counterparties and ownership chains, and build structures that are compliant on both sides.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the President (Xi Jinping) and the State Council (Premier Li Qiang)

Set national strategy, including the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), opening-up policy and annual growth targets

Primary reference point for China's policy direction and priority sectors

Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) and Invest in China

Leads foreign investment policy, the negative list, trade negotiations and the Unreliable Entity List

Standard first point of contact for market entry, and key counterpart on trade-policy matters

People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Central bank; sets monetary policy and the loan prime rate framework and oversees the renminbi

Relevant for financing conditions, currency and financial-sector entry

State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Manages foreign-exchange reserves and capital-account rules, including profit repatriation

Essential for structuring capital flows and repatriation

National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC)

Economic planning agency that co-issues the negative list and approves major projects

Relevant for large-scale projects and sector policy

Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC)

Regulates cybersecurity and cross-border data transfers under the PIPL and Data Security Law

Key regulator for any data-intensive or digital investment

China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT)

National trade-promotion body and business convenor, with links to arbitration services

Useful channel for business delegations, matchmaking and trade fairs

Business networks

The China-Britain Business Council and the British Chamber of Commerce in China are the main channels for UK companies, alongside the American Chamber of Commerce in China and the European Union Chamber of Commerce in China, which publish regular business-confidence surveys and run active regulatory dialogue. CCPIT and provincial commerce bureaus organise investment matchmaking and delegations. Major forums include the China International Import Expo in Shanghai, the China Development Forum in Beijing and the World Economic Forum's Annual Meeting of the New Champions. The World Bank, IFC and Asian Development Bank remain active in China, and the Beijing-based Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank and the Shanghai-based New Development Bank are useful contacts for regional infrastructure finance.

Who this market suits

China best suits investors with clear sector focus and strong compliance capacity: consumer, retail and brand investors addressing a market of 1.4 billion people; healthcare, eldercare and life-sciences firms responding to demographic change; advanced-manufacturing and industrial investors pursuing "China-for-China" production and access to the world's deepest supplier base; and financial-services and asset-management firms using steady financial-sector opening. Green-energy and electric-vehicle supply-chain partners can benefit from China's scale in batteries and renewables. Investors in sensitive technology, and any investor that is a US person or has US connections, should first confirm what is lawfully permissible under export-control and outbound investment rules.
