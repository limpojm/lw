Investor Entry Routes

  • Invest Guatemala, the national investment promotion agency, together with ProGuatemala in the Ministry of Economy, provides information, site-visit coordination, contacts and help with permits for foreign investors
  • Foreign Investment Law (1998) granting foreign investors the same property and ownership rights as nationals, with no inbound screening and expropriation only with prior compensation; Guatemala is an ICSID member and CAFTA-DR provides investor-state dispute settlement
  • Free Trade Zones under Decree 65-89 (as amended) and Special Public Economic Development Zones (ZDEEPs) under Decrees 22-73 and 30-2018, offering 10-year income tax exemptions and VAT and duty relief
  • Export and Maquila regime offering a 10-year income tax exemption and relief on machinery and inputs for apparel, textile and services exporters
  • Open foreign-exchange regime overseen by the Banco de Guatemala: no restrictions on converting or transferring investment funds, US dollars freely available and a quetzal that has been broadly stable since 1999
  • Competitive electricity tenders run by the Ministry of Energy and Mines and CNEE: the PEG-5 round awarded up to 1,400 MW to 57 companies on contracts of up to 15 years, with a PEG-6 round in preparation
  • Public-private partnerships through ANADIE and the priority road infrastructure law, supporting private participation in roads, ports and logistics

Market-Entry Observation

Guatemala in October 2026 combines Central America's largest economy with one of the region's most consistent macroeconomic records. GDP grew 4.3% in 2025 and economic activity expanded 4.4% in the first half of 2026, while the IMF projects 3.8% growth for 2026 converging to around 4% over the medium term. Public debt is low at around 27% of GDP, international reserves reached a record $32.7 billion at end-June 2026, and the current account recorded a surplus of 4.7% of GDP in 2025. Exports rose 7.1% to $15.6 billion in 2025 and foreign direct investment reached a preliminary $1.9 billion, up from about $1.6 billion in 2024, with a further 6.9% rise in January–May 2026. All three major agencies rate Guatemala one notch below investment grade (Moody's Ba1, S&P and Fitch BB+). The Agreement on Reciprocal Trade signed with the United States in January 2026, the 1,400 MW PEG-5 electricity tender and the appointment of a new Attorney General in May 2026 add further momentum.

Guatemala's main structural challenge is its infrastructure gap, the product of many years of low public investment and one of the lowest tax-revenue ratios in the region. Ports, roads and power networks have not kept pace with a growing, export-oriented economy. The government and its partners are now addressing this directly: Puerto Quetzal is being expanded with US Army Corps of Engineers support, the PEG-5 tender will bring new generation online from 2030, a priority road infrastructure law and renewed PPP framework are opening projects to private capital, and the IMF has endorsed scaling up public investment within a 2% of GDP deficit target. With a low debt burden, ample reserves and remittance inflows of around 20% of GDP sustaining domestic demand, Guatemala has the fiscal room to close the gap, creating sustained opportunities for investors in transport, energy, logistics and construction.

Investor Risk Note

Guatemala's risk profile is anchored by some of the strongest macroeconomic fundamentals in Latin America: low public debt of around 27% of GDP, record international reserves of $32.7 billion, a current account surplus, inflation within the central bank's target band and a stable quetzal with no restrictions on profit repatriation. These strengths have kept all three major rating agencies one notch below investment grade, with an upgrade a realistic medium-term prospect. The principal considerations for investors are governance and judicial predictability, community consultation for large extractive and energy projects, security, and the 2027 election cycle. Each can be managed: international arbitration and CAFTA-DR protections provide recourse, early community engagement underpins social licence, and the May 2026 change of Attorney General is a meaningful step for institutional confidence. Investors focused on manufacturing, services, renewable energy and infrastructure can position for the country's next phase of growth with confidence.

Market Access

Guatemala is a WTO member and part of the Dominican Republic–Central America–United States Free Trade Agreement (CAFTA-DR), its most important trade framework, complemented by the Agreement on Reciprocal Trade signed with the United States on 30 January 2026, which removes US reciprocal tariffs on CAFTA-DR textiles and apparel and on goods the US cannot produce in sufficient quantity. Central America is Guatemala's largest export market, taking 36.4% of exports in 2025, followed by the United States at 30.5%, and Guatemala participates in Central American economic integration. Association Agreements with the European Union and the United Kingdom provide preferential access to European markets. Leading exports include apparel, coffee, bananas, sugar and palm oil. With ports on both the Pacific and the Caribbean and a land border with Mexico, Guatemala is a natural nearshoring and logistics platform for North American and regional supply chains.

Current International Business Channels

The American Chamber of Commerce of Guatemala (AmCham Guatemala) is the leading foreign business association, alongside CACIF, the umbrella body for Guatemala's private sector, and AGEXPORT, the influential exporters' association with strong sector networks in apparel, agri-food and services. Invest Guatemala organises investor outreach and sector events. The US International Development Finance Corporation has a dozen active projects, mainly in finance and insurance, and the World Bank, Inter-American Development Bank and Central American Bank for Economic Integration are active in infrastructure, energy and private-sector development. ZOLIC works with foreign commercial counsellors to connect investors with zone opportunities.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Guatemala suits investors seeking a stable, low-debt economy with a strategic location next to Mexico and the United States: nearshoring manufacturers and apparel producers using ZDEEP and maquila incentives and CAFTA-DR access; renewable-energy developers participating in the PEG generation tenders; infrastructure, port and logistics investors positioned for the PPP pipeline; financial-services firms, the largest recipients of foreign direct investment in 2025; and ICT and business-services providers drawing on a young workforce. Agri-food investors can build on world-class coffee, banana, sugar and palm oil sectors, while extractive and large hydro investors should plan for thorough community consultation from the outset.
