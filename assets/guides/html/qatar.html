Investor Entry Routes

  • Invest Qatar (Investment Promotion Agency Qatar) provides one-stop facilitation, market intelligence and sector guidance for foreign investors
  • Law No. 1 of 2019 on non-Qatari capital investment permits up to 100% foreign ownership in most sectors, with protection from expropriation, arbitration of disputes and customs exemptions for project equipment; banking, insurance, natural resources and commercial agencies remain subject to separate rules
  • Qatar Financial Centre (QFC), an onshore platform with its own common-law-based regulator and court, and a 10% corporate tax rate, for financial, professional and technology businesses
  • Qatar Free Zones Authority (QFZA) zones at Ras Bufontas and Umm Alhoul, offering 100% foreign ownership, renewable 20-year corporate tax holidays and zero customs duties
  • Qatari riyal pegged to the US dollar at 3.64, with full capital repatriation and no personal income tax; the standard corporate tax rate outside the zones is 10%
  • Public-private partnership framework (2020) for infrastructure and services, and joint ventures with QatarEnergy for energy-sector participation
  • Law No. 16 of 2018 permitting non-Qatari freehold property ownership in designated areas and usufruct rights of up to 99 years elsewhere

Market-Entry Observation

Qatar combines one of the world's largest sovereign balance sheets with a fast-growing pool of foreign business activity. In 2025 the country recorded 373 FDI projects, up 52% year on year, with $3.4 billion of capital expenditure and more than 15,000 new jobs, and rose 21 places to 12th in fDi Intelligence's Greenfield FDI Performance Index. More than 14,500 non-Qatari companies registered in 2025, and the Qatar Financial Centre onboarded 1,135 firms in the first half of 2026, up 37%, taking its total above 4,700. The non-hydrocarbon economy grew 2.6% in the second quarter of 2026, led by real estate (+11%), finance and insurance (+8.3%), trade (+6.9%) and construction (+5.5%). Up to 100% foreign ownership under Law No. 1 of 2019, tax-free zones and a dollar-pegged riyal underpin the investment case, and all three rating agencies affirmed Qatar's AA/Aa2 ratings with stable outlooks in 2026.

Qatar's main structural challenge is its reliance on LNG exports through the Strait of Hormuz, which the 2026 Iran war has brought into sharp focus. Iranian strikes in March halted Ras Laffan and damaged two LNG trains representing 17% of capacity, with repairs expected to take three to five years; shipping restrictions cut second-quarter LNG exports to under 2 million tonnes, against around 20 million a quarter in 2025. The IMF projects a 15.6% contraction in 2026, and the second-quarter budget deficit reached QR21.2 billion ($5.8 billion). Qatar has responded from a position of exceptional strength: sovereign net foreign assets of around 227% of GDP in 2025, official reserves up 10.85% year on year to QR223.4 billion in September, a restart of exports to about one-third of pre-war levels from September, and a leading role in mediating US–Iran talks. Fitch expects a strong rebound in 2027–28 as the North Field expansion comes on stream, creating opportunities in energy services, logistics and the wider non-hydrocarbon economy.

Investor Risk Note

Qatar's risk profile reflects an economy with world-class financial buffers that is managing a large but external shock. The 2026 Iran war halted LNG production in March, damaged 17% of export capacity and has kept Strait of Hormuz traffic well below normal, producing a sharp but temporary fall in output and a widening budget deficit. The fundamentals beneath this remain strong: sovereign net foreign assets of over twice GDP, rising official reserves, a credible dollar peg, AA/Aa2 ratings affirmed with stable outlooks by all three agencies, and a non-hydrocarbon economy that has kept growing. Exports restarted in September at about one-third of pre-war levels, and the North Field expansion is set to lift capacity well above pre-war levels from 2027. Investors should conduct thorough security, insurance and shipping due diligence and follow the Qatar-mediated US–Iran talks closely, while recognising that Qatar is well placed to lead the regional recovery once passage through Hormuz normalises.

Market Access

Qatar is a member of the Gulf Cooperation Council customs union and the Greater Arab Free Trade Area, a WTO member since 1996, and a party to the UK–GCC free trade agreement concluded in 2026. Its LNG is sold mainly under long-term contracts to Asian buyers, including China, India, South Korea and Japan, and to European customers such as Italy and Belgium, while QatarEnergy's 18 million tonne Golden Pass LNG joint venture in the United States, whose first train began production in March 2026, adds an export platform outside the Gulf. Hamad Port and Hamad International Airport provide container and air-cargo connectivity, and Qatar is using ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman and Red Sea routing to keep cargoes moving. This combination of GCC integration, global energy relationships and an international supply portfolio gives Qatar a resilient market access profile that will strengthen further as shipping normalises.

Current International Business Channels

The Qatar Chamber is the principal domestic business association, alongside the American Chamber of Commerce in Qatar and other national business groups representing Western and Asian investors. Invest Qatar runs regular investor outreach and sector events, and the Qatar Economic Forum, Powered by Bloomberg, organised with the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, and Web Summit Qatar, which drew around 2,300 business licensing applications to the QFC in 2026, are leading networking platforms. Qatar Development Bank supports local small and medium-sized enterprises and is a useful partner for supply-chain and localisation projects. International energy companies with stakes at Ras Laffan and in the North Field expansion maintain substantial in-country presence and are a valuable source of practical intelligence on energy logistics.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Qatar suits investors seeking a well-capitalised, AA-rated Gulf base with long-term growth anchored in the world's largest LNG expansion programme: energy-services, engineering and construction firms positioned for the North Field expansion and Ras Laffan repairs; financial-services, fintech and professional-services firms using the QFC's common-law platform; technology and media companies drawn by QSTP and the QFC's fast-growing technology cluster; and logistics and downstream manufacturing investors using the tax-free Umm Alhoul and Ras Bufontas zones. Investors with shipping or export exposure should build Hormuz disruption scenarios into planning, while those focused on the domestic and services economy benefit from continued non-hydrocarbon growth.
