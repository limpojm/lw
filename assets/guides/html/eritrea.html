Investor Entry Routes

  • Investment Center, under the Office of the President: in practice, significant foreign investment is agreed through direct negotiation with the government rather than under the 1994 Investment Proclamation (No. 59/1994)
  • Mining licences under Eritrea's mining legislation and 2011 regulations, with the state's ENAMCO taking a 10% free stake and an option to buy a further 30%; joint ventures with ENAMCO are the established entry model
  • Mining is the sector the government actively encourages; most other sectors are effectively closed to foreign investment, and foreigners cannot generally own real property
  • Currency regime: the nakfa is non-convertible and fixed at 15 per US dollar, and all transfers in and out require central-bank approval, so repatriation and hard-currency terms should be secured in the negotiated agreement
  • Bilateral investment treaty with Italy in force; treaties with the Netherlands, Qatar and Uganda signed but not yet in force
  • Free Trade Zones designated at Massawa and on the Sudan border, offering a framework for future logistics and trade investment once operational
  • US sanctions designations of the ruling PFDJ, the Eritrean Defense Forces and affiliated companies were removed in September 2026, widening the scope for lawful engagement subject to standard screening

Market-Entry Observation

Eritrea in October 2026 offers a small, mining-led economy with notable external strengths and a growing pipeline of mineral projects. The African Development Bank estimates real GDP growth of 3.2% in 2025, with 2.8% projected for 2026 and 3.2% for 2027, driven by mining and services, while inflation eased from 7.5% in 2024 to 5.3% in 2025. The current account surplus reached an estimated 13.2% of GDP in 2025, and concessional borrowing keeps debt service low. Mining is expanding: the Bisha mine produced around 23,000 tonnes of copper and 83,000 tonnes of zinc in 2025, the Asmara polymetallic project delivered its first concentrate in April 2026, and national copper output is forecast at about 30,000 tonnes in 2026. Western explorers are also returning, with Alpha Exploration drilling at Kerkasha since July 2026, and the removal of US sanctions designations on the ruling party, the military and affiliated companies in September 2026 has widened the scope for lawful engagement.

Eritrea's main structural challenge is the combination of a closed, state-directed economic model and a tense regional environment. Investment terms are negotiated directly with the state, the nakfa is non-convertible, and the banking system has no correspondent relationships with foreign banks. Relations with Ethiopia worsened in 2026, and diplomatic ties were cut on 1 October amid renewed fighting in northern Ethiopia, although Eritrea denies involvement. Investors can mitigate these risks by structuring through ENAMCO joint ventures, securing currency and repatriation terms upfront, using offshore financing and putting robust security planning in place. The established Bisha, Zara and Asmara operations show that large mining projects can be built and run under this model. Eritrea's 1,200 km Red Sea coastline, its world-class potash at Colluli and its ports at Massawa and Assab give long-term investors significant upside from any future regional normalisation.

Investor Risk Note

Eritrea's risk profile is that of a frontier, state-directed economy with genuine mineral wealth, low external debt service and a strong current account surplus. Investors should be clear-eyed about its realities. Regional tensions escalated in October 2026 with the break in diplomatic relations with Ethiopia. The currency is non-convertible, and the investment framework rests on direct negotiation with the state. Labour practices linked to national service also require careful human-rights due diligence. The September 2026 removal of US designations on the ruling party, the military and affiliated companies is a meaningful step, but it is not a blanket lifting of restrictions: other listings remain, so every counterparty should be screened with sanctions counsel. Within these parameters, mining has proved workable. Bisha is in full production, Zara has produced gold since 2016 and the Asmara project reached first concentrate in 2026. Investors who negotiate clear fiscal, currency and dispute-resolution terms upfront, and who plan for security and energy needs, can position themselves early in one of the Red Sea's least-explored mineral provinces.

Market Access

Eritrea is a founding member of COMESA, giving a framework for preferential trade within Eastern and Southern Africa. It has no affiliation with the WTO, and it withdrew from IGAD in December 2025. It signed a Belt and Road Initiative memorandum with China in 2021, and Chinese companies hold majority stakes in the main operating and development mines. Mineral concentrates (copper, zinc and gold) are the principal exports. With around 1,200 km of Red Sea coastline and ports at Massawa and Assab close to one of the world's busiest shipping lanes linking Europe, the Middle East and Asia, Eritrea holds a strategic position. That position would offer substantial logistics and transit upside under a future regional settlement.

Current International Business Channels

Eritrea has no independent chambers of commerce or business associations. The state-linked National Confederation of Eritrean Workers is the only legal union, so engagement runs mainly through government channels: the Investment Center, the Ministry of Energy and Mines and ENAMCO. Foreign embassies in Asmara and the established mining operators, including Zijin and Sichuan Road and Bridge Mining, are valuable sources of practical intelligence. The African Development Bank maintains an active country programme. The US Development Finance Corporation is authorised to operate in Eritrea but is not currently active there, a channel that may become more relevant after the 2026 sanctions changes. International mining conferences and Canadian-listed explorers active in the country offer further networking routes.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Eritrea best suits experienced, risk-tolerant investors with long horizons and strong compliance capability. These include mining companies and junior explorers seeking underexplored gold, copper, zinc and potash ground, and strategic investors in critical minerals and fertilisers. Mining-services, equipment and off-grid power providers can supply the existing operations. Logistics and port investors may also position early for any future Red Sea normalisation. The market is less suited to consumer, retail or financial-services investors, given restrictions on most non-mining sectors, the non-convertible currency and the absence of foreign banking.
