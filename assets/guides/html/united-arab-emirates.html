Investor Entry Routes

  • Ministry of Investment, with Abu Dhabi Investment Office (ADIO) and Dubai's investment agencies at emirate level, providing facilitation, incentives and sector guidance for foreign investors
  • Commercial Companies Law (Federal Law No. 26 of 2020, as amended in 2021) permitting full foreign ownership for most mainland commercial and industrial activities, with conditions retained for a limited set of strategic-impact sectors
  • More than 40 free zones offering 100% foreign ownership and a 0% corporate tax rate on qualifying income for Qualifying Free Zone Persons; Dubai Executive Council Resolution No. 11 of 2025 now lets most non-financial Dubai free-zone companies operate on the mainland
  • Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) and the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC), financial free zones with their own common-law frameworks, courts and regulators
  • Competitive tax regime: 9% federal corporate tax above AED 375,000 of profit, a 15% Domestic Minimum Top-Up Tax for multinational groups with revenue of €750 million or more, and no personal income tax
  • Dollar-pegged dirham with full repatriation of profits, no minimum capital for limited liability companies, and customs tariffs of 0–5%
  • Golden Visa long-term residence of 5–10 years for investors, entrepreneurs and skilled professionals, extendable to family members

Market-Entry Observation

The UAE entered 2026 as the Gulf's most diversified and fastest-growing economy. GDP grew 6.2% in 2025 to Dh1.9 trillion, with non-oil GDP up 6.8%, and the country attracted a record Dh177.3 billion ($48.3 billion) of foreign direct investment, up 6%, ranking 9th in the world for FDI inflows. Greenfield investment accounted for close to 45% of inflows, with 1,562 projects announced, led by manufacturing and communications. Momentum has continued through 2026: GDP rose 0.4% in the first half despite regional conflict, with finance and insurance up 14.8% and information and communication up 7.3%; non-oil foreign trade rose 13.1% to Dh1.937 trillion and non-oil exports reached a record Dh452.8 billion, up 23.9%. Full foreign ownership onshore, more than 40 free zones, a 9% corporate tax rate and the UAE's growing network of comprehensive economic partnership agreements continue to underpin the investment case.

The UAE's main structural challenge in 2026 is the regional conflict that began on 28 February and has sharply restricted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian missile and drone attacks on the UAE, including a strike on the Fujairah oil zone in May, and travel disruption led to a 2.1% contraction in the second quarter, and the IMF now projects a 3.9% contraction for 2026. The UAE has responded from a position of strength: Fitch affirmed its AA- rating with a stable outlook in May, citing a consolidated fiscal surplus, government debt of around 27% of GDP and Abu Dhabi sovereign foreign assets of about 164% of GDP. Crude exports have been redirected through the Habshan–Fujairah pipeline, a second bypass line is being fast-tracked for 2027, and the UAE left OPEC on 1 May 2026 to pursue its 5 million barrels a day capacity. The non-oil PMI held at 55.3 in September, and the IMF expects growth to rebound to 7.7% in 2027, creating opportunities in energy logistics, AI infrastructure and the wider non-oil economy.

Investor Risk Note

The UAE's risk profile reflects a diversified, highly rated economy managing a significant external shock from a position of exceptional financial strength. Its open investment framework, with full foreign ownership onshore, more than 40 free zones, the common-law ADGM and DIFC, a competitive tax regime and a dollar-pegged currency, attracted record FDI in 2025 and ranked the country 9th globally for inflows. The 2026 Iran war has restricted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and brought missile and drone attacks on UAE territory, pushing the economy into a projected contraction this year; however, Fitch's AA- affirmation, a consolidated fiscal surplus, low government debt and large sovereign assets provide substantial buffers. The Fujairah export route, the fast-tracked second bypass pipeline and a non-oil PMI firmly in expansion in September demonstrate resilience. Investors should build shipping and security scenarios into planning and apply standard sanctions screening for Iran-linked counterparties, while the IMF's projected 7.7% rebound in 2027 points to a strong recovery as the region stabilises.

Market Access

The UAE is a member of the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), giving preferential access to the other five Gulf markets, and a member of the WTO. Its CEPA programme of bilateral comprehensive economic partnership agreements continues to expand, with non-oil trade with partners whose agreements are fully in force reaching Dh304.3 billion in the first half of 2026, and the UK–GCC free trade agreement concluded in 2026 will further deepen ties with the United Kingdom. China, Switzerland and India are the UAE's largest non-oil trading partners. Jebel Ali, Khalifa Port and the Fujairah terminals give the UAE world-class maritime connectivity on both sides of the Strait of Hormuz, while Dubai and Abu Dhabi airports connect it to markets across Asia, Africa and Europe. This combination of trade agreements, logistics infrastructure and re-export capability makes the UAE a natural regional base for serving the Gulf, South Asia and Africa.

Current International Business Channels

The British Chamber of Commerce Dubai, the British Business Group Abu Dhabi and the American Chamber of Commerce in Dubai and Abu Dhabi are the leading Western business associations, alongside the Dubai Chambers and the Abu Dhabi Chamber for domestic business liaison. The Ministry of Investment and partners host Investopia, whose fifth edition took place in Abu Dhabi in March 2026, while ADIO co-hosts Make it in the Emirates, the national industrial investment forum. ADGM and DIFC function as networking hubs for financial-services, fund and family-office investors, and the UK–GCC free trade agreement is expected to generate new bilateral trade and investment programmes. The sovereign investors and ADNOC run supplier and partnership programmes that offer a practical entry point for international firms.

Investor Profile Best Suited

The UAE suits a broad range of investors seeking a stable, well-regulated regional base with strong financial buffers: financial-services, asset-management and family-office investors using ADGM and DIFC; technology and AI investors drawn by the UAE–US AI Campus and the national digital agenda; logistics and trade investors serving the Gulf, South Asia and Africa through free zones and world-class ports; and manufacturing investors benefiting from industrial incentives and competitive energy. Energy and energy-logistics investors can participate in ADNOC's capacity expansion and the bypass pipeline programme, and long-horizon investors are well placed to benefit from the expected 2027 rebound, while building shipping and security scenarios into their planning.
