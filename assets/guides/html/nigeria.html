Investor Entry Routes

  • Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission (NIPC), the federal investment promotion agency, whose Act guarantees foreign investors the right to repatriate capital and profits
  • Certificate of Capital Importation (CCI), issued by an authorised dealer bank on behalf of the Central Bank within 24 hours of an inflow, the key document for repatriating dividends, profits and capital
  • Four Tax Reform Acts signed in June 2025, consolidating the tax system, creating the Nigeria Revenue Service and exempting small companies with turnover up to N100 million
  • Free trade zones licensed by the Nigeria Export Processing Zones Authority (NEPZA), offering tax and customs incentives for qualifying projects
  • Central Bank of Nigeria oversight of a unified, market-determined foreign-exchange market (NFEM), with the naira stable near N1,333 to the dollar in October 2026
  • Domestic capital market regulated by the Securities and Exchange Commission, which in 2026 hosted the Dangote refinery IPO seeking about N2.15 trillion
  • Upstream licensing through the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC) and state electricity markets enabled by the Electricity Act 2023

Market-Entry Observation

Nigeria in October 2026 is Africa's most populous market and one of its clearest reform stories. GDP growth rose to 4.43% in Q2 2026, inflation eased for a fourth consecutive month to 14.63% in September, and in September the Central Bank of Nigeria reset its policy rate 350 basis points lower to 23%. Gross external reserves stood at $55.07 billion in early October, which the central bank describes as the highest in more than 18 years, the current-account surplus reached $7.54 billion in Q2, and the naira has been stable in the unified foreign-exchange market. Investor confidence is visible: capital inflows rose 83.8% to $10.37 billion in Q1 2026, Nigeria exited the FATF grey list in October 2025, and S&P upgraded the sovereign to B in May 2026. Four Tax Reform Acts signed in 2025 and the Electricity Act 2023 are steadily modernising the investment framework.

Nigeria's main structural challenge is converting macroeconomic stabilisation into sustained, broad-based direct investment: portfolio flows made up 95% of Q1 2026 inflows, with FDI at 1.3%, and crude oil still accounts for close to half of goods exports. Progress on both fronts is tangible. Crude output averaged 1.72 million barrels a day in Q2 2026, up from 1.55 million in Q1. The Dangote refinery has lifted throughput to around 700,000 barrels a day, launched a domestic IPO and plans a $14.3 billion expansion to 1.4 million barrels a day by 2029, turning Nigeria into a regional refining hub. A goods trade surplus of about N12.6 trillion in Q2 2026 and strong reserves give the economy a solid buffer, while state electricity markets and free zones are opening opportunities for direct investors in energy, manufacturing and logistics.

Investor Risk Note

Nigeria's risk profile has improved markedly since 2023, reflecting a coherent reform programme of exchange-rate unification, monetary tightening followed by a rate reset, tax modernisation and power-sector decentralisation. Reserves above $55 billion, falling inflation, a current-account surplus, the exit from the FATF grey list and S&P's upgrade to B all point to a more predictable operating environment. The main considerations for investors are the cost of local capital, uneven power supply, regional security conditions and the run-up to the 2027 elections; each is manageable with sound structuring, careful site selection and appropriate financing. Investors who register capital correctly, hedge currency exposure and focus on established corridors are well placed to benefit from the scale of Nigeria's market as the reform dividend broadens.

Market Access

Nigeria is a member of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS), giving preferential access to West African regional markets, and is a party to the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA). It has been a WTO member since 1995. Crude oil accounted for 47.8% of goods exports in Q2 2026, with non-crude exports, including refined products from the Dangote refinery, now the larger share and reshaping regional fuel supply. Total goods trade reached N41.44 trillion in Q2 2026, up 5.6% year on year, with a surplus of about N12.6 trillion. The UK–Nigeria Enhanced Trade and Investment Partnership held its inaugural ministerial dialogue in March 2026, and the UK was the largest source of capital inflows in Q1 2026, while the Lagos ports, led by the deep-water Lekki port, give Nigeria a growing role as a regional logistics gateway.

Current International Business Channels

The UK–Nigeria Enhanced Trade and Investment Partnership provides a structured government-to-government channel, with its inaugural ministerial dialogue held in March 2026, and the Nigerian-British Chamber of Commerce and the American Chamber of Commerce in Nigeria are the leading bilateral business bodies. The Nigerian Economic Summit Group convenes the annual Nigerian Economic Summit, the main public-private policy forum. NIPC runs investor outreach and aftercare, and state governments, particularly Lagos, increasingly hold their own investment events. The World Bank, the International Finance Corporation and the African Development Bank are active in power, infrastructure and private-sector development, and large international energy companies and banks with long-standing Nigerian operations are useful sources of practical intelligence.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Nigeria suits investors seeking scale and long-term growth in Africa's largest consumer market: consumer-goods, retail and fintech investors drawn by a young, urbanising population; energy investors in midstream gas, refining and petrochemicals building on the Dangote refinery and rising upstream output; power investors able to work with the new state electricity markets; and manufacturing and logistics investors using free zones and the Lekki port corridor to serve Nigeria and West Africa. Portfolio and capital-markets investors benefit from FX reform, record reserves and lower policy rates, while patient, well-structured direct investors are best placed to capture the broader reform dividend.
