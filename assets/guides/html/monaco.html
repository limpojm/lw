The economy today

Monaco in November 2026 offers investors a stable constitutional monarchy, strong public finances and a newly strengthened financial reputation. The FATF removed Monaco from its grey list on 31 October 2026, recognising more than two years of reform, including a presumed money-laundering offence, a better-resourced financial regulator and a rise in convictions from 3 in 2021 to 28 in 2025. GDP passed €10 billion for the first time in 2024, reaching €10.24 billion after real growth of 8.8%, led by scientific, technical and support services (€2.4 billion) and finance and insurance (€1.8 billion). Private-sector employment reached a first-quarter record of 63,018 jobs in March 2026, up 2.0% year-on-year, and bank assets under management rose 5.6% to €179.6 billion. The State recorded a €193 million surplus in 2024, and the Constitutional Reserve Fund stood at €7.3 billion at the end of 2024.

Monaco's main structural challenge is sustaining growth on two square kilometres while property and construction activity cycle. Business turnover fell 20.3% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026, as only 2 new apartments were sold against 42 a year earlier after the Mareterra sales peak, and the 2026 budget projects a modest surplus of €8.8 million, down from €86 million in the amended 2025 budget. The fundamentals remain strong: hospitality, retail and administrative services all grew in early 2026, the Reserve Fund gives exceptional fiscal depth, and the Government is investing in housing, the new hospital and cross-border mobility. Exit from the FATF grey list should also ease banking relationships and support removal from the EU's high-risk list, creating openings for wealth management, family offices and high-value services.

How do I get in?

Company formation and business authorisation through the Government's MonEntreprise services, with the Monaco Economic Board supporting foreign investors with introductions and market information

A 25% corporate income tax applying only to firms earning more than 25% of their turnover outside Monaco, with a two-year exemption for genuinely new businesses, then a phased rise over four years

No personal income tax or wealth tax for residents, a long-standing draw for entrepreneurs, executives and family offices

The euro as legal tender under a monetary agreement with the EU, with no currency risk against the euro; ECB policy rates apply, with the deposit rate at 2.50% after the 29 October 2026 meeting

Inclusion in the EU customs territory, the only non-EU country to have this status, and open access to the Schengen area through France

Banking and asset management licences in a sector holding €179.6 billion of assets under management, plus Monegasque investment funds with €4.96 billion of net assets

Trade and market access

Monaco is not an EU member, but it is the only non-EU country integrated into the EU customs territory, so goods move freely with France and the wider EU, and its open border with France gives direct access to the Schengen area. The euro is legal tender under a monetary agreement with the EU, removing currency costs for trade and investment across the euro area. Talks on an EU association agreement were suspended in 2023 over Monaco's red lines on residence, employment and housing, so financial and other services are provided to the EU through bilateral arrangements with France and through EU-based entities. Monaco's workforce and supply chains are closely tied to the Alpes-Maritimes and Liguria, with Nice airport and rail links connecting it to European and global markets. Monaco held the Presidency of the Council of Europe's Committee of Ministers from 15 May 2026, raising its diplomatic profile.

The overall risk picture

Monaco's risk profile is that of a politically stable principality with strong public finances, the euro and close integration with France and the EU customs territory. Prince Albert II is head of state, the Government is led by Minister of State Christophe Mirmand, and the 24-member National Council, presided over by Thomas Brezzo, votes the budget; relations between the two were constructive in 2025–26. The most important development is the FATF's removal of Monaco from its grey list on 31 October 2026. Investors should note that Monaco was on the EU's high-risk third-country list in early 2026, so EU banks and firms must still apply enhanced due diligence until the Commission updates the list; specialist compliance advice remains essential. The near-term economic issue is a slowdown in property and construction activity, which is cushioned by the €7.3 billion Constitutional Reserve Fund and continued growth in tourism, services and employment.

Business networks

The Monaco Economic Board is the main business network, organising missions, events and introductions for foreign investors and connecting them with Monegasque companies, professional firms and banks. The Government's MonEntreprise portal guides company formation, authorisations and tax registration, and the Department of Finance and Economy is the counterpart for licensing questions. Monaco's calendar of international sporting, yachting and business events provides regular networking opportunities, and embassies and consulates accredited to Monaco, many of them based in Paris, are useful sources of introductions. Public projects such as the new hospital and State housing are funded from the budget, so procurement is the main route into infrastructure.

Who this market suits

Monaco suits investors seeking a stable, euro-based headquarters and wealth hub with a light tax regime: private banks, asset managers and family offices, now benefiting from the FATF exit; hospitality, luxury retail and events businesses in a market that welcomed more hotel guests in early 2026; construction, engineering and healthcare suppliers targeting the new hospital, housing and mobility projects; and high-value professional, technical and yachting services drawing on the cross-border talent pool. Financial-sector investors should plan for rigorous compliance and track the EU high-risk list update.
