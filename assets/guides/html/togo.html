The economy today

Togo in October 2026 is one of West Africa's steadiest growth stories, built on its role as the region's leading logistics hub. GDP grew about 6% in 2025, led by services, and the IMF projects around 5% in 2026, with inflation of just 0.4% in 2025. The fiscal deficit narrowed to 3.2% of GDP in 2025, on track for the 3% target in 2027, and the IMF completed the combined third and fourth reviews of Togo's Extended Credit Facility in June 2026, bringing disbursements to about $300 million. S&P upgraded Togo to B+ in 2025 on growth and fiscal reform. The FDI stock rose 27.3% in 2025 to $2.06 billion, with inflows of $221 million. The Port of Lomé handled about 2.4 million TEU in 2025, the Adétikopé Industrial Platform has drawn CFA350 billion of investment, and electricity access has risen from 50% in 2020 to 75% in 2025.

Togo's main structural consideration is its position next to the central Sahel. Armed groups from Burkina Faso have been active in the far-northern Savanes region, where a state of security emergency remains in place. Togo has responded with a sustained security deployment, and recorded incidents fell from 40 in the first half of 2025 to 15 in the first half of 2026. Economic activity is concentrated in the south, around Lomé and its port, far from the affected areas. The same Sahel link is also Togo's commercial strength: Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger account for 92% of the port's transit volume, and Togo's steady relations with these landlocked neighbours have made Lomé their preferred gateway to the sea. Buffers are sound, with WAEMU reserves covering about seven months of imports, an IMF programme in place and growing concessional financing.

How do I get in?

API-ZF (Agency for the Promotion of Investment and the Free Zone), whose powers were expanded in August 2026 to cover development and management of industrial zones, provides one-stop, digital facilitation for investors

2019 Investment Code guaranteeing equal treatment of foreign and Togolese companies, free management and movement of capital, and protection of private property, with no general restrictions on foreign ownership

Free Zone regime offering corporate tax and customs exemptions, 0% dividend tax in the early years and preferential utility and port tariffs for firms exporting at least 70% of output

Adétikopé Industrial Platform (PIA), offering ready-built industrial infrastructure close to the Port of Lomé for agro-processing and textile manufacturing

Investment Approval Committee, which had validated 37 projects worth CFA237 billion by end-2024 under the Investment Code's incentive schemes

CFA franc (XOF), pegged to the euro and managed by the BCEAO, providing exchange-rate stability; capital transfers by foreign investors are free under the Investment Code

Private-sector partnership under the Vision Togo 2040 roadmap, with the government inviting private capital into agro-processing, logistics, transport and energy

Trade and market access

Togo is a member of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS) and the West African Economic and Monetary Union (WAEMU) and is a party to the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) and a member of the WTO. Its deep-water Port of Lomé is the largest container port in sub-Saharan Africa and a leading transshipment hub for the Gulf of Guinea. Road corridors link Lomé to Ouagadougou, Niamey and Bamako, serving the landlocked Sahel. Exports grew 23.4% year on year in Q4 2025, led by phosphates, motorcycles, refined petroleum products, plastics, soy and palm oil. The main markets are India (18.6%), Côte d'Ivoire, Burkina Faso, Benin, Ghana, France and the United States.

The overall risk picture

Togo's risk profile is that of a well-managed, logistics-led economy with consistent growth of around 6%, low inflation and a credible reform programme. Fiscal consolidation is on track under the IMF's Extended Credit Facility, S&P upgraded the sovereign to B+ in 2025, and membership of the WAEMU monetary union gives the CFA franc a stable euro peg backed by pooled reserves. The main considerations are security in the far-northern Savanes region, where incident numbers fell markedly in 2026 and which lies far from the southern economic core, and the political transition to a parliamentary system in 2025, which brought protests but has seen continuity in economic policy. Investors should include standard security and political due diligence in their planning, and can take confidence from the depth of Togo's port, industrial-zone and financial-sector infrastructure and its strong record with multilateral partners.

Seat of executive power under the 2025 constitution; leads the Vision Togo 2040 strategy and private-sector partnership agenda

Primary reference point for Togo's strategic direction and flagship projects

API-ZF (Investment Promotion and Free Zone Agency)

National investment promotion body, now also responsible for developing and managing industrial zones

Standard first point of contact for market entry, approvals and free zone incentives

Key policy interlocutor for large or strategic investments

Relevant for currency, banking and capital-transfer matters

Oversee West Africa's leading container port and its transshipment and Sahel transit business

Regional development banks and a pan-African bank headquartered in Lomé, financing infrastructure and private projects

Useful co-financing and banking partners for regional transactions

Business networks

The Association of Large Enterprises of Togo (AGET) is the principal voice of the private sector and a partner to government on the Vision 2040 roadmap, alongside the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Togo and employers' associations. API-ZF provides the main investor-facing channel for approvals and free zone incentives. The World Bank and IFC maintain active country programmes, and the IMF's Extended Credit Facility anchors the reform agenda. Lomé's concentration of regional institutions, including BOAD, EBID and Ecobank, makes it a natural networking and deal-making hub for West African finance. French support instruments, including AFD and Bpifrance, are also active in the market.

Who this market suits

Togo suits investors seeking a stable, well-connected platform for West Africa and the Sahel: logistics, shipping and warehousing investors building on the Port of Lomé's regional hub role; manufacturers and agro-processors using the Adétikopé Industrial Platform and free zone incentives for export-oriented textiles, soy, shea and cashew; renewable energy developers and IPPs supporting the drive to universal electricity access by 2030; and financial-services investors drawn by Lomé's concentration of regional banks and development finance institutions. Mining investors can look at the phosphate and mineral-processing value chain, and DFI-backed infrastructure investors will find a government actively seeking private capital under Vision Togo 2040.

    Engage API-ZF and confirm incentive termsOpen dialogue with the investment promotion and free zone agency to confirm eligibility under the Investment Code or Free Zone regime and to use its one-stop facilitation.
  2. 2
    Assess sites at the Adétikopé Industrial PlatformEvaluate ready-built industrial capacity near the Port of Lomé for agro-processing, textiles and other export-oriented manufacturing.
  3. 3
    Map port and corridor logisticsWork with port authorities and operators to build supply chains around Lomé's transshipment hub and the road corridors to Burkina Faso, Niger and Mali.
  4. 4
    Line up regional co-financingApproach Lomé-based development banks such as BOAD and EBID, alongside the World Bank and IFC, to co-finance projects and share risk.
  5. 5
    Build security and governance due diligence into planningCommission location-specific security advice for any northern activities and monitor political and regulatory developments as part of routine risk management.
