The economy today

Slovakia offers investors a euro-area manufacturing base at the heart of Central Europe, with deep automotive expertise and a skilled industrial workforce. GDP grew 0.8% in 2025 and 0.8% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, when exports rose 3.0% and the trade surplus exceeded €0.5 billion, its highest since early 2024. The investment pipeline is substantial: SARIO concluded 33 projects worth about €439 million in 2025, with 69 more worth over €4.9 billion in progress, and Volvo Cars' Košice plant, designed for 250,000 cars a year, is due to reach large-scale production in early 2027. In January 2026 Slovakia and the United States signed an intergovernmental agreement for a new 1,200 MWe nuclear unit at Bohunice, backed by export-credit cooperation between the two countries' Eximbanks.

Slovakia's main structural challenge is restoring its public finances while keeping growth and investment on track. The general government deficit was 4.5% of GDP in 2025, is expected at 4.37% in 2026, and the draft 2027 budget targets 4.94%, while public debt rose to 61.4% of GDP in 2025. Consumer price inflation was 3.1% in September 2026, and the ECB raised its deposit rate to 2.50% in September 2026, holding it there on 29 October, as energy prices lifted euro-area inflation. The government has introduced consolidation measures, including the 24% corporate tax band and a 23% VAT rate, and Moody's affirmed Slovakia's A3 rating with a stable outlook in June 2026. Euro membership, EU funding and a strong export sector provide resilience, and new automotive and energy projects create sustained opportunities for suppliers and investors.

How do I get in?

SARIO, the Slovak Investment and Trade Development Agency, provides free investor facilitation, site selection and aftercare; it concluded 33 projects worth about €439 million in 2025 and has a pipeline of 69 projects worth over €4.9 billion

State investment aid under EU state-aid rules, including grants and tax relief for projects such as manufacturing, technology centres and shared-service centres, arranged through the Ministry of Economy and SARIO

Corporate income tax in three tiers: 10% for revenue up to €100,000, 21% up to €5 million and 24% above €5 million; standard VAT is 23%

Euro-area membership since 2009: the euro is the national currency, with free movement of capital and profit repatriation under EU rules

EU single-market and Schengen membership, giving tariff-free access to EU markets and passport-free movement across most of Europe

Government-backed industrial parks and strategic investment sites, including the Volvo site near Košice, offering serviced land for large manufacturing projects

EU co-financing through the Recovery and Resilience Plan and cohesion programmes for transport, energy, digital and innovation projects

Trade and market access

As an EU member, Slovakia gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market of some 450 million people and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Euro-area membership since 2009 removes exchange-rate costs for trade with its main partners, and Schengen membership allows passport-free movement to neighbouring Czechia, Austria, Hungary and Poland. Germany and its Central European neighbours are its main markets, and Slovakia's motorway and rail links, the Danube port of Bratislava and the broad-gauge rail terminal near Košice connect it with Western Europe and markets to the east. Exports rose 3.0% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026.

The overall risk picture

Slovakia's risk profile is that of an established EU, euro-area and NATO member with an open, export-led economy and an A3 credit rating affirmed by Moody's in June 2026. The main issues for investors are fiscal: the deficit remains above 4% of GDP and debt passed 60% of GDP in 2025, so further tax and spending measures are likely. Relations with EU institutions also merit monitoring after the European Parliament's May 2026 resolution on the rule of law, although the government reports that EU and recovery-plan funds continue to flow. Slovakia is not on the FATF list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring. These factors are manageable within the EU legal framework, and the euro removes currency risk for investors from the euro area.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the Government (Prime Minister Robert Fico)

Sets economic, fiscal and energy policy, and led the January 2026 nuclear agreement with the United States

Primary reference point for strategic projects and government priorities

SARIO (Slovak Investment and Trade Development Agency)

National investment and export promotion agency under the Ministry of Economy, with 69 projects worth over €4.9 billion in its pipeline

Standard first point of contact for market entry, sites and incentives

National Bank of Slovakia (NBS)

Member of the Eurosystem; its governor sits on the ECB Governing Council and it supervises banks and financial markets

Relevant for banking, payments and financial-sector licensing

Ministry of Finance (Minister Ladislav Kamenický)

Leads the budget, tax policy and fiscal consolidation

Key counterpart on tax and public-finance matters

Ministry of Economy

Oversees industrial policy, state investment aid, energy policy and SARIO

Relevant for incentives, energy and manufacturing investment

Slovenské elektrárne and the state nuclear programme

Slovenské elektrárne operates the Bohunice units; the new unit there is planned as a fully state-owned project

Counterpart for suppliers and partners in the nuclear and power sector

Eximbanka SR

State export-credit agency that signed a nuclear-financing cooperation agreement with the US Export-Import Bank in January 2026

Useful for export finance and project co-financing

Business networks

The American Chamber of Commerce in Slovakia, the British Chamber of Commerce in Slovakia, the Slovak-German Chamber of Industry and Commerce and the Slovak Chamber of Commerce and Industry are the main business associations, with sector committees and regular dialogue with government. SARIO runs investor events and business missions, including the Slovak Industry Vision Day forum, and supports supplier matchmaking with large investors such as Volvo. The Automotive Industry Association of the Slovak Republic represents the country's leading sector. The European Investment Bank, the EBRD and EU programmes managed by national ministries offer co-financing for qualifying projects, and embassies of major EU partners and the United States maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Slovakia suits investors seeking an industrial base inside the euro area: automotive and electric-vehicle suppliers serving Volvo in Košice and established carmakers in the west; engineering and energy companies targeting the new Bohunice nuclear unit and grid investment; technology and shared-service centres drawing on Bratislava and Košice talent; and logistics investors using Slovakia's position between Western Europe, Central Europe and Ukraine. Investors should plan for further fiscal measures and take tax advice on the tiered corporate tax regime.
