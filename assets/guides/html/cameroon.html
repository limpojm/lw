The economy today

Cameroon in October 2026 offers the largest and most diversified economy in Central Africa, with steady growth and low inflation. GDP grew 3.5% in 2025, matching 2024, with services remaining strong, and the IMF expects growth to rise towards 4.6% by 2031 as mining diversification develops. Average inflation eased to 2.6% in the year to August 2026, allowing the BEAC to cut its policy rates in June 2026 and hold its main rate at 4.5% in September. Cameroon became Central Africa's largest FDI recipient in 2025, attracting $599 million. A new investment incentive regime under Ordinance No. 2025/002 has already produced 36 investment conventions worth about CFA7 trillion in projected investment, while Kribi port's container throughput rose 82% to 750,000 TEUs in the second terminal's first year.

Cameroon's main structural task is to replace declining oil output with new sources of growth and revenue: oil GDP fell 7.2% in 2025, oil still accounts for nearly half of exports, and the IMF rates the country at high risk of debt distress even though public debt is a moderate 40.3% of GDP and projected to decline. The policy response is well defined. Three iron ore projects worth CFA1,748 billion are planned for 2026–2030, the 420 MW Nachtigal hydropower plant has added clean baseload power, and the Kribi Port Industrial Zone, backed by the African Development Bank, the EU's Global Gateway and the IFC, targets agro-industry, metallurgy and manufacturing. The government is prioritising concessional financing and fuel-subsidy reform, and maintains close engagement with the IMF. For investors, the transition creates openings in mining services, power transmission, logistics and agro-processing.

How do I get in?

Investment Promotion Agency (API), the national one-stop shop, which aims to approve applications within 10 days (down from about 25) and is digitalising its services

Ordinance No. 2025/002 of 18 July 2025, replacing the 2013 investment law with graduated tax credits, accelerated depreciation, customs facilities and targeted incentives for economic-zone and PPP projects; 36 investment conventions were signed in its first months

Economic zones, including the port zones of Douala and Kribi and the new Kribi Port Industrial Zone, offering tax and customs exemptions for qualifying projects

CFA franc issued by the BEAC and pegged to the euro at 655.957; capital and profits may be repatriated under CEMAC exchange rules, with BEAC authorisation required for foreign-exchange transactions above CFA50 million

OHADA harmonised business law with recourse to the Common Court of Justice and Arbitration (CCJA), plus ICSID and New York Convention membership; up to 100% foreign ownership permitted outside defence and national security

Sector entry through partnership with the National Hydrocarbons Corporation (SNH) in oil and gas, mining permits for minerals, and public-private partnerships in power and infrastructure, following the Nachtigal model

Trade and market access

Cameroon is the largest economy in the Economic and Monetary Community of Central Africa (CEMAC), a six-country monetary union of more than 50 million consumers sharing the CFA franc, and its ports at Douala and Kribi are the natural gateway for landlocked Chad and the Central African Republic. It is a WTO member and was chosen to host the WTO Ministerial Conference in Yaoundé in March 2026, is a signatory of the African Continental Free Trade Area, and is the only Central African state to have ratified an Economic Partnership Agreement with the EU, with a matching UK–Cameroon EPA in force since 1 January 2021. Oil (48.6%), cocoa (27.1%) and timber (10.2%) led exports in 2024, with the Netherlands, China, India, Italy and France the main markets. Kribi's transshipment role, serving ports from Nigeria to Angola, and the Chad–Cameroon pipeline extend Cameroon's reach across the Gulf of Guinea.

The overall risk picture

Cameroon's risk profile is that of a diversified, stable-currency economy managing a gradual transition away from oil. The euro peg, inflation of 2.6% and moderate debt of around 40% of GDP give a predictable macroeconomic base, and continued IMF engagement supports fiscal discipline. The main considerations for investors are practical: CEMAC exchange procedures that lengthen transfer times, the IMF's high debt-distress rating and its implications for public-sector payment terms, FATF increased monitoring, and security in parts of the Northwest and Far North, all of which can be managed through treasury planning, DFI partnership, enhanced due diligence and careful site selection in the stable economic core. Political succession is a genuine question given the President's age and extended absence from public view in 2026, and investors should include transition scenarios in their planning. The upside is substantial: Kribi's rapid growth, a new investment incentive regime and a multi-billion-dollar mining and power pipeline position Cameroon as Central Africa's leading gateway.

Business networks

GECAM, Cameroon's leading employers' association, is the main domestic business network and hosts an arbitration centre in Douala. The Investment Promotion Agency runs investor awareness workshops on the new incentive regime and supports sector outreach. Development finance institutions are central to the deal environment: the IFC, MIGA, Africa50 and EDF partnered on Nachtigal, while the African Development Bank, the EU's Global Gateway programme and the IFC are expected to finance the first phase of the Kribi Port Industrial Zone. Port operators and international shipping and logistics groups at Kribi and Douala, together with major oil, mining and agribusiness companies, are valuable sources of practical market intelligence.

Who this market suits

Cameroon suits investors seeking a long-term position in Central Africa's largest and most diversified economy: logistics, port and industrial investors building on Kribi's growth and the new industrial zone; agro-processing and timber investors adding value to cocoa, coffee and wood exports; mining-services, rail and power investors supporting the iron ore pipeline; and energy investors pursuing independent power and transmission projects on the Nachtigal model. Infrastructure funds and investors working alongside development finance institutions are particularly well placed, and all investors should plan for CEMAC exchange procedures and apply robust due diligence.
