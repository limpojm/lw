Investor Entry Routes

  • InvestTaiwan Center (Ministry of Economic Affairs Department of Investment Promotion), the lead promotion agency offering one-stop facilitation, incentive guidance and site support for foreign investors
  • Statute for Investment by Foreign Nationals, with approval by the MOEA Investment Review Department; most sectors are open, with a short negative list covering areas such as utilities, telecommunications, media and air and sea transport
  • Statute for Industrial Innovation incentives, including a 15% R&D tax credit and a 25% credit for qualifying semiconductor and key-technology investment
  • Science parks administered by the National Science and Technology Council, home to 1,171 companies, alongside seven free trade and free port zones
  • Managed-float New Taiwan dollar with no restrictions on repatriating earnings, dividends or capital from approved investment; advance reporting is required and very large conversions may be phased over several days
  • Kaohsiung Asset Management Zone, supervised by the Financial Supervisory Commission, for wealth management, family offices and cross-border financial services
  • Talent routes including the Employment Gold Card and an entrepreneur visa with a NT$2 million funding threshold

Market-Entry Observation

Taiwan in October 2026 is one of the world's fastest-growing advanced economies, powered by global demand for AI hardware. GDP grew 8.63% in 2025, the strongest in 15 years, and accelerated to 14.15% in the first half of 2026; the central bank now forecasts 11.48% growth for 2026, and the IMF projects Taiwan as the only advanced economy with double-digit growth this year. Exports reached a record $640.75 billion in 2025 and $661.55 billion in January–September 2026, up 46.2%, with September setting a monthly record of $87.22 billion. Approved foreign direct investment rose 44.98% to $11.39 billion in 2025 and to $13.92 billion in January–July 2026, up 78.13%, led by Micron ($7.45 billion) and the Hai Long offshore wind project. Foreign exchange reserves of $600.75 billion, an IMF-projected current account surplus of 22% of GDP and a stable 2% policy rate underpin the outlook.

Taiwan's main structural challenge is security of supply in a tense cross-Strait environment. The island imported 95% of its energy in 2025, LNG provided nearly 48% of power generation, and around one-third of LNG came from Qatar, a supply disrupted by the 2026 Middle East conflict. Beijing's "Justice Mission 2025" exercises in December 2025 rehearsed a blockade of Keelung and Kaohsiung and affected air routes for two days, although commercial activity continued. Taiwan has responded pragmatically: it secured replacement LNG cargoes from Australia and the United States, ruled out power rationing, and is reviewing nuclear options, with the regulator completing technical reviews for restarting the Maanshan plant in September 2026. Offshore wind, grid and storage investment, together with deepening supply-chain ties with the United States, Japan and Europe, offer investors both resilience and growth opportunities.

Investor Risk Note

Taiwan's risk profile combines exceptional economic strength with a geopolitical environment that investors should manage deliberately rather than avoid. Growth, exports and approved FDI are at or near record levels, foreign exchange reserves exceed $600 billion, the banking system is well capitalised, and earnings and capital from approved investments can be repatriated freely. The principal considerations are cross-Strait tensions, highlighted by the December 2025 exercises, and dependence on imported energy, which the 2026 Middle East conflict has tested; in both cases operations continued and the authorities secured alternative supplies. Taiwan is not subject to international sanctions, but investors should note that its distinct international status places it outside ICSID and the New York Convention, and that investors with PRC ownership face a separate, stricter approval track. With sound legal advice, business-continuity planning and appropriate insurance, investors can access one of the world's most dynamic technology economies.

Market Access

Taiwan has been a member of the WTO since 2002 as a separate customs territory, and has economic cooperation agreements with New Zealand and Singapore, investment agreements with Canada (2023) and Thailand (2024), and a first agreement under the US–Taiwan Initiative on 21st-Century Trade in force since December 2024. The January 2026 US–Taiwan trade and investment arrangement set a 15% US tariff ceiling, with Taiwanese firms committing $250 billion of US investment and $250 billion in credit guarantees. Exports reached a record $640.75 billion in 2025, with the United States now Taiwan's largest trade-surplus partner and the share of exports going to mainland China and Hong Kong falling to 23% from 42.3% in 2021, while exports to ASEAN also hit a record. Kaohsiung and Keelung ports and Taoyuan International Airport connect Taiwan to global markets, and the Taiwan Strait is one of the world's busiest shipping lanes.

Current International Business Channels

The American Chamber of Commerce in Taiwan is the most active foreign business association, meeting regularly with government agencies and publishing policy recommendations. The Taiwan External Trade Development Council (TAITRA) organises Computex, held in Taipei in June 2026 with keynotes from global technology leaders, and is a valuable channel for partner matching. The National Development Council hosts public consultation on new regulations, and the state-backed venture platform Taiwania Capital co-invests in technology funds. The US International Development Finance Corporation and Taiwan's International Cooperation and Development Fund have a cooperation memorandum for third-country projects, and the international owners of projects such as Hai Long, including Mitsui, Northland Power and Gentari, are useful sources of practical intelligence on energy investment.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Taiwan best suits investors seeking exposure to the global AI and semiconductor supply chain: equipment, materials, chemicals and services suppliers to TSMC and other chipmakers expanding fabs across the island's science parks; memory and advanced-packaging manufacturers; and technology investors drawn by record park revenues and incentives for key technologies. Energy-transition investors will find a growing market in offshore wind, storage and grid infrastructure, while asset managers and private banks can use the Kaohsiung Asset Management Zone. Investors should be comfortable managing cross-Strait and energy-supply scenarios through continuity planning and insurance.
