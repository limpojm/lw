The economy today

Andorra in November 2026 combines strong growth, a budget surplus and low debt with one of the most competitive tax regimes in Europe. Real GDP grew 3.9% in 2025, far ahead of Spain, France and the EU, and 3.3% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026, led by finance, professional services and construction. Nominal GDP reached about €4 billion in 2025, and the government recorded a surplus of €88.3 million against a planned deficit. The IMF puts public debt at 30.3% of GDP and describes the banking sector as strong. Rating agencies have turned positive: S&P moved its A- outlook to positive in May 2026 and Moody's affirmed its Baa1 rating with a positive outlook in July 2026. The signature of the EU Association Agreement on 19 October 2026, after negotiations that began in 2014, opens the way to full access to the EU single market.

Andorra's main structural challenge is diversifying a small, tourism-led economy with limited land and housing. The country received 9.2 million visitors in 2025, inflation reached 4.8% in August 2026 on higher fuel prices, and housing affordability is residents' leading concern, with the IMF linking it to labour shortages. The IMF expects growth to ease to 2.1% in 2026. Strengths provide a firm base for change: the euro, surpluses and liquid public assets give fiscal room, the government is building more than 600 affordable rental homes by 2027, and the Association Agreement, which a government-cited study expects to raise GDP by at least 5% by 2050, should open new markets and attract skilled people. The National Plan for Innovation and Diversification and new grants for digital, health, sport and audiovisual firms create openings for investors in higher-value activities.

How do I get in?

Andorra Business, the investment promotion agency chaired by the Minister of Economy, offers guidance on company set-up, residency and incentives, and runs a €2 million 2026 innovation grant programme open to foreign firms

Foreign investment authorisation from the Government: acquisitions above 10% of a company and all real-estate investments need prior approval, with a statutory decision period of two months

A 10% corporate income tax, a maximum 10% personal income tax and a 4.5% general indirect tax (IGI), with an 80% reduction for certain international intangible, trading and intra-group financing activities

Euro as legal tender under the 2011 Monetary Agreement with the EU, with no currency risk against the euro and no dividend withholding tax in general

Residence routes for entrepreneurs, self-employed professionals and passive investors, tightened by Law 2/2026 (minimum passive-residence investment raised to €1 million)

Customs union with the EU for manufactured goods since 1991, with single-market access to follow under the Association Agreement signed on 19 October 2026

Trade and market access

Andorra is not a member of the EU or Schengen, but it has had a customs union with the EU for manufactured goods since 1991 and uses the euro, and the EU accounts for more than 80% of its imports. The Association Agreement signed on 19 October 2026 will, once ratified, give Andorra access to the EU single market for goods, services, capital and people, with transition periods of 15 years for financial services, seven years for telecommunications and 30 years for tobacco, while Andorra keeps its own tax system and does not join the EU. Andorra is linked by road to France and Spain, its two dominant markets, with the nearest airports and rail connections across the border in both countries. From the third quarter of 2026 the European Central Bank's EUREP facility extends euro liquidity lines to Andorran banks, strengthening the financial link with the euro area.

The overall risk picture

Andorra's risk profile is that of a stable, prosperous microstate with a long record of political continuity, a sovereign co-principality and strong public finances. It uses the euro under its 2011 Monetary Agreement with the EU, ran a budget surplus in 2025 and has public debt of about 30% of GDP; S&P (A-) and Moody's (Baa1) both have positive outlooks. Andorra ended banking secrecy a decade ago and is not on the FATF grey list; MONEYVAL launched its sixth-round evaluation of the country in April 2026, and the IMF has encouraged continued risk-based supervision. The main near-term issues are domestic: inflation of 4.8% in August 2026, housing pressures and the ratification of the EU Association Agreement, which faces a referendum and elections due in spring 2027. Investors should note that foreign investment and property purchases require prior approval and that residency rules were tightened in 2026; with early engagement and local advice, these are manageable parts of a predictable process.

Business networks

The Chamber of Commerce, Industry and Services of Andorra (CCIS) is the main business association and publishes regular economic reports, while Andorran Banking represents the banking sector and maintains dialogue with regulators. Andorra Business organises investor events and promotes the country abroad, and works with Andorra Recerca i Innovació on innovation projects. Business relations are closely tied to France and Spain, whose embassies, chambers and professional networks are useful sources of introductions, and the co-princes, the President of France and the Bishop of Urgell, give the country distinctive links with both neighbours. Andorra is the IMF's 190th member, and IMF and rating-agency reviews provide regular independent assessment of the economy.

Who this market suits

Andorra suits investors seeking a stable, low-tax euro base with close links to France and Spain: wealth management, banking and professional-services firms drawing on a well-capitalised financial sector; digital, biotechnology, sports-science and audiovisual companies eligible for innovation grants and residency exemptions; tourism, hospitality and wellness investors serving 9.2 million annual visitors; and developers of rental and affordable housing and sustainable construction. Investors should be ready for prior-authorisation procedures and substance requirements, and should follow the ratification of the EU Association Agreement closely.
