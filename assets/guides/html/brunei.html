The economy today

Brunei in November 2026 offers a high-income, politically stable and financially sound base on the edge of the South China Sea, with GDP per capita of around $34,700 and an economy returning to growth. GDP rose 0.7% in 2025, led by a 3.1% increase in oil and gas output, and accelerated to 4.8% year on year in the fourth quarter; growth of 0.3% in Q1 2026 and a flat Q2, when services grew 1.6% and investment rose sharply, set the stage for 2026 growth that the government estimates at 1.5–2.4% and ADB at 1.6%. Inflation is low at -0.1% (September 2026), the 2025 merchandise trade surplus was B$5.06 billion, and inward FDI stock stood at $7.1 billion in 2025. Momentum is strongest downstream: Phase II of the Hengyi complex at Pulau Muara Besar was launched in January 2026 and awarded major process-technology contracts in October 2026, while the Muara Port expansion will more than double container capacity by end-2027.

Brunei's main structural challenge is its reliance on hydrocarbons, which account for about half of GDP and 74.9% of export value, with a fiscal deficit forecast at B$3.1 billion for FY2025/26. The government is responding with expenditure control in a B$6.3 billion 2026/27 budget, efforts to raise non-oil revenue and a clear diversification agenda under Wawasan Brunei 2035, which prioritises downstream oil and gas, food, tourism, ICT and services. Chemicals already contribute 22.2% of exports thanks to downstream investment, and the private sector's share of the workforce has risen from 53% to 70% over the past decade. For investors, this creates a government actively seeking partners in petrochemicals, aquaculture and halal food, logistics and digital services, supported by generous incentives, a stable currency and ASEAN-wide market access.

Where are the opportunities?

Bandar Seri Begawan (Brunei-Muara District)

Capital and seat of government, the central bank and the main financial institutions; base for most professional and business services

Financial services, Islamic finance, professional services, tourism

Pulau Muara Besar

Site of the Hengyi refinery and petrochemical complex, Brunei's largest foreign direct investment; Phase II will lift crude capacity to about 20 million tonnes a year, with mechanical completion targeted for end-2028

Refining, petrochemicals, downstream supply chains

Muara Port

Main container gateway, run since 2017 by a Brunei–Guangxi Beibu Gulf joint venture; an expansion due by end-2027 will more than double capacity from about 220,000 to 500,000 TEUs

Historic heart of onshore and offshore oil and gas production and associated downstream industry, including methanol and urea

How do I get in?

Brunei Economic Development Board (BEDB), under the Ministry of Finance and Economy, is the frontline agency for foreign investors and secures approval from the Foreign Direct Investment and Downstream Industry Committee

Darussalam Enterprise (DARe) provides business support, manages 26 industrial parks and helps investors obtain permits and licences

Investment Incentives Order 2001 offering pioneer-status tax relief of up to 11 years (up to 8 years for non-pioneer enterprises) and relief on qualifying agricultural, forestry and fishery export profits

Competitive tax regime: corporate income tax of 18.5% applied equally to local and foreign companies, with no personal income, sales or export taxes; 100% foreign ownership of incorporated companies is permitted, subject to resident-director rules

Brunei dollar pegged at par to the Singapore dollar, with both currencies accepted in each country; Brunei has accepted IMF Article VIII obligations, with no restrictions on current international payments and transfers

Terunjing Free Trade Zone and DARe industrial parks for trade, logistics and processing investment

Investor protection through the Brunei Darussalam Arbitration Centre, a Commercial Court, the International Arbitration Order 2010, and membership of ICSID and the New York Convention

Trade and market access

Brunei is a member of ASEAN, with preferential access across the region, and is party to the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), which it ratified in 2021, and the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), in force for Brunei since July 2023, which links it with partners including Japan, Australia and Canada. It is also a WTO member and a partner in the Brunei–Indonesia–Malaysia–Philippines East ASEAN Growth Area (BIMP-EAGA), whose 2026 meetings Brunei is hosting. Exports are dominated by mineral fuels (74.9% in 2025) and chemicals (22.2%), with Australia, China and Singapore the largest destinations, and China–Brunei trade reached $2.8 billion in 2024. A direct shipping route between Muara and Qinzhou in China has cut transit times to about a week and reduced shipping costs by around 30%, strengthening Brunei's role as a logistics link between ASEAN and China.

The overall risk picture

Brunei's risk profile is that of a small, wealthy and highly stable economy whose near-term performance is linked to the hydrocarbon cycle. Political continuity, a currency pegged at par to the Singapore dollar, low inflation, a well-capitalised and highly liquid banking sector and a B$5.06 billion trade surplus in 2025 provide a predictable operating environment, while the commercial legal system, arbitration centre and accession to ICSID and the New York Convention protect investors. The principal considerations are dependence on oil and gas, the current fiscal deficit, a small domestic market and regulatory features such as land rules, labour quotas and local-content requirements, all of which can be managed through industrial-park locations, early engagement with BEDB and local partners. Investors should also take legal advice on the Sharia Penal Code as it applies to staff conduct. The return to growth from late 2025, the launch of Hengyi Phase II and the Muara Port expansion show that diversification is moving from plan to delivery.

Who should I talk to first?

Prime Minister's Office (His Majesty Sultan Haji Hassanal Bolkiah)

Centre of executive authority; His Majesty is also Minister of Finance and Economy and sets national priorities under Wawasan Brunei 2035

Primary reference point for strategic direction and high-level investment partnerships

Brunei Economic Development Board (BEDB)

Frontline investment promotion agency; steers projects through the Foreign Direct Investment and Downstream Industry Committee

Standard first point of contact for market entry, incentives and approvals

Ministry of Finance and Economy

Leads fiscal policy, the national budget and diversification planning, and oversees BEDB, DARe and national statistics

Key counterpart for incentive certificates and policy dialogue

Brunei Darussalam Central Bank (BDCB)

Monetary and financial regulator; manages the currency arrangement with the Singapore dollar and supervises a well-capitalised banking sector

Relevant for banking, Islamic finance, fintech and currency matters

Darussalam Enterprise (DARe)

Manages 26 industrial parks and supports businesses with permits and licensing

Practical partner for site selection and operational set-up

Darussalam Assets

State holding company and joint-venture partner in Muara Port with Guangxi Beibu Gulf International Port Group

Relevant for logistics, port and state-linked co-investment opportunities

Hengyi Industries / Damai Holdings

Operator of the Pulau Muara Besar complex, 70% owned by Zhejiang Hengyi Group and 30% by Damai Holdings under the government's Strategic Development Capital Fund

Anchor for petrochemical supply-chain, services and downstream opportunities linked to Phase II

Business networks

The Brunei Economic Development Board and Darussalam Enterprise run regular investor outreach and are the main entry channels for foreign companies. Regionally, the BIMP-EAGA Business Council (Brunei) and Brunei's representatives on the ASEAN Business Advisory Council connect investors with private-sector networks across Brunei, Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines, and Brunei's hosting of the 2026 BIMP-EAGA meetings offers timely access to these forums. The Guangxi–Brunei Economic Corridor provides a framework for cooperation in aquaculture, spice processing, the digital economy and green technology. UK companies can draw on the Department for Business and Trade's in-market support, with UK–Brunei trade rising to £256 million in the four quarters to Q1 2026, while IMF Article IV reviews and ADB economic outlooks provide authoritative independent analysis.

Who this market suits

Brunei best suits investors seeking a stable, high-income and well-governed base with ASEAN, RCEP and CPTPP access: petrochemical and engineering firms positioned to serve the Hengyi Phase II expansion and its supply chain; logistics and port operators benefiting from Muara's capacity growth and direct China links; aquaculture, halal food and agribusiness investors aligned with food-security priorities; and ICT, Islamic finance and fintech firms supported by the Digital Economy Masterplan. Long-term strategic investors and sovereign or institutional partners comfortable with a small home market and a partnership-led approach with state entities are particularly well placed.
