The economy today

Dominica in October 2026 is a small, open island economy in an active investment phase. Real GDP grew 4.5% in 2025 (IMF preliminary estimate) after 3.5% in 2024, driven by public infrastructure investment and tourism, with growth of around 3% projected for 2026–27 and inflation of 2.3% in 2025. Tourism is a clear strength: stayover arrivals rose 19% to 99,846 in 2025, surpassing 2019 levels, with a further 10% gain in the first quarter of 2026, while the 2025–26 cruise season was the strongest since 2010–11. Two transformational projects are reshaping the outlook: the 10 MW Laudat geothermal plant, which entered commercial operation in 2026 and is expected to lift the renewable share of electricity from about 25% to about 75%, and a new international airport due for completion in 2027. Foreign direct investment was around 5.6% of GDP in 2025, supported by tax holidays of up to 15 years and full profit repatriation.

Dominica's defining structural challenge is exposure to hurricanes and natural hazards, and its response has made it a regional leader in resilience. Tropical Storm Erika (2015) and Hurricane Maria (2017) caused combined damage of about $1.8 billion, almost three times GDP; since then the country has rebuilt with climate-resilient housing, a World Bank catastrophe deferred drawdown option and a Vulnerability, Risk and Resilience Fund that the IMF recommends building to 12% of GDP. Reconstruction has left public debt at around 103% of GDP in 2025, and the IMF has recommended a primary surplus of about 3.5% of GDP to reach the regional 60% benchmark by 2035, a path supported by revenue reforms in the 2026/27 Budget. The geothermal plant, cutting oil import needs by around 20%, shows how resilience investment is also becoming a commercial opportunity in energy, construction and climate-adapted tourism.

How do I get in?

Invest Dominica Authority (national investment promotion agency) provides facilitation, incentive processing and sector guidance for priority areas including tourism, organic agribusiness, knowledge services and renewable energy

Fiscal Incentives Act (Chap. 84:51), as amended in 2019, offering tax holidays of up to 15 years and duty-free concessions for manufacturers, agro-processors, ICT enterprises and other service providers

Hotels Aid Act (Chap. 85:04) providing import duty exemptions for hotel and resort development, with Cabinet-approved development policies for tourism and real-estate projects above EC$3 million

Eastern Caribbean dollar pegged at EC$2.70 to $1 and managed by the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank, with 100% repatriation of profits and no capital gains tax

Legal system based on English common law; land purchases by non-nationals require a licence under the Alien Landholding regime

Citizenship by Investment programme, now being aligned with a regional regulator (ECCIRA) and a common 30-day physical-presence rule, channels investment into approved hotel and real-estate projects

2026/27 Budget measures including a proposed flat 10% personal income tax from January 2027 and tax deductions for commercial rental buildings and approved business disaster funds

Trade and market access

Dominica is a member of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS) Economic Union and the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union, giving tariff-free trade, free movement and a shared currency with its neighbours, and of CARICOM's single market. It is a WTO member and, through CARIFORUM, a party to the Economic Partnership Agreement with the European Union, while the US Caribbean Basin Initiative provides preferential access to the US market. Merchandise trade is modest, with 2023 exports of about $22 million to markets including Antigua and Barbuda, the EU and the US, and the economy's main export earner is tourism, served by direct flights from the US and regional hubs and a cruise sector at its strongest in over a decade. The new international airport due in 2027 is expected to transform direct connections to North America and Europe.

The overall risk picture

Dominica's risk profile is that of a small island state turning its exposure to natural hazards into a resilience and green-energy investment story. Growth of 4.5% in 2025, record stayover arrivals, a geothermal plant now supplying around half of electricity demand and a new international airport due in 2027 are tangible achievements that broaden the economy's base. Public debt of around 103% of GDP reflects post-hurricane reconstruction and capital investment, and the IMF-recommended consolidation path, supported by 2026/27 revenue reforms, targets the regional 60% benchmark by 2035; the EC dollar peg, backed by ECCB reserves of 97.6%, provides a stable currency anchor. Investors should note that the US has applied partial entry restrictions and shorter visa validity to Dominican nationals since January 2026, citing CBI vetting, and that the EU has raised concerns about CBI programmes; Dominica is responding with in-person passport collection, a physical-presence requirement and a new regional regulator. Investors should treat CBI-linked structures as a compliance matter requiring legal advice, and build hurricane resilience and insurance into project design.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the Prime Minister (Roosevelt Skerrit)

Sets the national development agenda, including the airport, geothermal energy, CBI reform and climate resilience

Primary reference point for understanding Dominica's strategic direction

Invest Dominica Authority

National investment promotion agency; processes fiscal incentives and facilitates foreign investment

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentive applications

Eastern Caribbean Central Bank (ECCB)

Regional monetary authority managing the EC dollar peg and supervising banks across the currency union

Relevant for currency, banking and payments matters

Ministry of Finance (Dr Irving McIntyre, Minister)

Leads fiscal policy, the 2026/27 Budget reforms and the debt-reduction path

Key counterpart for tax, incentives and public-private partnership discussions

Citizenship by Investment Unit

Administers the CBI programme that channels investment into approved hotel and real-estate projects

Relevant for developers using CBI funding, subject to the new regional rules

Dominica Electricity Services (DOMLEC)

National utility and offtaker under the 25-year power purchase agreement for the Laudat geothermal plant

Key counterpart for energy, renewables and power-intensive investment

Eastern Caribbean Citizenship by Investment Regulatory Authority (ECCIRA)

New regional regulator for CBI programmes in five OECS states, scheduled to launch in September 2026

Relevant for understanding future CBI standards and their implications for project financing

Business networks

The Dominica Association of Industry and Commerce is the principal private-sector body, alongside the Dominica Hotel and Tourism Association for hospitality investors. Invest Dominica Authority provides direct facilitation and sector outreach, and regional bodies including the OECS Commission and the ECCB, whose Monetary Council met in Dominica in July 2026, offer insight into the wider currency-union market. The World Bank, the Caribbean Development Bank, the Green Climate Fund and Canada are active financiers of resilience and clean-energy projects, including the geothermal plant and its transmission network, and are useful partners for co-financing. International hotel brands investing on the island, including InterContinental, Marriott and Hilton, are a practical source of market intelligence on tourism demand.

Who this market suits

Dominica suits investors seeking exposure to a high-growth eco-tourism destination with a strong resilience and clean-energy story: boutique and branded hospitality investors positioned for the airlift expansion that the 2027 international airport will bring; renewable energy and resilient-infrastructure investors building on the geothermal plant and donor-funded climate programmes; organic agribusiness, agro-processing and water investors drawing on fertile volcanic soils and fresh-water resources; and wellness and adventure-tourism operators around the Morne Trois Pitons National Park. Investors with long horizons and experience of small-island markets will be best placed, and should build hurricane resilience and insurance into project design.
