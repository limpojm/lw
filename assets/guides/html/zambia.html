The economy today

Zambia in October 2026 is a recovery story gaining momentum. Real GDP grew 7.7% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2026, led by agriculture, after 4.6% growth in 2025, and the IMF projects 5.6% growth for 2026 as mining and power output rise. Copper production reached a record of about 890,000 tonnes in 2025, with the government expecting output to pass 1 million tonnes in 2026 and targeting 3 million tonnes by 2031; mining investment commitments exceeded $10 billion during President Hakainde Hichilema's first term. FDI rose to $1.24 billion in 2024, the highest since 2015, and annual inflation was 6.3% in October 2026, after 6.1% in September, the lowest since 2018, allowing the Bank of Zambia to cut its policy rate by 250 basis points to 10.75%. President Hichilema's re-election in August 2026 with around 61% of the vote provides policy continuity, and IMF staff reached agreement on a new $1.47 billion, 36-month Extended Credit Facility on 9 October 2026.

Zambia's main structural challenge is completing its financial repair while broadening growth beyond copper, which earns more than 70% of export revenue. After its 2020 default, Zambia has finalised agreements covering about 94% of the $13.3 billion of external debt under restructuring, prompting S&P to upgrade the sovereign to CCC+ (stable) in December 2025, and the IMF projects public debt to fall from 133% of GDP in 2023 to about 78% in 2026. A 2024 drought exposed reliance on hydropower, which provides around 85% of generation capacity, yet copper output still reached a record in 2025. The new IMF programme targets a primary surplus of 3% of GDP by 2029 and greater transparency in mining licensing, while gross reserves of $6.1 billion provide a cushion. For investors, these priorities create opportunities in solar and diversified power generation, mineral processing, agriculture and the Lobito and northern export corridors.

How do I get in?

Zambia Development Agency (ZDA), the national investment promotion agency, providing investor registration, facilitation and access to incentives

Multi-Facility Economic Zones (MFEZs) offering a 10-year tax holiday for qualifying foreign investment, as announced by the ZDA in March 2026

Mining sector entry through licensing by the Ministry of Mines and Minerals Development, with the government targeting 3 million tonnes of copper a year by 2031

Bank of Zambia oversight of a flexible, market-determined kwacha; exchange-rate flexibility and deeper foreign-exchange markets are commitments under the IMF-supported programme

Joint ventures and partnerships with state investment holdings in mining and energy, and public-private partnerships in infrastructure overseen by the Ministry of Finance and National Planning

Lobito Corridor infrastructure participation via the Africa Finance Corporation-led Zambia rail project, which is raising debt and equity ahead of a targeted Q4 2027 financial close

Trade and market access

Zambia is a member of the Southern African Development Community (SADC) and the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA), giving preferential access to large regional markets, and is a party to the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) and a WTO member. Its location at the centre of southern Africa, bordering eight countries, positions it as a regional hub. Copper is exported mainly to Switzerland and China, with the Democratic Republic of Congo a major regional market. Exports move through ports in Tanzania, South Africa, Namibia and Mozambique, and the US-backed Lobito Corridor rail link to Angola's Atlantic coast will add a direct western route once complete, targeted for 2030.

The overall risk picture

Zambia's risk profile has improved markedly since its 2020 default. Debt restructuring is largely complete, S&P has upgraded the sovereign to CCC+ with a stable outlook, inflation is close to its lowest since 2018 and within the 6–8% target band, and the Bank of Zambia has been able to cut rates four times in a row. A 97.85% take-up of a 2026 cash tender for outstanding notes and a new IMF staff-level agreement point to growing confidence in the policy framework. The main considerations are the completion of the remaining creditor agreements, copper-price exposure, power-supply reliability after the 2024 drought, and fiscal slippage in 2026, which the new IMF programme is designed to correct. The August 2026 election delivered a clear result and policy continuity. Investors should build copper-price and power scenarios into project planning, and can take comfort from rising reserves, a recovering growth path and a government publicly committed to private-sector-led growth.

Re-elected in August 2026; sets the private-sector-led growth agenda, including the 3 million-tonne copper target

Primary reference point for Zambia's strategic and investment direction

Zambia Development Agency (ZDA)

National investment promotion agency; administers investor registration and MFEZ incentives

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentive guidance

Bank of Zambia

Monetary authority; manages the flexible kwacha and cut the policy rate to 10.75% in September 2026

Relevant for currency, financing and banking-sector matters

Ministry of Finance and National Planning

Leads fiscal policy, debt restructuring and the new IMF programme, and oversees public-private partnerships

Key counterpart on fiscal incentives, PPPs and sovereign-linked projects

Ministry of Mines and Minerals Development

Issues mining licences and drives the copper-expansion strategy

Essential for any mining or mineral-processing investment

ZCCM Investments Holdings

State-controlled holding company with stakes across the Zambian copper sector

Potential partner for mining joint ventures and value-addition projects

Africa Finance Corporation (Lobito Corridor)

Lead developer of the 515 km Zambia rail link to the Lobito corridor

Key counterpart for logistics, rail and corridor-linked investment

Business networks

The American Chamber of Commerce in Zambia, which works with the US Embassy to promote bilateral trade and investment, and the Zambia Chamber of Commerce and Industry are the leading business associations, alongside the Zambia Chamber of Mines for the extractive sector. The Zambia Development Agency hosts country-specific business forums with visiting delegations, such as its 2026 Finland Business Forum. Major mining houses including First Quantum, Barrick and Abu Dhabi's International Resources Holding maintain substantial in-country presence and are a valuable source of practical intelligence. The IMF, World Bank and African Development Bank are active in the country, while the Africa Finance Corporation, the US International Development Finance Corporation and the Development Bank of Southern Africa are leading financiers of the Lobito Corridor.

Who this market suits

Zambia suits investors seeking exposure to a recovering economy at the heart of global copper supply: mining and mining-services investors positioned for the drive toward 3 million tonnes a year; renewable-energy and power investors meeting strong demand from mines and industry; infrastructure and logistics investors linked to the Lobito and northern corridors; and agriculture and agro-processing investors building on the sector that led growth in early 2026. Manufacturers can benefit from MFEZ tax holidays and SADC and COMESA access. Investors should plan for copper-price cycles and power-supply variability, while benefiting from falling inflation and lower interest rates.
