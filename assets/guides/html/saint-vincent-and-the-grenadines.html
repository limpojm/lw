The economy today

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines enters late 2026 with a record tourism performance, a renewed investment agenda and transformed infrastructure. Real GDP grew 4.5% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025, driven by tourism and construction, and the IMF projects 2.8% growth in 2026 with medium-term growth of around 2.7%; inflation averaged just 0.9% in 2025. Stayover arrivals reached a record 120,599 in 2025, up 17.4%, supported by new airlift into Argyle International Airport and new room stock from Sandals and others, while foreign direct investment reached 11.8% of GDP. The EC$720 million Kingstown Port opened in October 2025, and the New Democratic Party government elected in November 2025 under Prime Minister Godwin Friday has prioritised ease of doing business, including a new Investment Act, five-day company registration and performance-based incentives.

The country's main structural challenge is the combination of climate exposure and the public-debt legacy of recovery and investment. Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 caused damage of about $230 million, or 22% of GDP, and public debt stood at 113.1% of GDP in 2025 after successive reconstruction and capital programmes. The response has been practical and well supported: the World Bank's $63 million BERRy project was launched within three months, the government is reactivating its fiscal responsibility framework and establishing a fiscal council, and pension reform has extended the fund's life to 2060. Membership of the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union, whose reserve backing ratio stands at 97.6%, anchors monetary stability. For investors, the rebuilding and resilience agenda, including a target of 60% renewable energy by 2030, creates sustained demand for resilient construction, clean energy, water and tourism infrastructure.

How do I get in?

Invest SVG (national investment promotion agency), led since April 2026 by Executive Director Anna Young, provides facilitation, incentive guidance and diaspora-investment outreach

Fiscal Incentives Act (No. 5 of 1990), administered by the Department of Industry, granting import-duty concessions for qualifying enterprises over periods of 5, 10 or 15 years

Hotel Aid Act (No. 16 of 1988), offering a 100% import-duty waiver on materials and equipment for hotel construction, expansion or improvement, available equally to nationals and non-nationals

Investment-climate reforms under the new government: a proposed Investment Act guaranteeing fair and equitable treatment and non-discrimination, performance-based incentives, company registration in five business days and a planned online Business Gateway

Eastern Caribbean Currency Union membership, with the Eastern Caribbean dollar fixed at EC$2.70 to $1 for 50 years and managed by the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank, removing exchange-rate risk for dollar-based investors

Co-financing and partnership opportunities in infrastructure, resilience and energy alongside the Caribbean Development Bank, the World Bank and the UK Caribbean Infrastructure Fund

Trade and market access

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is a member of the Caribbean Community (CARICOM) and its Single Market and Economy, of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States (OECS) Economic Union, and of the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union, giving investors a shared currency and regional market access across the Eastern Caribbean. It is a WTO member and, through CARIFORUM, a party to the Economic Partnership Agreement with the EU, while the US Caribbean Basin Initiative provides preferential access to the United States, the main market for its agricultural exports. Traditional exports include bananas, root crops such as dasheen and eddoes, arrowroot and fish, with regional markets in CARICOM offering diversification. The new Kingstown Port and Argyle International Airport significantly strengthen sea and air connectivity, supporting both trade and the tourism sector that drives the economy.

The overall risk picture

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines' risk profile reflects a small, open island economy that has delivered solid growth through successive shocks and is now converting a major public investment cycle into productive capacity: a modern port, an international airport attracting transatlantic and North American airlift, a new acute hospital and an expanding hotel base. The public-debt level of 113.1% of GDP is the principal macroeconomic consideration, and the IMF assesses the risk of debt distress as high and the debt as unsustainable without firm consolidation; the new government has committed to reducing debt, reactivating its fiscal rule and establishing a fiscal council, and concessional partners remain closely engaged. Climate exposure is a permanent feature of the operating environment, but the rapid, well-financed response to Hurricane Beryl demonstrates strong institutional and donor support. The fixed Eastern Caribbean dollar removes currency risk for dollar-based investors. Investors should build disaster and insurance planning into project design, and treat the planned CBI programme as a policy development to monitor rather than a source of demand.

Business networks

The Saint Vincent and the Grenadines Chamber of Industry and Commerce is the principal private-sector body for business liaison, while Invest SVG runs investor outreach, including its 2026 "Home is Where the Heart Is" diaspora investment tour across the UK, the British Virgin Islands and other markets, and works with the Centre for Enterprise Development on MSME support. The Caribbean Development Bank, the World Bank and the UK Caribbean Infrastructure Fund maintain active programmes in infrastructure, recovery and resilience, and are useful sources of project intelligence and co-financing. Sandals Resorts and other established hotel operators provide practical insight into tourism-sector logistics, labour and supply chains, and regional bodies such as the OECS Commission and the ECCB offer a wider Eastern Caribbean perspective.

Who this market suits

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines suits investors seeking exposure to a fast-growing tourism destination with improving connectivity: resort and boutique hospitality developers building on record arrivals and new airlift; yachting and marine-services operators in Bequia, Mustique, Canouan and the Southern Grenadines; renewable-energy, water and resilient-construction firms supporting the 60% renewable target and post-Beryl rebuilding; and agro-processing investors serving tourism supply chains and regional markets. Infrastructure investors and DFIs are well placed to partner on PPP structures, and the Vincentian diaspora is an active and growing source of investment. Investors should build hurricane insurance and resilient design into project economics.
