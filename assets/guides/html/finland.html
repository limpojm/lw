The economy today

Finland in November 2026 offers investors a stable, highly skilled euro-area economy that is recovering steadily and attracting record clean-energy investment. GDP grew 0.5% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, the fourth consecutive quarter of expansion, and 1.8% year-on-year, the strongest annual growth since late 2024. The Bank of Finland forecasts growth of 1.7% in 2026 and 1.6% in 2027, led by exports and investment. Foreign investors made a net €8.4 billion of direct investment in 2025, lifting the inward stock to €92.5 billion, with Sweden, the United States and Luxembourg the largest ultimate investors, and Business Finland recorded 527 foreign investment projects, 160 more than the year before. The Confederation of Finnish Industries reports a record €8 billion of green-transition investment completed in 2025, with about €24 billion of new projects launched, led by data centres and hydrogen.

Finland's main structural challenge is restoring its public finances while unemployment remains high. The general government deficit was 3.4% of GDP in 2025 and debt reached 88.5% of GDP, and the EU opened an excessive deficit procedure in January 2026, with a 2028 deadline to bring the deficit below 3%. Unemployment stood at 10.4% in September 2026, while defence spending is rising from 2.5% of GDP in 2025 to 3.2% by 2030 following NATO accession. The response is clear and credible: the government is combining spending cuts with growth measures, including the proposed cut in corporate tax to 18% and incentives for clean industry and data centres. Inflation of 2.5% in September is moderate, rapid growth in wind power supports energy-intensive investment, and the parliamentary election on 18 April 2027 will set the next phase of policy.

How do I get in?

Business Finland, the government's trade, innovation and investment agency, provides free support to foreign investors through its Invest in Finland services and funds R&D and innovation

A clean-transition investment tax credit, under which Business Finland has awarded €2 billion in credits to 37 projects, and a planned 10-year tax refund scheme for data centres

A 20% corporate income tax, which the government's 2027 budget proposal cuts to 18%

Euro-area membership: the euro is the national currency, with free capital movement under EU rules and monetary policy set by the European Central Bank

EU single-market and Schengen membership, giving free movement of goods, services, capital and people and access to all EU trade agreements

NATO membership and a defence budget rising from 2.5% of GDP in 2025 to 3.2% by 2030, opening routes for defence and dual-use suppliers

A large green-industry pipeline in wind power, hydrogen, data centres and bioproducts, creating supply-chain and partnership routes for international investors

Trade and market access

As a member of the EU, Finland gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Euro membership removes exchange-rate costs with euro-area partners, and Schengen membership allows passport-free travel. Sweden is Finland's largest investor and a key trading partner, and Finland's Baltic Sea ports connect it by sea with Sweden, Estonia, Germany and wider European markets. The country's position at the heart of the Nordic-Baltic region, alongside its NATO membership, makes it a growing base for defence, energy and digital-infrastructure supply chains.

The overall risk picture

Finland's risk profile is that of a stable, well-governed EU, euro-area and NATO member with strong institutions, a skilled workforce and a fast-growing clean-energy sector. The economy has expanded for four consecutive quarters, inflation is moderate and investment in data centres, hydrogen and wind power is at record levels. The main near-term issue is fiscal: the deficit of 3.4% of GDP in 2025 has placed Finland under an EU excessive deficit procedure, and debt of 88.5% of GDP and rising defence spending mean continued consolidation, though the government has protected growth measures such as the proposed corporate tax cut. Finland's land border with Russia is closed to traffic and EU sanctions on Russia apply in full, so investors should take sanctions and export-control advice for any Russia-related business; for all other activity, Finland offers the legal certainty of EU law and the convenience of the euro.

Leads the coalition government, which sets economic, fiscal and reform policy ahead of the April 2027 election

Primary reference point for Finland's strategic direction and major projects

Business Finland

National trade, innovation and investment agency; provides Invest in Finland services and administers innovation funding and clean-transition tax credits

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentives

Member of the Eurosystem whose governor sits on the ECB Governing Council; publishes the national economic forecast

Relevant for macroeconomic outlook, banking and payments

Ministry of Finance (Minister Riikka Purra)

Leads the budget, tax policy and the response to the EU excessive deficit procedure

Key counterpart on tax, fiscal and public-finance matters

Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment

Oversees industrial, energy and labour policy, including the framework for energy and industrial investment

Relevant for energy projects, permits and incentives

Ministry of Defence

Counterpart for defence and dual-use suppliers

Confederation of Finnish Industries (EK)

Leading employers' organisation, which tracks the green-transition investment pipeline

Useful source of market intelligence and introductions to Finnish industry

Business networks

The Finland Chamber of Commerce and its regional chambers, the Confederation of Finnish Industries and bilateral bodies such as the American Chamber of Commerce in Finland and the Finnish-British Chamber of Commerce are the main business networks. Business Finland organises investor programmes and represents Finland at international forums, while VTT, the state research centre, offers partnerships in AI, quantum and industrial technology. The European Investment Bank, which backed Finland with €1.6 billion in 2025, and the EU Innovation Fund co-finance energy, industrial and infrastructure projects. Embassies of major EU partners and the United States maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Finland suits investors seeking a secure, well-governed base in the EU and the euro area with a fast-growing clean-energy sector: data-centre and AI infrastructure investors drawn by clean power and planned tax refunds; energy investors in onshore wind, storage and hydrogen; industrial and bioproduct investors in forestry-based materials and renewable fuels; technology companies in telecoms, quantum, and health technology; and defence and dual-use suppliers serving a growing NATO budget. Investors with any Russia-linked activity should take sanctions advice before engagement.
