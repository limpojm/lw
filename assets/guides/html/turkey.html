Investor Entry Routes

  • Presidency of the Republic of Türkiye Investment Office (Invest in Türkiye), the national investment promotion agency, providing facilitation and sector guidance, with a single One-Stop Office model for investors under development
  • Foreign Direct Investment Law No. 4875, guaranteeing foreign investors national treatment with no minimum local shareholding requirement, supported by Turkey's membership of ICSID and MIGA
  • Investment incentive certificate system offering general, regional, strategic and project-based incentives, including VAT and customs exemptions on machinery, tax reductions and social-security support for new jobs
  • Law No. 7582 (June 2026), part of the Powerhouse for Investments programme, offering 95–100% corporate tax deductions for overseas trading profits and qualified regional service centres, and a 20-year exemption on foreign-source income for qualifying new residents
  • Istanbul Financial Center, with a 100% corporate tax deduction on qualifying financial-services export income extended to 2047 and freedom for participants to choose the governing law of contracts with each other
  • Free zones (customs and VAT exemption, 100% corporate tax exemption for manufacturers and free transfer of profits), Organised Industrial Zones and technology development zones with R&D tax exemptions until 2028
  • Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye oversight of a market-based lira; foreign investors can repatriate profits and capital, and standard hedging is advisable for local-currency exposure

Market-Entry Observation

Turkey enters late 2026 as a large, diversified and still-expanding economy. GDP grew 3.6% in 2025 to around $1.59 trillion, and growth continued at 2.5% in the first quarter and 2.3% in the second quarter of 2026, led by information and communication (+8.6%), agriculture (+13.3%) and industry (+2.4%). The September 2026 Medium-Term Programme projects growth of 3.3% in 2026 rising to 4.2% in 2027 and 5% by 2029, with the economy set to exceed $1.8 trillion this year. Exports reached a record $273.4 billion in 2025, and the 12-month total rose further to a record $283.7 billion by September 2026. Foreign direct investment rose 12.2% to $13.1 billion in 2025, with non-real-estate FDI of $10.7 billion the highest in a decade and cumulative inflows since 2003 exceeding $292 billion. The investment framework strengthened again in June 2026 with Law No. 7582, which introduced competitive tax treatment for regional headquarters, overseas trading and financial-services exports, and Fitch affirmed Turkey's BB- rating with a stable outlook in July 2026.

Turkey's main structural challenge is bringing inflation down durably while managing the economy's sensitivity to energy prices, both of which were tested by the regional conflict that began with the strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026. The policy response showed considerable resilience: the central bank drew on record reserves of $210 billion to steady the lira, tightened liquidity and kept its policy rate at 37%, and gross reserves recovered from $155 billion in late March to $188.2 billion by late August, standing at $171.6 billion in early October. Annual inflation eased to 29.73% in September from 32.6% in May, and the government targets single-digit inflation by 2029, while the current account deficit remains moderate at 2.3% of GDP on an annualised basis. Rising domestic gas output from the Black Sea Sakarya field, and steady progress on renewables and efficiency, reduce energy-import exposure over time and create investment opportunities in energy, while continued disinflation should gradually lower borrowing costs and strengthen the case for long-term investment.

Investor Risk Note

Turkey's risk profile reflects a large, diversified economy with deep industrial capacity, record export performance and a strong record of absorbing external shocks. The economic programme has kept monetary policy tight, the policy rate at 37% remains well above inflation, and the central bank's use of reserves during the March 2026 regional shock, followed by a substantial rebuilding of those reserves, demonstrates the credibility of its toolkit. Fitch's July 2026 affirmation at BB- with a stable outlook recognises low government debt, a resilient banking sector and continued market access. The principal considerations for investors are the pace of disinflation and the sensitivity of the external accounts to energy prices; both can be managed through contract indexation, currency hedging and energy-price scenario planning. Investors in export-oriented manufacturing, energy and services are best placed to benefit from Turkey's cost competitiveness and its expanding tax incentives.

Market Access

Turkey has been in a Customs Union with the European Union since 1996, giving tariff-free access for industrial goods to the EU single market, and bilateral EU–Turkey trade has reached a record $233 billion. It is a founding member of the WTO and has an extensive network of free trade agreements, including a 2021 agreement with the UK that is being upgraded through an enhanced FTA, where five negotiating rounds have been held and 11 chapters closed by mid-2026. Its main export markets in 2025 were Germany (8.1%), the UK (6.1%), the US (6.0%), Italy (4.8%) and Iraq (4.5%). Bridging Europe, the Middle East and Central Asia, Turkey is the western land gateway of the Middle Corridor and the northern end of Iraq's $17 billion Development Road, positioning it as a growing transit hub between the Gulf, Asia and Europe.

Current International Business Channels

The Foreign Economic Relations Board (DEİK) runs bilateral business councils with partner countries, including the United Kingdom, and is the main channel for structured government–business dialogue. The International Investors Association (YASED) represents foreign investors and publishes regular FDI analysis, while the Union of Chambers and Commodity Exchanges of Turkey (TOBB) and the Turkish Exporters Assembly (TİM) provide access to domestic companies and sector associations. The British Chamber of Commerce of Turkey supports UK firms in market. The EBRD, World Bank Group and other development finance institutions maintain substantial Turkish programmes in energy, infrastructure and private-sector finance, and Invest in Türkiye organises sector-focused investor events and outreach.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Turkey suits investors seeking scale, an established industrial base and a platform for regional reach: export-oriented manufacturers, notably in automotive, machinery, chemicals and electronics, benefiting from Customs Union access to the EU and from free-zone and industrial-zone incentives; companies establishing regional headquarters, treasury or trading operations under Law No. 7582 and the Istanbul Financial Center; energy investors in renewables, gas and efficiency; and technology and defence-industry partners drawn by a deep engineering talent pool. Logistics and infrastructure investors can position for the Development Road and Middle Corridor, while long-term investors with hard-currency revenues are best placed to manage inflation and currency movements during the disinflation period.
