Investor Entry Routes

  • ProInversión (private investment promotion agency) manages public-private partnerships and asset projects, with a portfolio of around $40 billion across 86 projects and $2.5 billion awarded in January–July 2026
  • Legal stability agreements under Legislative Decrees 662 and 757, signed with ProInversión, which lock in the tax and legal regime applicable to qualifying investments
  • Public-private partnership (APP) framework and the Works-for-Taxes (Obras por Impuestos) scheme, which set a record in 2024 with more than $1 billion of projects awarded
  • Private Special Economic Zones regime, with its regulation approved on 22 April 2026, offering tax and customs incentives and legal stability, alongside the existing Special Development Zones at Paita, Ilo, Matarani and Tacna
  • Central Reserve Bank of Peru (BCRP) oversight of a free-floating sol, with national treatment for foreign investors and no restrictions on capital movements or profit repatriation
  • Mining concessions and permits through the Ministry of Energy and Mines, which reports a mining project portfolio of more than $64 billion and mining investment of around $6.3 billion in 2026
  • PromPerú trade and investment promotion, which secured $2.5 billion in foreign direct investment commitments in 2025 and targets food, tourism, manufacturing, knowledge services and renewable energy

Market-Entry Observation

Peru in October 2026 combines one of Latin America's strongest macroeconomic foundations with a new government focused on growth and productivity. GDP expanded 3.4% in 2025, and the central bank projects 3.2% growth in 2026, led by non-primary sectors growing 4.7% and capital-goods imports up nearly 27% in Q2. Exports reached a record $90.1 billion in 2025, up 21%, delivering a trade surplus of $43 billion over the twelve months to April 2026 and lifting international reserves above $100 billion for the first time, equivalent to 28% of GDP. Public debt of around 30% of GDP and a fiscal deficit targeted to fall from 2.2% of GDP in 2025 to 1.8% in 2026 underpin its investment-grade standing. Peru accounted for 6% of Latin America's FDI in 2025 and holds a registered FDI stock of $34.2 billion. President Keiko Fujimori, inaugurated on 28 July 2026, appointed former central bank director Elmer Cuba as Economy Minister, with priorities of productivity, job creation and tackling tax evasion.

Peru's defining structural feature is the contrast between frequent political turnover, with nine presidents in a decade, and remarkable macroeconomic continuity. An autonomous central bank, fiscal rules and long-standing legal stability agreements have kept policy predictable across administrations, and Moody's has noted that Peru retains greater credibility than peers with similar ratings. The economy is currently navigating a strong coastal El Niño: sea temperatures were about 4.6°C above normal in September, anchovy and fishmeal output fell 73% in January–July, and BBVA Research estimates an impact of 0.9 points on 2026 growth and 1.3 points in 2027. Mitigation is under way, with the government reporting 60% of prevention works complete, and the event is strengthening the case for investment in water, irrigation, flood defence and resilient infrastructure. Inflation of 4.5% in September is above the 1–3% target range, and the central bank, holding its rate at 4.25%, has signalled readiness to act.

Investor Risk Note

Peru's risk profile reflects an economy with exceptional macroeconomic buffers and a long track record of policy continuity through political change. Reserves above $100 billion, public debt of around 30% of GDP, a fiscal deficit on a declining path and record exports provide substantial resilience, and Moody's has highlighted Peru's credibility relative to similarly rated peers. Political turnover is the principal consideration, but the new administration's appointment of a former central bank director as Economy Minister and its focus on productivity and investment are encouraging signals. Community relations in mining regions and informal mining require careful management, which experienced operators address through early engagement, local benefit-sharing and robust due diligence. The 2026 coastal El Niño is a temporary but material factor for fishing, northern agriculture and logistics into early 2027; investors should build climate scenarios into planning while recognising the opportunities created by prevention and reconstruction spending.

Market Access

Peru has been a WTO member since 1995 and is among the most open economies in Latin America, with free trade agreements covering its principal markets, including the US–Peru Trade Promotion Agreement, an FTA with China, a trade agreement with the EU (and a parallel agreement with the UK), and membership of the CPTPP, the Pacific Alliance, the Andean Community and APEC. China is the leading export destination, taking 36.2% of exports in 2025, with the United States and the European Union also major markets. Exports are anchored by copper, gold and other minerals, which grew 25.9% in 2025, alongside a fast-growing agro-export sector. The Chancay port, with direct services to Chinese ports, and the established hub at Callao give Peru a strong position as a Pacific gateway between South America and Asia.

Current International Business Channels

The American Chamber of Commerce of Peru (AmCham Peru), the Lima Chamber of Commerce and the Peruvian-British Chamber of Commerce are the leading channels for international business, alongside the national business confederation CONFIEP. Sector bodies such as the National Society of Mining, Petroleum and Energy (SNMPE), the National Society of Industries (SNI) and the exporters' association ADEX provide practical intelligence and policy access. ProInversión and PromPerú run regular investor events and roadshows, and the biennial PERUMIN mining convention in Arequipa is a key meeting point for the mining sector. The World Bank, IFC, Inter-American Development Bank and CAF – Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean maintain active programmes supporting infrastructure, climate resilience and private-sector development.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Peru suits investors seeking exposure to a well-managed, resource-rich economy with a strong Pacific trade orientation: mining and mining-services investors drawn by a project portfolio of more than $64 billion and a world-class copper endowment; infrastructure and PPP investors able to engage with ProInversión's $40 billion pipeline; logistics and manufacturing investors positioning around Chancay, Callao and the new private special economic zones; and agribusiness and food-processing investors building on record agricultural exports. Financial investors benefit from deep reserves and a credible central bank, while all investors should apply careful community-engagement and climate-resilience planning.
