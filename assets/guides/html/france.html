The economy today

France in November 2026 remains Europe's leading destination for foreign investment, combining the EU's second-largest economy with a deep talent pool, world-class infrastructure and almost entirely low-carbon electricity. The EY Attractiveness Survey recorded 852 foreign investment projects in 2025, ahead of the UK (730) and Germany (548), making France first in Europe for the seventh consecutive year; 42% were industrial projects, AI projects rose 26%, and 38% of international executives rate France the most attractive European country. The Choose France summit in June 2026 brought a record €93 billion of pledges from 71 groups, led by SoftBank's €45 billion commitment to AI data centres in Hauts-de-France. GDP grew 0.9% in 2025, ahead of government expectations, and EDF's six-reactor EPR2 programme, estimated at €72.8 billion, anchors a long-term pipeline of energy investment.

France's main structural challenge is restoring its public finances while sustaining growth. Public debt stands at about 119% of GDP, the deficit is projected at 5.4% of GDP in 2026, and the government's 2027 budget targets 5% through €54 billion of savings, to be negotiated in a parliament without a clear majority. Growth has also softened, with GDP slipping 0.1% in the third quarter of 2026 as investment and trade weakened, while inflation rose to 3.4% in October 2026 on higher energy prices. France's strengths remain considerable: a large and diversified economy, deep capital markets, euro-area membership and access to ECB policy, and a clear multi-year path back towards a 3% deficit. For investors, consolidation creates opportunities in efficiency, energy and private finance for infrastructure, and the April 2027 presidential election offers a defined point for policy clarity.

How do I get in?

Business France (Invest in France) provides free support for foreign investors, from site selection to incentives, and runs the annual Choose France summit, which attracted a record €93 billion of pledges in June 2026

A 25% standard corporate income tax rate, with a 15% rate on the first €42,500 of profit for qualifying SMEs; a temporary surtax on the largest companies was extended for 2026 with a €1.5 billion turnover threshold

Research and innovation incentives, including the research tax credit and the France 2030 investment plan, supporting R&D, industrial decarbonisation and strategic technologies

Euro-area membership: the euro is the national currency, with free movement of capital under EU rules

EU single-market and Schengen membership, giving goods, services, capital and people free movement across the EU

Foreign investment screening by the Ministry of the Economy applies to acquisitions in sensitive sectors such as defence, energy and critical technologies; early engagement keeps approvals predictable

Regional development agencies and industrial sites in each region offer local incentives, land and recruitment support

Trade and market access

As a founding member of the EU, France gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market of some 450 million consumers and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Euro-area and Schengen membership remove currency and border frictions with most of its neighbours, and Germany, Belgium, Italy and Spain are among its closest trading partners. France has Atlantic, Channel, North Sea and Mediterranean coastlines, with major ports at Le Havre, Dunkirk and Marseille-Fos, high-speed rail links to London, Brussels and other European capitals, and strong ties with francophone Africa. Its largely nuclear power system makes it a net exporter of electricity, an advantage for energy-intensive investors and data centres.

The overall risk picture

France's risk profile is that of a large, diversified and highly developed EU and euro-area economy with strong institutions, the rule of law and a long record as Europe's top destination for foreign investment projects. As a founding member of the EU and of the Financial Action Task Force, it offers investors legal certainty, a mature financial system and full access to the single market. The main issues are fiscal: public debt of about 119% of GDP and a deficit projected at 5.4% in 2026 mean continued consolidation, with a 2027 budget targeting 5% still being negotiated in a fragmented parliament. Growth has slowed and inflation rose to 3.4% in October 2026, while the ECB, which held its deposit rate at 2.50% on 29 October 2026, is managing energy-driven price pressures. The presidential election of April and May 2027 is the key political milestone. None of these issues restricts lawful investment, and investors can manage them through scenario planning, tax advice and early engagement with government.

Who should I talk to first?

Presidency (President Emmanuel Macron) and Prime Minister's office (Sébastien Lecornu)

Set economic, fiscal and industrial strategy; the President hosts the Choose France summit

Primary reference point for strategic direction and flagship projects

Business France (Invest in France)

National agency for foreign investment and export promotion, supporting investors from first contact to implementation

Standard first point of contact for market entry, sites and incentives

Banque de France (Governor Emmanuel Moulin)

Part of the Eurosystem; its governor sits on the ECB Governing Council, and it oversees banking supervision with the ECB

Relevant for banking, payments and financial-sector matters

Ministry of the Economy and Finance (Minister Roland Lescure)

Leads the budget, tax policy and foreign investment screening

Key counterpart on tax, fiscal and approval matters

EDF

State-owned power utility leading the six-reactor EPR2 programme and the existing nuclear fleet

Essential partner for nuclear, power and energy-infrastructure engagement

Bpifrance

Public investment bank financing and co-investing in companies, innovation and industrial projects

Co-investment and financing partner for growth and industrial projects

Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF)

Regulator of the Paris financial markets

Relevant for listings, funds and capital-markets activity

Business networks

The American Chamber of Commerce in France, the Franco-British Chamber, the French-German Chamber of Commerce and Industry and the national network of Chambers of Commerce and Industry are the main business associations, with active sector committees and regular dialogue with government. MEDEF represents French employers, and Business France and regional development agencies run investor events, culminating in the annual Choose France summit at Versailles. Bpifrance, the European Investment Bank and the Caisse des Dépôts provide co-financing for qualifying projects, and France 2030 calls for projects offer support in strategic sectors. Embassies of major partners maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

France suits investors seeking scale, talent and stability inside the EU and the euro area: AI and data-centre investors drawn by low-carbon power and the record Choose France commitments; manufacturers in aerospace, automotive, chemicals and life sciences serving European markets; energy and infrastructure investors targeting the EPR2 nuclear programme, grids and renewables; and financial, software and headquarters functions in Paris. Investors in sensitive sectors should plan for foreign investment screening, and all investors should factor the 2027 budget and election into their timing.
