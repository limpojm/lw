The economy today

Belarus has an export-oriented industrial economy, with GDP growth of 1.3% in 2025 and 2.1% in January–July 2026 against a government target of 2.8%. Inflation was a moderate 4.6% in September 2026, and the National Bank cut its refinancing rate to 9.25% from 1 June 2026. Gold and foreign exchange reserves were $14.6 billion on 1 October 2026, and foreign trade reached $89.8 billion in 2025, with exports of $41.4 billion. Since late 2025, the United States has eased part of its sanctions in step with the release of political prisoners, including 250 people freed in March 2026 and 25 more in September: it has authorised dealings with Belavia, removed the main potash companies from its sanctions list, rescinded its directive on the Ministry of Finance and the Development Bank, and relieved further industrial companies. US officials have linked further relief to further releases.

Belarus's defining feature for international investors is the legal environment created by its support for Russia's war against Ukraine and by domestic repression. The EU renewed its sanctions in February 2026 for one year without change, keeping 312 individuals and 57 entities listed together with broad restrictions on trade, finance, transport and energy, including a potash ban whose application to transit through Lithuania was upheld by the EU General Court in 2026. The UK maintains its own wide-ranging Belarus sanctions regime, and the EU and US did not recognise the January 2025 presidential election. For compliant investors, the opportunity lies in two areas: lawfully permitted activity, where US relief has widened the scope for US persons in particular, and careful readiness for any further easing, given Belarus's skilled workforce, industrial base and position on the China–Europe transit route.

How do I get in?

Sanctions licensing route: US (OFAC), UK (OFSI and the Department for Business and Trade) and EU national competent authorities issue licences; US general licences now cover Belavia (GL 11) and Belinvestbank (GL 14)

US partial relief since 2025: Belaruskali, the Belarusian Potash Company and Agrorozkvit were removed from the US sanctions list in March 2026, and Lakokraska and Bellesbumprom were relieved in September 2026; the EU and UK have not followed

Great Stone Industrial Park: corporate income tax exemption for 10 years from first profit, then half the national rate, and property and land-tax exemptions until 2062, for investments from $500,000

National Agency of Investment and Privatization, which promotes investment, and investment agreements with the state for priority projects, for investors from non-sanctioning jurisdictions

Eurasian Economic Union membership and the Union State with Russia, giving tariff-free access to EAEU markets

Normalisation-readiness route: maintaining compliant entities, contracts and relationships so that investors can act promptly if sanctions are lawfully eased further

Trade and market access

Belarus is a member of the Eurasian Economic Union and the Union State with Russia, giving tariff-free access to Russia, Kazakhstan, Armenia and Kyrgyzstan; it is not a WTO member. Foreign trade reached $89.8 billion in 2025, with Russia and other EAEU members the main partners. Brest sits on the main China–Europe rail corridor, but EU sanctions restrict many Belarusian goods and road carriers in the EU, potash cannot transit Lithuania to Klaipėda, and border crossings with Lithuania were closed temporarily in late 2025. As a result, exports flow mainly east and through Russian ports.

The overall risk picture

Belarus offers a skilled workforce, an industrial and IT base, moderate inflation of 4.6% and reserves of $14.6 billion, and the United States has eased part of its sanctions since late 2025 in return for prisoner releases. However, EU and UK sanctions remain comprehensive and were renewed by the EU in February 2026, covering trade, finance, transport, energy and potash, and the EU and US did not recognise the 2025 presidential election. Belarus's support for Russia's war against Ukraine means Russia-related sanctions and export controls often also apply. Investors must take specialist sanctions advice in every relevant jurisdiction, confine any activity to what is lawfully permitted for each party, and never use Belarus to route trade or payments that would be prohibited elsewhere. The opportunity is therefore limited to lawful activity now and to readiness for any further, lawful normalisation.

Business networks

Formal Western business channels with Belarus are limited by sanctions, and Western development lenders are not currently a route for new projects. The Belarusian Chamber of Commerce and Industry and the Great Stone Park Administration are the main local business contacts, and Chinese and Russian partners are prominent in current investment. For Western investors, the most useful channels are specialist sanctions counsel, embassy commercial and political sections, and industry associations that track the US relief process, together with the US envoy's announcements on prisoner releases and further licences.

Who this market suits

Belarus is currently suited only to a narrow range of investors: existing operators maintaining licensed or permitted activities; US persons in sectors now covered by US relief, such as potash trading or aviation-related services, where no EU or UK nexus arises; investors from non-sanctioning jurisdictions with strong secondary-sanctions advice; and long-horizon investors keeping a watching brief for further normalisation. Any engagement requires multi-jurisdiction sanctions advice and enhanced human-rights due diligence.
