Investor Entry Routes

  • Agence pour la Promotion des Investissements (API) as the national investment promotion agency, with incentives such as reduced registration fees and accelerated depreciation granted on application to the Ministry of Finance's National Committee on Investments
  • Special Economic Zones framework (Laws 24-2017 and 25-2017) covering Pointe-Noire, Brazzaville-Maloukou, Oyo-Ollombo and Ouesso, offering tax breaks, favourable tariffs and a one-stop shop
  • Bilateral investment treaties with the United States, France, China, Germany, Italy, South Korea, Switzerland and the United Kingdom
  • Production-sharing contracts and permits with the national oil company SNPC under the Ministry of Hydrocarbons for oil and gas participation
  • CFA franc (XAF) issued by the Bank of Central African States and pegged to the euro; no legal restrictions on repatriating investment funds through authorised banks, while extractive exporters must repatriate a rising share of export receipts (35% now, 50% from 2027, 70% from 2028)
  • Tax and legal reforms, including a cut in corporate income tax from 30% to 27%, with a new investment law and a state-enterprise governance law in preparation

Market-Entry Observation

The Republic of the Congo enters late 2026 with an improving growth profile driven by a new gas economy alongside its established oil sector. Government estimates put GDP growth at 4.3% in 2025 and 5.6% in 2026, rising to a projected 6.9% in 2027 as oil output increases from 105 million to 112 million barrels a year, while the IMF's more conservative estimates are 2.4% for 2025 and 2.8% for 2026. The second phase of Eni's Congo LNG project shipped its first cargo in February 2026, lifting liquefaction capacity to 3 million tonnes a year. Inflation remains low within the CFA franc zone, averaging 2.6% in 2025, and the regional central bank cut its policy rate to 4.50% in June 2026. FDI inflows were $596 million in 2025 on an FDI stock of about $35.9 billion, led by investors from France, China, the Netherlands, Mauritius and Italy. Reforms include a corporate tax cut from 30% to 27% and four special economic zones under development.

Congo's main structural challenge is its public debt, which the IMF estimates at 97.2% of GDP at end-2025, together with arrears to some creditors and large refinancing needs in the regional treasury market. The authorities are addressing this directly: the government formally requested a new IMF-supported programme in May 2026, adopted a 2026–2028 medium-term debt strategy in July 2026, and its draft 2027 budget projects a surplus of CFAF 432.4 billion and debt falling from 88.6% of GDP in 2026 to 80.9% in 2027. The 2025 budget closed with an overall surplus, and the IMF considers the debt sustainable provided fiscal adjustment continues. For investors, a new IMF programme would unlock concessional financing, speed up arrears clearance and strengthen public counterparties, while growth in gas, potash and SEZ-based manufacturing offers a path to a broader, less oil-dependent economy.

Investor Risk Note

Congo's risk profile combines substantial hydrocarbon and mineral wealth, a euro-pegged currency providing monetary stability, and a new gas-export industry with the need to rebuild fiscal headroom. The IMF classifies the country's debt as in distress but sustainable, conditional on continued fiscal adjustment, and the government's request for a new IMF-supported programme, its 2026–2028 debt strategy and its budget surpluses point to a clear consolidation path. President Denis Sassou Nguesso's re-election in March 2026 provides policy continuity, and the regional central bank has begun easing monetary conditions. Investors should plan for slower payment cycles with public counterparties, factor CEMAC foreign-exchange rules into treasury planning, and conduct careful due diligence, while recognising that successful IMF engagement would materially improve the operating environment and unlock concessional co-financing.

Market Access

The Republic of the Congo is a member of the Central African Economic and Monetary Community (CEMAC), which shares the BEAC-issued CFA franc and a regional customs and financial framework, and has been a WTO member since 1997. It participates in OPEC+ and is now an LNG exporter through Congo LNG. China is the largest export market at around 36% of goods exports, followed by Europe at 29% and Singapore at 13%. Pointe-Noire's deep-water port is the main gateway for oil, LNG, timber and container trade, and road upgrades between Brazzaville, Owando and the Cameroon border are strengthening links with the wider Central African market.

Current International Business Channels

The African Development Bank held its 2026 Annual Meetings in Brazzaville, underlining the country's engagement with development finance, and the AfDB, World Bank and IMF remain the main multilateral partners for infrastructure, energy and public-finance reform. The national employers' organisation is an active voice on business-climate reform, and the National Economic and Financial Committee (CNEF) provides regular official economic updates. Major investor communities from France, China, Italy, the Netherlands and Mauritius maintain a substantial in-country presence, with international energy companies such as Eni and Chinese contractors such as PowerChina's SEPCO offering practical insight into large-project delivery.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Congo suits investors with experience of resource-rich frontier markets: oil and gas companies and oilfield-service providers positioned for rising output and the expanding LNG industry; mining and fertiliser investors interested in world-scale potash and iron-ore resources; infrastructure, power and logistics investors able to work with DFIs on gas-to-power, ports and roads; and agro-industry and timber-processing investors drawn by abundant land and forest resources and SEZ incentives. Investors with patient capital and DFI partnerships are best placed to benefit as fiscal consolidation and IMF engagement progress.
