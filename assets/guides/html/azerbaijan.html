Investor Entry Routes

  • AZPROMO, the national investment and export promotion agency working alongside the Ministry of Economy, provides market-entry guidance and investor facilitation and convenes the Azerbaijan International Investment Forum
  • Alat Free Economic Zone, established under constitutional law, offering exemption from VAT, income and corporate taxes and customs duties, no restrictions on foreign-exchange transactions or profit repatriation, and a free choice of governing law except in criminal matters
  • Comparable zone-style incentives in the Karabakh and East Zangezur reconstruction region and in Nakhchivan, aimed at attracting manufacturing, energy and agricultural investment
  • Production-sharing and consortium arrangements with SOCAR and international partners for oil and gas participation, the framework behind the Azeri-Chirag-Gunashli and Shah Deniz developments
  • Renewable energy law setting state support mechanisms, producer selection rules, tariff determination and land allocation for renewable projects, used for the ACWA Power and Masdar programmes
  • Central Bank of Azerbaijan oversight of a stable manat, supported by record central bank reserves of $15.3 billion; foreign investors repatriated $3.4 billion in 2025
  • Co-investment with Azerbaijan Investment Holding, the state investment vehicle, as shown by the $211 million aluminium can plant agreed with Hungary's Hell Energy in the Alat zone

Market-Entry Observation

Azerbaijan in October 2026 offers a steadily diversifying economy backed by some of the strongest financial buffers in the region. GDP grew 1.4% in 2025 to 129.1 billion manat (about $75.9 billion), and growth accelerated through 2026 to 1.5% in January–September, led by the non-oil economy (+2.6%), with transport and warehousing up 10% and information and communication services up 11.1%. Fixed investment rose 10.9% to $8.64 billion in the first nine months, and foreign direct investment inflows reached $3.66 billion in the first half of 2026, up 13.8% year-on-year, with non-oil FDI up 19.6%. Inflation is within the central bank's target range at 5.7% (August), the refinancing rate stands at 6.5%, its lowest since 2021, and public debt is a modest 20.1% of GDP. Strategic foreign-currency reserves of $85.8 billion, including $72.6 billion in the State Oil Fund (SOFAZ), and a first-half current account surplus of $4.7 billion (12.4% of GDP) give the economy considerable resilience.

Azerbaijan's main structural task is reducing reliance on maturing oil and gas output while managing a more complex regional environment. Oil and gas value added fell 1.2% in the first nine months of 2026 and oil export volumes fell 7.8% in January–August, yet higher prices lifted oil export revenue 10.6% and total exports 27.8%, producing a trade surplus of $10.39 billion. The regional conflict involving Iran since February 2026 included a drone strike on Nakhchivan in March; Azerbaijan responded with measured diplomacy, did not join hostilities and reopened its border crossings within days. The country is turning its position into an advantage: transit freight reached a five-year high of 9.1 million tonnes in January–July 2026, gas exports reached 25.2 bcm in 2025 with more than half going to the EU, and renewables are planned to reach about 43% of the energy mix by 2035, creating opportunities in logistics, green energy and non-oil manufacturing.

Investor Risk Note

Azerbaijan's risk profile is that of an energy economy with exceptionally strong financial buffers that is steadily broadening its growth base. Strategic reserves of $85.8 billion, public debt of around 20% of GDP and a large current account surplus give the country wide room to absorb external shocks, and non-oil growth, rising investment and a 13.8% increase in FDI inflows in the first half of 2026 point to sustained momentum. The regional conflict involving Iran since February 2026 is the main external consideration: Azerbaijan has kept out of hostilities, managed the March drone incident in Nakhchivan through diplomacy and kept trade routes open, while the conflict has increased the strategic value of its Caspian and Middle Corridor routes. The peace process with Armenia is well advanced though not yet finalised, and its completion would unlock new transport links. Azerbaijan itself is not subject to broad sanctions, but investors trading along North–South routes should apply sanctions screening for Russia- and Iran-linked counterparties. Investors who build regional-security scenarios into planning and use the Alat Free Economic Zone's legal framework can approach the market with confidence.

Market Access

Azerbaijan sits at the junction of the East–West Middle Corridor and the North–South transport corridor, linking Central Asia and China to Georgia, Türkiye and Europe, and handled a five-year high of 9.1 million tonnes of transit freight in January–July 2026. The EU is the leading export market, taking 53.9% of exports in January–August 2026, with Italy the largest European buyer of both oil and gas. Gas exports reached 25.2 bcm in 2025 via the Southern Gas Corridor, including the Trans Adriatic Pipeline, while oil flows west through the Baku–Tbilisi–Ceyhan pipeline. Azerbaijan is not a member of the Eurasian Economic Union and is pursuing WTO accession, having signed bilateral market-access protocols with partners including Türkiye, Japan and, in 2026, China. Planned green-energy corridors to Europe and Central Asia, and the TRIPP route through Armenia to Nakhchivan, are set to widen access further.

Current International Business Channels

The American Chamber of Commerce in Azerbaijan is the leading Western business association, and AZPROMO organises business forums, trade missions and the Azerbaijan International Investment Forum. Bilateral business forums hosted by the Ministry of Economy, such as the 2026 Azerbaijan–Slovakia Business Forum, provide direct access to ministers and zone authorities. The annual Southern Gas Corridor Advisory Council brings together governments, energy companies and financiers, and BP and other international energy companies with long-standing Caspian operations are a valuable source of practical market intelligence. The EBRD and the Asian Development Bank are active development partners, including funding feasibility studies for the Baku-based Green Corridors Alliance, and Masdar and ACWA Power are anchoring a growing renewable-energy investor community.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Azerbaijan suits investors seeking exposure to a financially strong economy at the crossroads of Europe and Asia: logistics, port and rail investors positioned to benefit from record Middle Corridor transit growth; renewable-energy and grid investors aligned with the 2035 target of about 43% renewables and planned export corridors; manufacturing and agri-food investors using the Alat Free Economic Zone and reconstruction-region incentives; and energy-services and engineering firms supporting continued Caspian gas development. ICT and digital-services investors can build on double-digit sector growth. All investors should include regional-security scenarios and sanctions screening of neighbouring-market counterparties in their planning.
