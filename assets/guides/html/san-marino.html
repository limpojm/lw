The economy today

San Marino in November 2026 is on its strongest footing in more than a decade, with an investment-grade rating, falling debt and a signed agreement on access to the EU internal market. Real GDP grew 1.5% in 2025, with inflation of 2.3%, and the government expects 1.3% growth in 2026. Fitch raised its rating to BBB in June 2026 and S&P to A- in the same month, and in April 2026 the Republic issued a €350 million five-year Eurobond at 3.625%, against 6.5% on the bond it replaces. Public debt is projected to fall to 58.1% of GDP by end-2026, and the IMF has praised strong fiscal performance and substantial debt reduction. The labour market is tight, with strict unemployment of 3.1% and 5,318 registered companies at end-2025, and the EU Association Agreement signed on 19 October 2026 opens the way to the full EU internal market.

San Marino's main structural challenge is completing the repair of its banking system while broadening a small tax base. The IMF notes that legacy non-performing loans, high operating costs and tight capital at some banks still need attention, and the government is replacing perpetual bonds at the state-owned Cassa di Risparmio. Progress is clear: the banking system posted a fifth consecutive year of profit in 2025, with all banks profitable, and the Central Bank recorded one of its best results. On revenue, the income tax reform in force since January 2026 is expected to raise about €17 million a year, and the government is assessing a VAT. Ratification of the Association Agreement and the planned electronic invoicing system should modernise the economy, creating opportunities in financial services, technology and EU-facing manufacturing.

Agenzia per lo Sviluppo Economico – Camera di Commercio, a public-private company that supports foreign investors and helps identify local partners, including in the high-tech manufacturing cluster

EU Association Agreement signed on 19 October 2026, providing for participation in the EU internal market once ratified, with phased access for financial services

Trade and market access

San Marino is not an EU member, but it forms a customs union with the EU for goods and shares an open border with Italy, its main trading partner and source of cross-border workers. The euro is legal tender under the 2012 monetary agreement with the EU, updated in October 2026. The Association Agreement signed with the EU on 19 October 2026, after negotiations concluded in December 2023, will give San Marino participation in an extended EU internal market under common rules, with access for financial services phased in once its supervisory framework is judged equivalent. The agreement also covers cooperation in research, education, environment and consumer protection. San Marino's manufacturers already supply mechatronics, appliances and components into Italian and European supply chains across the neighbouring Italian regions.

The overall risk picture

San Marino's risk profile is that of a stable parliamentary republic with an investment-grade rating, the euro and close integration with Italy and the EU. The June 2024 general election gave the Christian Democrats (PDCS) the largest group, with 22 of 60 seats, and Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs Luca Beccari has led the EU negotiations. Rating upgrades by Fitch and S&P in 2026, a successful Eurobond refinancing and a declining debt ratio show that the fiscal position has strengthened considerably. The main issues are the remaining legacy of the banking crisis, which is being addressed through provisioning, recapitalisation and the conversion of perpetual bonds, and the time needed to ratify the Association Agreement. San Marino is not on the FATF grey list, and no sanctions or legal restrictions limit ordinary investment.

Business networks

The Agenzia per lo Sviluppo Economico – Camera di Commercio is the main channel for foreign investors, organising introductions to local companies, sector clusters such as high-tech manufacturing, and corporate, tax and legal advisers. San Marino Innovation supports technology start-ups and investors. San Marino has been a shareholder of the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development since 2018; although it is not an operating country, the government and the Chamber of Commerce are working to help local firms access EBRD support. Italian business associations and banks in neighbouring Emilia-Romagna and Marche, and San Marino's consulates abroad, including in London, are further sources of contacts.

Who this market suits

San Marino suits investors seeking a low-tax, euro-based European base with close links to Italian industry: manufacturers in mechatronics, electronics and appliances serving Italian and EU supply chains; technology start-ups and innovation firms using the 0–8% start-up tax regime; financial investors interested in a consolidating banking sector as it prepares for EU internal-market access; and tourism, hospitality and renewable-energy investors drawn by government plans for tourism sites and energy independence.
