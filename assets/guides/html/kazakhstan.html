Investor Entry Routes

  • Kazakh Invest (national investment promotion agency) provides one-stop facilitation, incentives, and sector guidance for foreign investors
  • Investment agreements under the Investment Policy Concept through 2030 offering legislative stability guarantees for up to 25 years
  • Astana International Financial Centre (AIFC), operating under English common law with its own independent court, offering a distinct regulatory environment for financial-services and related investment
  • Special Economic Zones (18) and industrial zones (67) offering tax holidays and customs exemptions for qualifying manufacturing and export projects
  • National Bank of Kazakhstan oversight of currency and capital-account activity; the tenge is free-floating with no major repatriation restrictions
  • Production-sharing and joint-venture arrangements with KazMunayGas for oil and gas sector participation
  • Altyn Visa programme offering tax advantages and streamlined residence procedures for international investors and entrepreneurs, alongside a National Digital Investment Platform acting as a one-stop shop

Market-Entry Observation

Kazakhstan in October 2026 combines strong and broadening economic fundamentals with a proven capacity to absorb external shocks. The non-oil economy continues to grow robustly: GDP expanded 4.1% in the first seven months of 2026 following 6.5% growth in 2025, with construction (+15.3%), manufacturing (+9%) and trade (+5.9%) leading real-sector growth even as oil output declined. Foreign direct investment reached $20.5 billion in 2025, up 14.4% year-on-year, with greenfield investment surging 266% to $19 billion and capturing nearly 89% of all Central Asian greenfield flows. The government's Investment Policy Concept through 2030, offering legislative stability guarantees for up to 25 years, an expanding network of special economic and industrial zones, and a new Constitution with investor-rights protections in force since July 2026 have materially improved the investment climate.

Kazakhstan's resilience has also been demonstrated in the energy sector. The Caspian Pipeline Consortium (CPC), which carries around 80% of Kazakhstan's crude exports and roughly 1% of global oil supply, has experienced periodic disruption from Black Sea conflict spillover since November 2025, including a temporary suspension of loadings in late July 2026. Operations were restored quickly on each occasion — loadings resumed on 27 July after about a week — and Tengiz production recovered after earlier interruptions. Kazakhstan has responded with measured diplomacy and practical action: engaging the US and EU on shipping security, directing additional crude to the Shymkent refinery, and accelerating diversification through the Atasu–Alashankou pipeline to China, whose capacity is set to reach 146.6 million barrels a year, and through increased trans-Caspian shipments. The wider economy has strengthened at the same time, with a $2.4 billion current account surplus in H1 2026 and combined reserves and National Fund assets of almost $139 billion providing a substantial buffer.

Investor Risk Note

Kazakhstan's risk profile reflects a country with increasingly credible non-oil fundamentals, strong financial buffers and a track record of managing external shocks. The government's investment-climate reforms, legislative stability guarantees of up to 25 years, the new Constitution's investor-rights provisions and the AIFC's common-law framework are genuine and sustained improvements that have driven strong foreign direct investment, including nearly 89% of Central Asia's greenfield flows in 2025. The CPC disruptions of late 2025 and 2026 were external in origin, and the speed with which exports were restored, together with a return to current account surplus in H1 2026, demonstrates the economy's resilience. Kazakhstan's balanced diplomacy and accelerating diversification toward China and the trans-Caspian route are steadily reducing concentration risk. Investors in oil-linked sectors should include short-term disruption scenarios in their planning, while investors across the wider economy can take comfort from the depth of Kazakhstan's reserves and the breadth of its non-oil growth.

Market Access

Kazakhstan is a member of the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU), providing tariff-free access to Russia, Belarus, Armenia and Kyrgyzstan — a market of more than 180 million people — and is a central link in the Trans-Caspian International Transport Route (Middle Corridor), which complements China's Belt and Road Initiative and has grown in strategic importance since 2022. The country has been a WTO member since 2015 and has an Enhanced Partnership and Cooperation Agreement with the EU, which takes around 60% of Kazakhstan's crude oil and petroleum product exports. Oil exports flow mainly via the CPC pipeline to Novorossiysk, with growing volumes moving to China via the Atasu–Alashankou pipeline and westward across the Caspian. This combination of EAEU membership, Western partnerships and Chinese connectivity gives Kazakhstan a genuinely multi-directional market access profile, with export flexibility increasing as new routes are scaled up.

Current International Business Channels

The American Chamber of Commerce in Kazakhstan and the EU-Kazakhstan Business Council are the leading Western business associations, alongside the National Chamber of Entrepreneurs "Atameken" for domestic business liaison. Kazakh Invest runs regular investor forums and maintains active sector-specific outreach. Major international oil companies with Tengiz and CPC stakes maintain substantial in-country presence and are a useful source of practical intelligence on energy-export logistics. The World Bank, EBRD, and Asian Development Bank maintain active country programs supporting infrastructure, energy transition, and private-sector development, and the AIFC itself increasingly functions as a networking and dealmaking hub for regional financial-services investment.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Kazakhstan suits a broad range of investors seeking exposure to a growing, diversifying economy with strong financial buffers: manufacturing and logistics investors benefiting from the Special Economic Zone incentive framework and Middle Corridor positioning; financial-services and fintech investors leveraging the AIFC's common-law structure; technology investors drawn by the national AI and digital agenda; and agriculture and food-processing investors building on strong recent growth in that sector. Oil and gas investors benefit from world-class resources and long-standing international partnerships, and should build standard contingency planning for export-route disruption into project economics.
