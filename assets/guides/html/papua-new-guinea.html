Investor Entry Routes

  • Investment Promotion Authority (IPA) certification under the Investment Promotion Act 1992, required for foreign enterprises before starting business and issued within 35 days of a complete application; small-scale activities on the Cottage and Business Activities List are reserved for nationals
  • Investment Promotion Act guarantees against expropriation without compensation, for repatriation of capital and earnings (subject to tax and exchange control laws) and for non-discrimination, supplemented by bilateral agreements with Australia, Germany and the United States
  • Special Economic Zones under the Special Economic Zones Authority Act 2019, offering corporate tax holidays of up to 15 years, duty-free capital goods and lower dividend withholding tax on equal terms for local and foreign investors
  • Bank of Papua New Guinea foreign-exchange regime: a crawl-like exchange-rate arrangement is narrowing kina overvaluation and supporting a gradual return to convertibility, with FX order backlogs significantly reduced
  • Petroleum and mining entry through gas agreements and special mining leases negotiated with the State, which typically exercises equity back-in rights through Kumul Petroleum Holdings or comparable state vehicles
  • Energy and infrastructure participation supported by a new off-grid regulation for mini-grids and by EU Global Gateway co-financing for hydropower and port projects

Market-Entry Observation

Papua New Guinea in October 2026 is completing a successful period of macroeconomic reform with a strengthening fiscal position and a large resource investment pipeline. The IMF estimates growth of 5.6% in 2025 and projects 3.8% in 2026 as LNG output levels off at full capacity, while the Bank of Papua New Guinea projects 3.5% for 2026 and held the Kina Facility Rate at 5.0% in September, with headline inflation of 5.3% in the June quarter. The fiscal deficit narrowed to 2.4% of GDP in 2025 and is projected at 1.1% in 2026, with a primary surplus for the first time since 2011, public debt expected to fall from 48.4% to 45.5% of GDP and a balanced budget targeted for 2027. The IMF programme of around $1.06 billion has met all its end-2025 performance criteria, gross reserves stood at about $4 billion (around five months of imports) at end-2025, and Papua LNG, a $14 billion project now operated by ExxonMobil, is targeting a final investment decision on 15 December 2026.

The economy's main structural challenge has been access to foreign exchange, long reflected in an overvalued kina and delays for firms remitting profits. Steady progress is being made: the crawl-like adjustment of the kina has narrowed its overvaluation, FX order backlogs have been significantly reduced, and the central bank is aligning its policy rate with the exchange-rate regime and strengthening the interbank market as it works towards full convertibility. High commodity prices are supporting inflows, with gold exports to Australia up 28% to K11.5 billion in the year to July 2026 and LNG plants running at 99.8% of capacity. With the IMF programme concluding at the end of 2026, the durability of these gains is a key point to monitor, but a new LNG train, a reopened Porgera mine and a growing SEZ network give Papua New Guinea a broader base of export earnings to support a more liquid currency market.

Investor Risk Note

Papua New Guinea's risk profile is improving on the back of three years of IMF-supported reform that have narrowed the fiscal deficit, put public debt on a declining path and eased the foreign-exchange shortages that long concerned investors. The country's resource endowment is world-class, and the near-term pipeline, led by the $14 billion Papua LNG project and supported by a reopened Porgera mine under a 51% PNG-owned structure, gives tangible evidence that large-scale partnerships can be concluded. Investors should plan for the practical realities of profit repatriation, customary land and lengthy project negotiations, and should apply enhanced due diligence following the country's placement under FATF increased monitoring in February 2026. The 2027 general election and the end of the IMF programme in 2026 are milestones worth monitoring, but the government's commitment to a balanced budget, the deepening security and economic partnership with Australia under the Pukpuk Treaty, which entered into force in July 2026, and continuing multilateral engagement provide a stable anchor.

Market Access

Papua New Guinea has applied the Economic Partnership Agreement with the EU since 2009, giving it duty-free, quota-free access to the EU market with flexible rules of origin, and it is a member of the WTO and APEC. China and Australia are its most important trading partners, alongside Japan, with LNG accounting for around a third of exports and gold, copper and palm oil also significant. The country is the largest economy in the Pacific Islands, and its location around 150 km north of Australia places it at the junction of Australian, Asian and Pacific supply chains. Growing investment in ports, including the EU-backed Rabaul upgrade, and the Caution Bay LNG export hub supports the country's role as an export platform for energy, minerals and agricultural products.

Current International Business Channels

The annual Papua New Guinea Investment Conference, organised by Business Advantage International and held in Brisbane in August 2026, where Prime Minister Marape welcomed genuine investors, is the leading international platform for the market, alongside the PNG Chamber of Commerce and Industry and the Australia-PNG Business Council for business networking. Major resource operators, including ExxonMobil, Santos, TotalEnergies and Barrick, maintain a substantial in-country presence and are valuable sources of practical intelligence. The IMF, World Bank, Asian Development Bank and IFC maintain active programmes on economic reform, infrastructure and the business environment, while the EU's Global Gateway and Australia's development and labour-mobility programmes provide co-financing and partnership routes for infrastructure, energy and skills investment.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Papua New Guinea is best suited to investors with a long-term horizon and experience in resource-rich emerging markets: energy and engineering firms positioned for Papua LNG construction and related services; mining companies and contractors seeking world-class gold and copper resources under partnership models with the State and landowners; infrastructure, power and port investors working alongside development finance institutions; and agribusiness, fisheries and processing investors able to use SEZ incentives and duty-free EU access. Tourism and property developers are also active in Port Moresby's licensed zones. Investors should be prepared to invest in local partnerships, community relations and careful treasury planning.
