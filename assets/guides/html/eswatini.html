Investor Entry Routes

  • Eswatini Investment Promotion Authority (EIPA) provides a one-stop shop for registration, permits and licensing, with an Investor Facilitation and Aftercare Department for visas and residence permits
  • Investor guarantees including 100% foreign ownership in most sectors, non-discriminatory treatment, protection against expropriation and guaranteed repatriation of funds; Eswatini is an ICSID member and introduced specialised commercial courts in 2022
  • Special Economic Zones under the SEZ Act 2018, including the Royal Science and Technology Park: 20-year corporate tax exemption then 5%, duty and VAT remission on inputs, and exemption from foreign-exchange controls, with new SEZ regulations at an advanced drafting stage
  • Development Approval Orders offering corporate tax of up to 10% for ten years (against a standard 27.5%) for qualifying manufacturing, mining, tourism and international-services projects, plus a 50% initial allowance on machinery and industrial buildings
  • Lilangeni pegged one-to-one to the South African rand within the Common Monetary Area, with full repatriation of profits and dividends permitted
  • Independent Power Producer route: around 80% of electricity is imported, and reforms are opening generation to private investors, as shown by Tsamela Solar PV reaching financial close in 2026
  • Royal directive to establish at least ten 'mega companies' with annual turnover of E10 billion each, creating a channel for large-scale partnerships with government

Market-Entry Observation

Eswatini enters late 2026 on the back of solid growth and a stable monetary anchor. Real GDP grew 4.9% in 2025 according to the IMF, with year-on-year growth of 5.7% in the fourth quarter, while inflation remains low at 2.5% in July 2026, comfortably inside the 3–6% target range. The lilangeni's one-to-one peg to the rand provides currency predictability, and the Central Bank of Eswatini moved its discount rate to 7.0% in September 2026 in step with South Africa to keep the peg secure. Investor interest is translating into projects: the inaugural Eswatini Investment Conference in May 2025 secured over E37 billion (about $2.2 billion) in pledges across energy, agro-processing, manufacturing, mining, logistics and textiles, and in April–June 2026 alone five projects worth E7.4 billion (about $440 million) moved into implementation, including an $80 million anchor commitment by Texray Group at the Taiwan Innovation Industrial Park. The 2026/27 budget raises capital spending by 8.1% to E7.78 billion, focused on infrastructure, energy, water and digital systems.

Eswatini's main structural challenge is the reliance of its public finances on Southern African Customs Union (SACU) receipts, budgeted at E11.4 billion for 2026/27, alongside a rise in public debt to 44.7% of GDP at end-2025/26 and reserve cover of around two to two-and-a-half months of imports. The government is addressing this directly: over E300 million is being placed in a SACU Stabilisation Fund, the wage bill has fallen from 42% to 33% of expenditure since 2018/19 with a target below 30%, and the IMF projects debt to peak above 52% of GDP before declining to about 45% by 2031/32 under a planned consolidation path. The rand peg and the Common Monetary Area framework provide a strong external anchor. For investors, the drive to broaden the revenue base is itself the opportunity: domestic power generation, irrigated agriculture, agro-processing and export manufacturing are national priorities backed by generous incentives, a young, largely English-speaking workforce and a current account surplus of 2.4% of GDP in 2025.

Investor Risk Note

Eswatini offers a stable, rand-anchored economy with low inflation, solid recent growth and a government actively seeking private partners. No international sanctions restrict investment, so the main considerations are commercial and governance-related. Public finances rely heavily on SACU receipts and debt has risen, but consolidation is underway with IMF engagement, a SACU Stabilisation Fund and a falling wage-bill share, and the Central Bank has shown it will act promptly to protect the peg. Eswatini is an absolute monarchy, the 2021 civil unrest and corruption perceptions remain relevant to planning, and investors should apply rigorous due diligence and anti-corruption controls while drawing on ICSID arbitration, specialised commercial courts and EIPA aftercare support. Investors in US-bound exports should confirm current AGOA status, while those focused on southern African markets benefit from a predictable currency and deep integration with South Africa.

Market Access

Eswatini is a member of the Southern African Customs Union (SACU), the Common Monetary Area, the Southern African Development Community (SADC) and the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA), and is participating in the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), giving manufacturers duty-free access to South Africa and preferential access to wider regional markets. South Africa is the leading export destination, taking around 62% of exports, followed by Nigeria and Kenya (about 7% each) and Mozambique. Key exports include soft-drink concentrates, sugar, processed fruit and apparel. Goods move mainly through the ports of Durban and Maputo, with transit rail traffic forming most of Eswatini Railways' revenue. SACU and the US have a Trade, Investment and Development Cooperative Agreement, and Eswatini's close partnership with Taiwan, including a $300 million financing agreement in 2025 and the Taiwan Innovation Industrial Park, adds a further investment channel.

Current International Business Channels

EIPA's Eswatini Investment Conference, first held in Ezulwini in May 2025, is the main platform for meeting government and major domestic companies, and the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Trade runs the Buy Eswatini platform, the Royal Signature Expo and trade missions to Taiwan. Leading corporates such as Royal Eswatini Sugar Corporation, Kellogg's Tolaram and regional banks including Standard Bank and FNB Eswatini are useful sources of practical intelligence. The US government actively promotes Eswatini to American companies through its Commerce Department market guidance, and the IMF and the AfCFTA Secretariat are engaged in supporting fiscal, regulatory and investment-protocol reforms.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Eswatini suits investors seeking a rand-anchored, low-inflation base with access to South Africa and the wider SADC and COMESA markets: renewable-energy and independent power producers responding to high import dependence; agribusiness and agro-processing investors linked to sugar and the new irrigation schemes; export manufacturers using SEZ and Development Approval Order incentives in textiles, food and light industry; and logistics investors positioned on the Durban and Maputo corridors. Mid-sized and specialist investors comfortable with a small domestic market of about 1.2 million people, and with standard governance due diligence, are best placed to benefit.
