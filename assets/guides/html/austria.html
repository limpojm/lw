The economy today

Austria in November 2026 offers investors a wealthy, highly skilled and stable euro-area economy at the heart of Central Europe, with a long record as a regional headquarters base for Central and Eastern Europe. The economy has emerged from the 2023–2024 recession: GDP grew 0.7% in 2025 and returned to growth in the third quarter of 2026, up 0.2% on the previous quarter, with WIFO forecasting 0.9% growth in 2026 and 1.3% in 2027. Investor interest remains strong: the Austrian Business Agency recorded a record 26,736 consultations in 2025 and supported 283 relocations and expansions, worth about €900 million and over 2,700 jobs, led by ICT and life sciences. The government's Industry Strategy 2035, launched in early 2026, commits €2.6 billion to research in key technologies, and voestalpine's €1.5 billion green-steel investment shows the scale of industrial renewal under way.

Austria's main structural challenge is restoring competitiveness and public finances after a period of high inflation and weak growth. Consumer price inflation was 3.6% in October 2026, driven by energy prices up 16.5%, the 2025 budget deficit was 4.1% of GDP and public debt 81.5%, and Austria has been under an EU excessive deficit procedure since 2025. The response is clear: the coalition government has committed to consolidating the budget within EU fiscal rules, the fuel price brake has been extended, and Industry Strategy 2035 targets energy costs and innovation. Austria's strengths remain considerable, including the euro, a highly skilled workforce, strong research capacity and a central location, creating opportunities in advanced manufacturing, green technology, life sciences and ICT.

How do I get in?

Austrian Business Agency (ABA) provides free location consulting, site search and support with permits and skilled-worker recruitment; it supported 283 company relocations and expansions in 2025

A 23% corporate income tax, group taxation for companies linked by more than 50% ownership, and more than 90 double taxation agreements

A 14% research premium on R&D expenditure, paid out even without a profit, certified by the Austrian Research Promotion Agency (FFG)

Industry Strategy 2035, with €2.6 billion for research and development in nine key technologies by 2029 and a planned industrial electricity price from 2027

Euro-area membership: the euro is the national currency, with free movement of capital and no exchange controls

EU single-market and Schengen membership, giving free movement of goods, services, capital and people

Red-White-Red Card and EU Blue Card routes for recruiting skilled workers from outside the EU, supported by ABA's Work in Austria service

Trade and market access

As an EU and euro-area member, Austria gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and all of the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Germany is its largest trading partner, and Austria's location on the Danube and the main Alpine transit routes connects it with Central and Eastern Europe, Italy and the Balkans. Vienna is a long-established gateway for companies managing Central and Eastern European operations. Austria's gas supply has diversified since OMV ended its Gazprom contract in December 2024, and its role as a Central European gas and power hub supports industrial investment.

The overall risk picture

Austria's risk profile is that of a wealthy, stable euro-area member with strong institutions, high living standards and deep legal certainty under EU law. A three-party coalition of the ÖVP, SPÖ and NEOS under Chancellor Christian Stocker took office in March 2025, committed to consolidating the budget within EU rules. The main near-term issues are the fiscal deficit, now under an EU excessive deficit procedure, inflation of 3.6% in October 2026 driven by energy prices, and slow growth; each is being addressed through consolidation measures, energy relief and Industry Strategy 2035. As a euro member, Austria's monetary policy is set by the ECB, which raised its deposit rate to 2.5% in September 2026 and held it in October. Investors with links to Russia should apply EU sanctions screening and take specialist advice, as Austrian banks and energy groups are still unwinding legacy exposures.

Who should I talk to first?

Federal Chancellery (Chancellor Christian Stocker)

Leads the ÖVP-SPÖ-NEOS coalition that took office in March 2025 and sets economic and fiscal priorities

Primary reference point for strategic policy and major projects

Austrian Business Agency (ABA)

National investment promotion agency supporting relocations, expansions and skilled-worker recruitment

Standard first point of contact for market entry, sites and permits

Oesterreichische Nationalbank (OeNB)

National central bank within the Eurosystem; its governor sits on the ECB Governing Council

Relevant for financial stability, payments and banking matters

Federal Ministry of Finance

Leads the budget, tax policy and the response to the EU excessive deficit procedure

Key counterpart on tax, incentives and public finance

Federal Ministry of Economy, Energy and Tourism

Responsible for Industry Strategy 2035, energy policy and business location policy

Relevant for industrial incentives, energy costs and tourism investment

Austrian Research Promotion Agency (FFG) and Austria Wirtschaftsservice (aws)

Public funders for research, innovation and business growth; FFG certifies the research premium

Key partners for R&D funding, grants and guarantees

Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (WKO)

Statutory business chamber representing all companies and running the Advantage Austria trade network

Valuable for market intelligence, partner searches and introductions

Business networks

The Austrian Federal Economic Chamber (WKO) and its Advantage Austria network of offices abroad are the main channels for business introductions and market intelligence, alongside the Federation of Austrian Industries and bilateral chambers such as the American Chamber of Commerce in Austria. Provincial business agencies, including the Vienna Business Agency and Business Upper Austria, support site selection and cluster partnerships at regional level. The Austrian Business Agency hosts investor events, while FFG and aws provide research and growth funding. Vienna is also home to international organisations including OPEC, the IAEA and UNIDO, which bring a steady flow of international events and networks.

Who this market suits

Austria suits investors seeking a stable, high-skill euro-area base with excellent research capacity: companies establishing regional headquarters for Central and Eastern Europe in Vienna; advanced manufacturers and automotive, machinery and steel-chain suppliers in Upper Austria and Styria; ICT, life-sciences and pharmaceutical firms using the 14% research premium; green-technology and energy investors supporting industrial decarbonisation; and tourism investors in the Alpine regions. Investors with Russia-linked counterparts should apply EU sanctions screening.
