The economy today

Moldova in November 2026 offers investors a low-tax, EU-oriented base on the EU's eastern border, with accession negotiations under way and strong EU financial support. GDP grew 2.4% in 2025, and growth improved to 0.9% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026 from 0.4% in the first, led by a 13.6% rise in exports and higher manufacturing, agriculture and energy output. Net foreign direct investment reached $459.5 million in 2025, taking the FDI stock to $6.32 billion, 84.5% of it from EU investors. The EU opened the first negotiating cluster with Moldova on 15 June 2026, and the €1.9 billion Reform and Growth Facility continues to disburse, with a third payment of €157 million in October 2026 bringing the total made available to €661 million. The IT park, where 2,725 resident companies generated turnover of MDL 18.9 billion in 2025, is a standout success.

Moldova's main structural challenges are energy security and rising inflation within a small, open economy. Inflation reached 6.96% in August 2026, above the National Bank's target range, and the bank raised its base rate in steps from 5% in May to 9% in September. The IMF expects a wider budget deficit of 4.8% of GDP in 2026, driven largely by higher capital spending. Energy resilience is improving: renewable capacity has passed 1,000 MW and supplied 29.1% of electricity consumption in 2025, and new interconnection with Romania is under way, while gas supply to the Transnistrian region is managed by Moldovagaz under arrangements running to the end of 2026. A new government led by Prime Minister Vasile Tofan, appointed in July 2026, has pledged privatisation, fiscal discipline and an accession treaty by 2028, creating opportunities in energy, infrastructure and export manufacturing.

How do I get in?

Invest Moldova Agency, the national investment and export promotion agency, providing investor guidance, site selection and aftercare

Investment law guarantees including non-discrimination, protection against unlawful expropriation and free transfer of profits, with 39 bilateral investment treaties; new legislation is set to replace Law 81/2004

A 12% corporate income tax, among the lowest in the region, with SMEs able to defer tax on profits reinvested in 2023–26

Moldova Innovation Technology Park: a 7% single tax on turnover for IT companies, with fiscal stability guaranteed until 2035

Regional State Aid Scheme for manufacturing: grants and corporate tax exemption covering up to 60% of eligible costs for investments from MDL 10 million

Free economic zones and industrial parks with customs, VAT and infrastructure benefits for export manufacturers

EU candidate status and the Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area, with free trade agreements covering 47 countries

Trade and market access

Moldova trades with the EU under its Association Agreement and Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area, and its free trade agreements cover 47 countries and a market of more than 870 million consumers. It is a WTO member and EU accession negotiations opened in June 2026, steadily aligning Moldovan rules with the single market. Romania is the main gateway to the EU, with road and rail crossings on the Prut and energy and gas interconnections, while trade with Ukraine reached an estimated $1.32 billion in 2025. The Giurgiulești port on the Danube gives access to the Black Sea, and Moldova is positioning itself as a logistics and supply base for Ukraine's reconstruction.

The overall risk picture

Moldova's risk profile is that of a small, reforming EU candidate on the frontier of the Russia–Ukraine war. Its strengths are clear: a 12% corporate tax, a successful IT park, EU candidate status with negotiations opened in June 2026, and €1.9 billion of EU Growth Plan support for 2025–27. The main near-term issues are inflation of 6.96% and a base rate of 9%, a wider budget deficit, energy security and the unresolved status of the Transnistrian region, whose gas supply is under temporary arrangements to the end of 2026. Political turnover in 2026 produced a new government with continued pro-EU direction. Moldova is not on the FATF grey list, and EU-aligned reforms are steadily strengthening the investment climate; investors should focus on right-bank locations and take advice on any Transnistria-linked dealings.

Business networks

The American Chamber of Commerce in Moldova, the Foreign Investors Association, the European Business Association and the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of the Republic of Moldova are the main business associations, with regular dialogue with government on reforms. Invest Moldova organises investor events and has studied Moldova's role in Ukraine's reconstruction, and the annual EU–Moldova Association Council and Growth Plan reviews set the reform agenda. The EBRD, the European Investment Bank, the World Bank and IFC are active in energy, transport, finance and private-sector development, and EU-funded programmes offer co-financing for qualifying projects.

Who this market suits

Moldova suits investors seeking a low-cost, low-tax base aligned with EU rules: export manufacturers in automotive components, electronics and textiles using free economic zones, industrial parks and state aid; IT and software firms using the 7% IT park regime; energy investors in renewables, storage and interconnection; agri-food and wine producers; and logistics companies supporting EU–Ukraine trade and Ukraine's reconstruction.
