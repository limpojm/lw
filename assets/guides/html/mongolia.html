Investor Entry Routes

  • Invest Mongolia (Investment and Trade Agency, under the Ministry of Economy and Development) facilitates and registers foreign investment and provides sector guidance
  • Investment Law (2013) stabilisation certificates fixing corporate income tax, customs duty, VAT and royalty rates for 5–18 years in mining, heavy industry and infrastructure (5–15 years in other sectors), with longer terms for projects above MNT500 billion
  • Foreign investment is generally permitted without prior approval; approval applies only where a foreign state-owned investor acquires 33% or more of a company in mining, banking and finance, or media and communications. A revised Foreign Investment Law is in preparation
  • Free Zone Law (2015) regime at Zamyn-Üüd, Altanbulag and Tsagaannuur, with Khushig Valley in development, offering customs, VAT and property-tax exemptions for qualifying activities
  • Floating tögrög managed by the Bank of Mongolia; profits and dividends can be remitted abroad subject to foreign-exchange compliance and withholding tax (10% on dividends), reducible under 26 double taxation treaties
  • Mining entry through licences under the Minerals Law and partnership with Erdenes Mongolia on deposits of strategic importance, as in the Oyu Tolgoi and Orano uranium investment agreements
  • Investment protection agreements with 43 countries, New York Convention arbitration and Energy Charter Treaty membership support dispute resolution for long-term projects

Market-Entry Observation

Mongolia in October 2026 is one of Asia's fastest-growing economies, powered by a mining upswing that is lifting exports, reserves and public revenues. GDP grew 7.7% in the first half of 2026, with mining up 31.5% and agriculture up 11.3%, following 6.8% growth in 2025; the IMF expects 6.2% for the full year. Exports reached $16.2 billion in January–September, up 51.2%, led by copper concentrate (+79.4%) as the Oyu Tolgoi underground mine ramped up to an annualised rate of around 450,000 tonnes, and gross international reserves rose to $8.9 billion by end-September from $7.0 billion at end-2025. Cumulative FDI since 1990 stands at $52.1 billion, almost 39% of it invested since 2020, and new flagship projects continue to break ground, including Orano's Zuuvch Ovoo uranium mine in June 2026. Stabilisation certificates under the 2013 Investment Law, the EAEU interim trade agreement in force since July 2026 and IMF-backed work on a revised Foreign Investment Law are strengthening the platform for investors.

Mongolia's main structural challenge is concentration: China takes over 93% of exports, and the country imports nearly all its refined fuel, much of it from Russia. Russian refinery outages produced fuel queues in Ulaanbaatar from August 2026, and together with food prices pushed inflation to 12.9% in September, prompting the Bank of Mongolia to raise its policy rate to 12.5%. Mongolia has responded on several fronts: mining revenues kept the budget balanced through August, reserves have reached $8.9 billion, and the $1.7 billion Mongol Refinery at Altanshiree, financed by India's Exim Bank and due in 2028, will provide 1.5 million tonnes a year of domestic refining capacity. The Gashuunsukhait–Ganqimaodu railway, the EAEU agreement and long-standing "third neighbour" ties with Japan, the EU, the US and the UK are widening trade routes, creating openings in fuel storage, logistics, processing and energy for investors positioned for diversification.

Investor Risk Note

Mongolia's risk profile is that of a resource-rich economy with exceptional growth momentum and improving buffers. Copper output at Oyu Tolgoi is rising strongly, exports grew 51.2% in the first nine months of 2026 and reserves reached $8.9 billion, while long-term investment agreements with Rio Tinto and Orano demonstrate that large, stable partnerships are achievable. The main risks are concentration in mining and on China, reliance on imported fuel, inflation of 12.9% and an expansionary fiscal stance that the IMF has advised should be balanced by rebuilding buffers. These are actively managed through monetary tightening, the Mongol Refinery, new rail links and wider trade agreements. Mongolia is not subject to international sanctions, but investors with Russian supply chains should apply standard sanctions screening. Investors are best protected by securing stabilisation certificates or investment agreements, hedging currency exposure and building fuel and logistics contingencies into plans.

Market Access

Mongolia has been a WTO member since 1997 and holds preferential access to Japan through an Economic Partnership Agreement in force since 2016, to the EU through GSP+ since 2005, and to Asia-Pacific markets through APTA since 2021. The EAEU interim trade agreement, in force since 22 July 2026, cuts duties on 367 product lines on each side with Russia, Kazakhstan, Belarus, Armenia and Kyrgyzstan. China is by far the main export market (93.2% of exports in January–September 2026), followed by Switzerland, while China and Russia supply most imports. The Trans-Mongolian Railway through Zamyn-Üüd and Altanbulag links Mongolia into China–Russia transit, and the new Gashuunsukhait–Ganqimaodu line will add a second heavy-freight rail link to China from 2027. Investment protection agreements with 43 countries and 26 double taxation treaties support cross-border structuring.

Current International Business Channels

The Mongolian National Chamber of Commerce and Industry is the principal domestic business body, while the American Chamber of Commerce in Mongolia, EuroCham Mongolia, BritCham Mongolia and the Business Council of Mongolia represent international investors and run regular policy dialogues with government. The annual Mongolia Economic Forum in Ulaanbaatar and the Mongolia–EU Business and Investment Forum provide high-level access to ministers and state companies. The EBRD, Asian Development Bank and World Bank Group maintain active programmes in finance, energy, infrastructure and SMEs, and Rio Tinto, Orano and other major operators are a valuable source of practical intelligence on mining logistics and investment agreements.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Mongolia best suits investors with long horizons and experience of resource markets: mining and mining-services companies drawn by world-class copper, coal, gold and uranium deposits; infrastructure, rail and logistics investors positioned for new China and EAEU links; energy investors in refining, fuel storage and renewables; and agri-food, cashmere and textiles processors building on strong agricultural growth and GSP+ access to the EU. Financial investors and DFIs co-investing alongside the EBRD, ADB and IFC can also benefit from the growth of Mongolia's banking and capital markets, provided currency exposure is hedged.
