The economy today

Spain in November 2026 is one of the euro area's best-performing large economies, with a broad base in services, tourism, manufacturing and renewable energy. GDP grew 2.8% in 2025, roughly double the EU average, and 2.7% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, with exports growing faster than imports and employment reaching a record 22.85 million. The Banco de España expects growth of 2.3% in 2026. Public debt fell to 100.7% of GDP in 2025, its lowest since 2020, and the deficit narrowed to 2.18% of GDP. Foreign direct investment totalled €30.76 billion in 2025, led by Luxembourg, the United States and the United Kingdom; it was lower than in 2024 but rebounded strongly in the second half to almost €11 billion a quarter. Flagship projects such as the €4.1 billion CATL–Stellantis battery plant in Aragon show Spain's pull for industrial investment.

Spain's main structural challenge is political fragmentation, which has left the country without a new state budget since 2023 and is complicating the final stage of its EU recovery plan. The minority government's 2027 spending framework was rejected by parliament in July 2026, and the next general election must be held by August 2027. Inflation reached 4.9% in October 2026, driven by energy prices, and unemployment stood at 10.18% in the third quarter. The fundamentals nonetheless remain strong: rolled-over budgets have not prevented deficit reduction, €78 billion of recovery funds has already been received, and abundant renewable power gives Spain a lasting cost advantage. This creates opportunities in energy-intensive industry, data centres that can meet new renewable-sourcing rules, and electrification projects.

How do I get in?

ICEX–Invest in Spain, the national investment promotion agency, supported by regional development agencies offering site selection and incentive guidance

Corporate income tax of 25%, with 23% for SMEs (falling to 20% by 2029), 19–21% for micro-enterprises and 15% for new companies and qualifying start-ups

EU co-financing through the €102 billion Recovery and Resilience Plan, of which €78 billion had been received by August 2026, alongside EU cohesion funds

Low-cost renewable power: renewables supplied 55.5% of electricity in 2025, and long-term power purchase agreements are widely available

Euro-area, EU single-market and Schengen membership, with free capital movement under EU rules

Foreign investment screening for non-EU investors in strategic sectors, with authorisation required before completing qualifying transactions

Trade and market access

As an EU member, Spain gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and all EU free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Euro and Schengen membership remove currency risk and border checks with France and Portugal, its main neighbouring markets. Spain's Mediterranean and Atlantic ports and its position close to North Africa make it a natural logistics hub between Europe, Africa and Latin America, where shared language and long-standing business ties give Spanish-based companies an advantage. Exports grew 1.3% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter of 2026, and Spain's growing renewable surplus and interconnection projects support its ambition to become a clean-energy and industrial hub for southern Europe.

The overall risk picture

Spain has a low-risk profile as a large EU, euro-area and Schengen member with diversified growth, falling debt and deep capital markets. Growth of 2.8% in 2025 and 2.7% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026 has outpaced the euro area, the deficit has narrowed to 2.18% of GDP and debt to 100.7%. The main issue for investors is political: a fragmented parliament has prevented a new budget since 2023, and an election is due by August 2027, so tax and regulatory direction may change with the next government. Energy-led inflation of 4.9% and the end-2026 closure of the EU recovery facility also call for planning. Spain is a FATF member with no grey-list concerns, and non-EU investors in strategic sectors should allow time for foreign investment screening. EU law and the euro provide a stable framework for long-term investment.

Primary reference point for national priorities and strategic projects

ICEX–Invest in Spain

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentives

Banco de España (Governor José Luis Escrivá)

National central bank within the Eurosystem and banking supervisor; the ECB deposit rate has been 2.50% since September 2026

Relevant for banking, payments and financial-sector matters

Ministry of Economy, Trade and Business (Minister Carlos Cuerpo)

Leads economic policy, trade, the Recovery Plan with Brussels and foreign investment screening

Key counterpart on investment authorisations and EU funds

Ministry of Finance

Responsible for tax policy, budgets and EU payment requests under the Recovery Plan

Counterpart on tax and fiscal matters

Red Eléctrica (Redeia)

Transmission system operator managing grid access and expansion as electricity demand grows

Essential partner for energy-intensive investments and grid connections

Regional governments and development agencies

Autonomous communities such as Madrid, Catalonia and Aragon manage permits and offer their own incentives

Important for site selection, permitting and regional support

Business networks

The British Chamber of Commerce in Spain, the American Chamber of Commerce in Spain, the German Chamber of Commerce for Spain and the Spanish Chamber of Commerce are the main business associations, alongside the employers' confederation CEOE and regional business organisations. ICEX–Invest in Spain and regional agencies organise investor events and promote Spain internationally. The European Investment Bank and EU recovery and cohesion programmes co-finance energy, transport, digital and industrial projects, and the Spanish public bank ICO provides financing lines for companies. Embassies of major partners maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Spain suits investors seeking scale and diversity within the EU and the euro area: energy-intensive manufacturers and battery and electric-vehicle supply chains attracted by low-cost renewable power; renewable-energy, storage and grid investors; data-centre developers able to meet new renewable-sourcing rules; services, technology and headquarters functions in Madrid and Barcelona; logistics and agri-food companies using Spain's ports and its links to Latin America and North Africa; and tourism and real-estate investors.
