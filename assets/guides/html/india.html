The economy today

India in October 2026 remains the fastest-growing major economy, combining scale, momentum and a steadily improving investment framework. Real GDP grew 7.7% in FY2025-26 and accelerated to 7.8% in April-June 2026, with services expanding 12.1%; the IMF raised its 2026 forecast to 7% in October and the RBI projects 7.1% for FY2026-27. Gross FDI inflows reached a record $94.5 billion in FY2025-26, with equity inflows up 18% to $58.8 billion and US investment more than doubling to $11.2 billion, while total exports hit a record $863 billion. Reform momentum is strong: the Labour Codes took effect in November 2025, insurance was opened to 100% FDI, the India-UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement entered into force on 15 July 2026, and India-EU free trade negotiations were concluded in January 2026.

India's main structural challenge is its dependence on imported energy, with around 88.7% of crude oil imported. The West Asia conflict since late February 2026 has lifted oil prices, with Brent above $100 at times, and contributed to a weaker rupee, around 6% lower against the dollar this year, and retail inflation of 5.54% in September; the RBI responded by raising the repo rate to 5.50% on 7 October under a stance of calibrated tightening. The external position has proved resilient: the current account deficit was just 0.5% of GDP in April-June 2026, cushioned by net services receipts of $51.6 billion and remittances of $42.9 billion, foreign exchange reserves stood at $721 billion in mid-October, and RBI swap windows drew in more than $52 billion of FCNR(B) deposits. Officials reported that around 70% of crude imports were sourced from routes outside the Strait of Hormuz, and the drive for energy security is accelerating opportunities in renewables, storage and domestic energy infrastructure.

How do I get in?

Invest India (national investment promotion and facilitation agency under DPIIT) provides sector guidance, investor tracking and support with central and state approvals

Consolidated FDI Policy with most sectors open under the automatic route; insurance opened to 100% FDI under the Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha Act 2025, and Press Note 2 of 2026 eased rules for small, non-controlling stakes from land-border countries

GIFT City International Financial Services Centre (IFSC), regulated by IFSCA, offering a dollar-denominated, tax-efficient platform for banking, funds, bond listings and external borrowing

Special Economic Zones (368 notified) and state industrial parks offering ready land, customs facilitation and export infrastructure

Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) framework administered by the RBI: a market-determined rupee with current-account convertibility and repatriation of dividends and FDI proceeds, supported in 2026 by RBI FCNR(B) and ECB swap facilities

Production-Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes and the India Semiconductor Mission offering capital and output-linked support for manufacturing in electronics, semiconductors and other priority sectors

Four consolidated Labour Codes in force since 21 November 2025, replacing 29 laws and simplifying compliance for employers

Trade and market access

India is a founding member of the WTO and offers one of the world's largest domestic markets, with a population of more than 1.4 billion. It has an expanding network of trade agreements: CEPAs with the UAE (2022) and Oman (in force June 2026), the ECTA with Australia (2022), the EFTA Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (in force October 2025), the India-UK CETA (in force 15 July 2026) and an FTA with New Zealand taking effect on 20 October 2026, alongside the ASEAN-India FTA and South Asian arrangements. The India-EU FTA, concluded in January 2026, is expected to be signed in December 2026 and could take effect in 2027, while a bilateral agreement with the United States is under negotiation. Exports reached a record $863 billion in FY2025-26, split almost evenly between goods ($442 billion) and services ($421 billion). India is also a partner in the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor (IMEC), positioning it as a manufacturing and services base with growing preferential access to Western, Gulf and Asia-Pacific markets.

The overall risk picture

India's risk profile is that of a large, diversified and fast-growing economy with substantial buffers and a policy framework that has steadily become more investor-friendly. Record gross FDI of $94.5 billion in FY2025-26, the opening of insurance to full foreign ownership, the Labour Codes and a rapidly expanding trade-agreement network, including the UK CETA in force since July 2026, are sustained structural gains. The main near-term sensitivity is energy: the 2026 West Asia conflict has raised oil prices, pressured the rupee and lifted inflation, prompting a measured RBI rate rise to 5.50%. These pressures are external in origin and are being met from a position of strength, with a current account deficit of only 0.5% of GDP, reserves of $721 billion and growth still above 7%. Investors in energy-intensive or import-dependent activities should include oil-price and currency scenarios in planning, while investors in services, technology, manufacturing for the domestic market and energy transition can draw confidence from the breadth of India's growth.

Business networks

The UK-India Business Council, the Confederation of Indian Industry (CII), the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FICCI) and NASSCOM, the technology-industry body, are the principal channels for business liaison, alongside AmCham India and other bilateral chambers. The India-UK Joint Economic and Trade Committee and the new CETA's implementation bodies provide government-to-government routes for UK investors. Invest India and state investment agencies host regular investor forums, and state summits such as Vibrant Gujarat are major dealmaking events. The World Bank, Asian Development Bank, AIIB and British International Investment maintain large India programmes in infrastructure, energy transition and private-sector finance, and GIFT City increasingly serves as a hub for cross-border financial-services networking.

Who this market suits

India suits a broad range of investors seeking scale and long-term growth: technology, AI and global capability centre investors drawn by deep talent pools in Bengaluru, Hyderabad and other cities; manufacturing investors using PLI schemes, SEZs and new FTAs to build export platforms in electronics, semiconductors, automotive and pharmaceuticals; infrastructure, data-centre and renewable-energy investors aligned with record public capital expenditure and the energy-security agenda; and financial-services, insurance and fund investors using the newly opened insurance sector and GIFT City. UK investors are particularly well placed to benefit from the CETA. Investors in energy-intensive or import-dependent activities should build oil-price and currency scenarios into project economics.
