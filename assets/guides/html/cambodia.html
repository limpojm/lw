The economy today

Cambodia in October 2026 combines a strong investment track record with a manufacturing base that continues to expand. GDP grew 5.3% in 2025, exports rose 14.7% to $30.15 billion and total trade reached $64.03 billion. The CDC approved a record $10 billion of investment across 630 projects in 2025, up 45% year-on-year and expected to create some 438,000 jobs, with manufacturing taking 56% and infrastructure 38%. Momentum has continued into 2026, with $5.45 billion approved across 346 projects in January–August and total trade of $58.48 billion in the first nine months, including double-digit growth with ASEAN (+16%) and the EU (+14%). The 2021 Law on Investment, a widening network of 57 SEZs and industrial parks, Techo International Airport and a growing set of free trade agreements underpin Cambodia's ambition to move up the manufacturing value chain.

Cambodia's main challenge in 2026 is a set of overlapping external shocks that have slowed services while industry keeps growing. Higher global energy prices, the border dispute with Thailand, weaker tourism and a softer property market have led the IMF to project growth of 3% in 2026 (the National Bank of Cambodia expects 3.9%) and inflation of 5.6%, after 2.5% in 2025. The policy response has been prompt: a government support package of about $1.2 billion for households and businesses, $250 million of emergency ADB support, and the redeployment of returning migrant workers into domestic jobs. Reserves remain adequate at about eight months of imports, foreign investment has stayed strong and the riel has been broadly stable. The IMF expects growth to recover in 2027, giving investors who enter now an opportunity to build positions ahead of the upturn and of Cambodia's scheduled graduation from Least Developed Country status in 2029.

Where are the opportunities?

Phnom Penh

Capital and commercial centre, now served by Techo International Airport, which opened in September 2025; hosts one SEZ and six industrial parks

Financial services, logistics, light manufacturing, retail, real estate

Sihanoukville (Preah Sihanouk province)

Cambodia's deep-sea port and main maritime gateway, with six special economic zones serving export manufacturers

Port logistics, export manufacturing, tourism

Svay Rieng / Bavet (Vietnam border)

Cambodia's densest SEZ cluster, with 12 zones on the main road corridor between Phnom Penh and Ho Chi Minh City

Garments, footwear, travel goods, electronics assembly

Kandal and Kampong Speu (Phnom Penh periphery)

Fast-growing industrial belt hosting SEZs and industrial parks close to the capital's labour pool and airport

Manufacturing, warehousing, agro-processing

Siem Reap

Home of Angkor and the heart of Cambodia's tourism economy, positioned for recovery as visitor flows rebuild

Tourism, hospitality, cultural and eco-tourism

Funan Techo Canal corridor (Mekong to the coast)

Planned $1.7 billion waterway linking the Mekong to the sea, with completion targeted for 2028, to lower freight costs and route more trade through Cambodian ports

Inland logistics, river ports, construction

Banteay Meanchey / Poipet and Koh Kong (Thailand border)

Border provinces with three SEZs each; trade has been reduced by the border dispute, leaving capacity ready for a recovery once normal crossings resume

Cross-border trade, logistics, manufacturing

How do I get in?

Council for the Development of Cambodia (CDC), the national investment authority, offering one-stop online registration with ministry review within 20 working days

Law on Investment (October 2021) providing Qualified Investment Project (QIP) incentives: an income tax exemption of three to nine years or special depreciation of up to 200%, plus VAT and export-tax relief

Statutory guarantee that investors may freely buy foreign currency and transfer capital, profits, dividends and sale proceeds abroad through authorised banks, in a highly dollarised economy with a broadly stable riel

Special Economic Zones (34) and industrial parks (23) with operating factories at end-2025, offering serviced land and customs facilitation for export-oriented manufacturing

Preferential market access through RCEP and bilateral free trade agreements with China, South Korea and the UAE, alongside the October 2025 Agreement on Reciprocal Trade with the United States

Joint ventures and long-term leases with Cambodian partners for land-based projects, as land ownership is reserved for Cambodian nationals

Trade and market access

Cambodia is a member of ASEAN and the WTO and a party to the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), the world's largest trading bloc, which links it to China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand and its ASEAN partners. It has bilateral free trade agreements with China, South Korea and the UAE, and in October 2025 signed an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade with the United States, its largest export market, which took about 43% of exports in 2025. Vietnam, China, Japan and Canada follow as leading destinations, while trade with ASEAN and the EU grew 16% and 14% respectively in January–September 2026. Goods move by sea through Sihanoukville, by road to Vietnamese ports via Bavet, and by Mekong waterway, with the Funan Techo Canal set to add a direct inland-to-coast route by 2028.

The overall risk picture

Cambodia's risk profile reflects an export-manufacturing economy with strong investment momentum, adequate reserves and a clear growth strategy, temporarily slowed by external shocks. The 2026 slowdown is concentrated in tourism, construction and property, while garments, non-garment manufactures and agricultural exports continue to grow and record investment approvals point to a deep project pipeline. The Thailand border dispute has been contained by a ceasefire since December 2025, and trade has been redirected through Sihanoukville and the Vietnam corridor. Investors should treat sanctions and anti-money-laundering compliance as a core requirement: the US and UK designated 146 Prince Group-linked entities in October 2025, so thorough screening of counterparties, property owners and banking partners is essential and should be handled with qualified legal and sanctions counsel. With these safeguards in place, Cambodia offers open capital repatriation, generous QIP incentives and a cost-competitive base for regional manufacturing, with the IMF expecting growth to recover in 2027.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the Prime Minister (Hun Manet)

Sets national economic strategy under the Pentagonal Strategy, including industrial upgrading, infrastructure and diversification

Primary reference point for Cambodia's strategic direction and flagship projects

Council for the Development of Cambodia (CDC)

National investment authority; registers and approves Qualified Investment Projects and oversees SEZs

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentive applications

National Bank of Cambodia (Governor Chea Serey)

Central bank and banking supervisor; manages the riel in a largely dollarised system

Relevant for banking partners, currency matters and financial-sector entry

Ministry of Economy and Finance (Deputy Prime Minister Aun Pornmoniroth)

Leads fiscal policy, the 2026 support package and public investment, including partnerships with development banks

Key counterpart for tax, infrastructure and public–private projects

Ministry of Commerce

Negotiates and implements trade agreements, including RCEP, bilateral FTAs and the US reciprocal trade agreement

Relevant for export market access, rules of origin and trade facilitation

Sihanoukville Autonomous Port

Operator of Cambodia's deep-sea port and central to export logistics

Key counterpart for port, logistics and SEZ-linked investment

Ministry of Mines and Energy

Oversees power-sector policy and the expansion of renewable generation

Relevant for energy investment and for securing reliable supply to industrial projects

Business networks

EuroCham Cambodia, the American Chamber of Commerce in Cambodia and the British Chamber of Commerce Cambodia are the leading international business associations, alongside the Cambodia Chamber of Commerce for domestic business liaison. The US-ASEAN Business Council maintains an active Cambodia programme, and the government's long-standing Government–Private Sector Forum provides a channel for raising regulatory issues. The Asian Development Bank, World Bank Group (including IFC) and other development partners are active in energy, infrastructure, finance and skills, and are useful co-financing partners. Garment and footwear manufacturers' associations and established SEZ developers are valuable sources of practical intelligence on labour, logistics and compliance.

Who this market suits

Cambodia suits investors seeking a cost-competitive, open manufacturing base within ASEAN and RCEP: garment, footwear, travel-goods and electronics producers diversifying regional supply chains through the SEZ network; logistics and infrastructure investors drawn by Sihanoukville, the Vietnam corridor and the Funan Techo Canal; renewable-energy developers responding to industrial power demand; and agribusiness and food-processing investors building on growing agricultural exports. Tourism and real estate investors with a longer horizon can position for recovery, while all investors should apply rigorous counterparty due diligence.
