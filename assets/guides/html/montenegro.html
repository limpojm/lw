The economy today

Montenegro in November 2026 is the front-runner among EU candidates, combining a euro-based economy, low corporate taxes and rapid progress towards membership. It has opened all 33 negotiating chapters and provisionally closed 21, including three on 12 October 2026, and the government is aiming for full membership in 2028. GDP grew 3.8% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, after 2.7% in 2025, with fixed investment up 12.7% and household consumption up 7.1%. Unemployment fell to a historic low of 10.5% in 2025. Foreign direct investment inflows reached €867 million in January–November 2025, with Turkey the largest source at €127.1 million, followed by investors from Serbia, Germany and the United States. Works on the €694 million second section of the Bar–Boljare motorway began in June 2026.

Montenegro's main structural challenge is strengthening public finances and the external position while sustaining growth beyond tourism. The budget deficit reached 4.3% of GDP in 2025, public debt was 64.3% of GDP at the end of the year, and the current-account deficit widened to 20.5% of GDP; inflation rose to 5.2% in September 2026. The IMF has recommended a stronger fiscal rule, an operational Fiscal Council and a gradual return to a balanced primary budget. The path is supported by the EU accession process, which anchors reforms and opens access to grants and concessional finance, including €150 million from the EU and a €200 million EBRD loan for the motorway. Diversification into energy, manufacturing, logistics and year-round tourism offers investors a growing range of opportunities.

How do I get in?

The national Investment Agency acts as a one-stop shop for foreign investors, providing information and access to incentives

Progressive corporate income tax of 9% on profits up to €100,000, 12% up to €1.5 million and 15% above, among the lowest in Europe

Eight-year corporate tax exemption, capped at €200,000, for new production companies in less developed municipalities

Port of Bar free zone, offering exemption from customs duties and VAT, unlimited duty-free storage and simplified procedures

Use of the euro as the domestic currency, removing exchange-rate risk for euro-based investors

EU accession front-runner status, with all 33 negotiating chapters open and 21 provisionally closed, steadily aligning laws with EU standards

EU and EBRD co-financing for transport and energy infrastructure under the Economic and Investment Plan for the Western Balkans

Trade and market access

Montenegro is a member of the WTO and of the Central European Free Trade Agreement (CEFTA), and its Stabilisation and Association Agreement with the EU gives most of its goods duty-free access to the EU market. Use of the euro removes exchange-rate costs for trade with the euro area, and the accession process is progressively aligning customs, competition and standards with EU rules, with the customs union chapter provisionally closed in July 2026. The Port of Bar links the country to Mediterranean shipping and, by rail, to Serbia, while the Bar–Boljare motorway will improve road links to Serbia and Central Europe. The EU's Growth Plan for the Western Balkans aims to open parts of the single market to the region ahead of membership.

The overall risk picture

Montenegro's risk profile is that of a small, open, euro-using economy on a credible path to EU membership. It is a NATO member, is not on the FATF grey list and is not subject to sanctions, and its accession talks are the most advanced in the Western Balkans, with 21 of 33 chapters provisionally closed by October 2026. The main issues are macroeconomic: a deficit of 4.3% of GDP, debt of 64.3% and a large current-account deficit, together with inflation of 5.2%. These are being addressed with IMF and EU guidance, and the EU process continues to strengthen institutions and the rule of law. Investors should plan for seasonal cash flows in tourism, slower permitting outside major projects and possible policy shifts around elections due by 2027, but the low-tax, euro-based environment and EU-funded infrastructure provide a supportive base.

Business networks

The Chamber of Economy of Montenegro, the American Chamber of Commerce in Montenegro and the Montenegrin Employers Federation are the main business associations, with regular dialogue with government. The Investment Agency of Montenegro organises investor meetings and promotes the country abroad. The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development, the European Investment Bank, the World Bank and the EU's Western Balkans Investment Framework are active in transport, energy and private-sector finance, and the EU accession process brings additional grant funding. Embassies of EU member states, the United Kingdom, the United States and Turkey maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Montenegro suits investors seeking a low-tax, euro-based entry point to the Western Balkans ahead of EU membership: tourism and hospitality groups targeting year-round and high-end segments on the coast and in the mountains; real-estate developers; logistics and processing companies using the Port of Bar free zone; construction and engineering firms working on EU- and EBRD-financed motorway and energy projects; and manufacturers able to use tax exemptions in less developed northern municipalities.
