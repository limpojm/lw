Investor Entry Routes

  • Ministry of Finance, which absorbed the former Ministry of Planning and Investment in 2025, together with provincial Departments of Finance, oversees foreign investment and issues Investment Registration and Enterprise Registration Certificates
  • Law on Investment No. 143/2025/QH15, in force since 1 March 2026, and its guiding Decree 96/2026/ND-CP, introducing a three-tier special-incentive structure for large projects and a pre-certificate route for establishing a local entity
  • Expedited investment procedure for projects in industrial parks and high-tech zones, which bypasses investment policy approval and moves directly to registration
  • Vietnam International Financial Centre (Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang) under Resolution 222/2025/QH15, offering 10% corporate income tax for priority sectors, a liberalised foreign-exchange regime, a choice of foreign law and English as a working language
  • State Bank of Vietnam oversight of foreign-exchange and capital-account matters; the dong is managed within a trading band and profits are remitted through licensed bank accounts
  • Equity-market entry following FTSE Russell's upgrade of Vietnam to Secondary Emerging market status, effective 21 September 2026
  • Sector market-access rules set out in the guiding decree, which lists sectors closed to foreign investors and a wider list of conditional sectors with ownership or licensing requirements

Market-Entry Observation

Vietnam in October 2026 is one of the fastest-growing economies in the world. GDP expanded 9.01% in the first nine months of 2026, accelerating to 9.95% in the third quarter, following 8.02% growth in 2025, with manufacturing (+11.36%) and construction (+12.22%) leading. Foreign investors are responding strongly: registered FDI reached $50.36 billion in the first nine months, up 76.4% year-on-year, while disbursed FDI rose 12.1% to $21.07 billion, the highest nine-month level in five years, after $27.6 billion in 2025. A sweeping reform agenda underpins this momentum, including a new Law on Investment in force since March 2026, a streamlined two-tier administration, the launch of the Vietnam International Financial Centre and FTSE Russell's upgrade to emerging market status from September 2026. Moody's raised Vietnam's outlook to positive in May 2026, citing improving institutions and faster project approvals.

Vietnam's principal structural consideration is the concentration of its export model on the US market and on imported inputs. The US took $140 billion of Vietnamese exports in the first nine months of 2026, and US trade policy has shifted repeatedly: a 10% Section 122 surcharge expired in July 2026 and an additional 12.5% Section 301 duty has applied to Vietnamese goods since 24 July, with further investigations open on intellectual property and excess capacity. Vietnam has responded constructively, with its Deputy Prime Minister holding talks with the US Trade Representative in August 2026 and committing to tighten controls on transshipment. At the same time, its network of trade agreements is broadening demand: the trade surplus with the EU rose 25.3% to $36.06 billion in the first nine months. For investors, rising scrutiny of origin rewards genuine local manufacturing and supplier development, creating opportunities in components, materials and higher value-added production.

Investor Risk Note

Vietnam's risk profile is that of a fast-growing, reform-minded economy with a diversified export base, rising investor confidence and a sovereign credit trajectory moving in the right direction: Moody's (Ba2) placed Vietnam on positive outlook in May 2026, describing it as the only Asia-Pacific economy with a positive outlook from the agency, while S&P and Fitch rate it BB+. Moody's cites a low and stable government debt load, with public debt forecast at 36–37% of GDP for end-2026, well within the National Assembly's 60% ceiling. The main external consideration is US trade policy, including the 12.5% Section 301 duty in place since July 2026 and pending investigations; Vietnam's constructive engagement with Washington and its wide network of trade agreements help mitigate this. Investors with US-facing exports should model tariff scenarios and maintain robust origin documentation, while those serving domestic and regional markets can draw on growth above 9%.

Market Access

Vietnam has been a WTO member since 2007 and is a member of ASEAN, giving it a central place in Southeast Asian supply chains. It is a party to the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP) and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), and holds free trade agreements with the EU (EVFTA) and the UK (UKVFTA), providing preferential access to most major economies. The United States is its largest export market, taking $140 billion of goods in the first nine months of 2026, while the EU generated a $36.06 billion surplus and China is its largest source of imports. Deep-water ports at Hai Phong and Cai Mep–Thi Vai, the forthcoming Long Thanh International Airport and the planned 1,541 km North–South high-speed railway strengthen its role as a manufacturing and export hub.

Current International Business Channels

The American Chamber of Commerce in Vietnam, the European Chamber of Commerce in Vietnam (EuroCham) and the British Chamber of Commerce Vietnam are the leading Western business associations, alongside the Vietnam Chamber of Commerce and Industry (VCCI) for domestic business liaison. The Vietnam Business Forum provides a regular platform for dialogue between foreign investors and the Government. Asian investor networks, including Japanese, Korean and Singaporean business associations, are highly active and offer practical intelligence on manufacturing supply chains. The World Bank, the Asian Development Bank and IFC maintain active programmes in infrastructure, energy and private-sector development, and the new Vietnam International Financial Centre is emerging as a networking hub for financial-services investment.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Vietnam suits a wide range of investors seeking exposure to one of Asia's fastest-growing economies: manufacturers in electronics, semiconductors, components and materials, particularly those that deepen local value added; logistics and infrastructure investors drawn by new ports, Long Thanh airport and the high-speed rail programme; financial-services and fintech investors using the Vietnam International Financial Centre; portfolio investors entering following the FTSE emerging-market upgrade; and consumer, retail and agro-processing investors serving a growing domestic market. Investors with significant US-bound exports should build tariff and origin-compliance planning into project economics.
