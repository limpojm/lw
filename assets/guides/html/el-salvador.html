Investor Entry Routes

  • INVEST (Agency for the Promotion of Investment and Exports of El Salvador), created in 2023 to replace PROESA, provides investor facilitation, sector targeting and export promotion
  • Free Zones Law (reformed December 2025) offering duty and VAT-free entry of goods, and up to ten additional years of incentives where investment and employment are doubled (minimum $500,000 for free zones, $800,000 for active processing depots)
  • Law for the Promotion of Innovation and Technology Manufacturing (2023) granting 15-year exemptions from income, municipal and import taxes for software, artificial intelligence, data analytics, cybersecurity and hardware manufacturing
  • Full dollarisation: the US dollar is legal tender, removing exchange-rate risk; foreign-owned firms paid $981.1 million in dividends in 2025, illustrating open profit repatriation
  • Investment Banking Law (August 2025) allowing institutions with at least $50 million in capital to serve sophisticated investors, including in digital assets; Bitcoin acceptance by businesses has been voluntary since 2025
  • Concession and mixed-economy models for infrastructure, as in the 50-year Acajutla and La Unión port concession (Yilport 80%, CEPA 20%), alongside multilateral co-financing from CAF, the IDB and Spain

Market-Entry Observation

El Salvador in October 2026 is one of the fastest-growing economies in Central America. GDP grew 3.9% in 2025 and accelerated to 4.8% in Q1 and 5.1% in Q2 2026, led by construction, energy and professional services; the IMF (October 2026) and World Bank both project 4.5% for 2026, and the central bank 4.5–5%. Record remittances of $9.99 billion (+17.8%), a homicide rate of 1.3 per 100,000 and tourism receipts above $3.6 billion support demand, while net international reserves rose 15.2% in H1 2026 to $5.17 billion. A $1.4 billion IMF programme anchors reform, with staff-level agreement on its combined second and third reviews reached in September 2026, and Moody's moved its B3 outlook to positive in February 2026. Net FDI eased to $474.8 million in 2025 from $755.8 million, which the central bank attributes to intra-group debt repayments and dividends, alongside record foreign-company profits of $1.64 billion.

El Salvador's main structural challenge is public debt of around 88% of GDP, with interest absorbing more than 18% of revenues and no independent monetary policy under dollarisation. The authorities have responded with clear fiscal consolidation: the overall deficit narrowed to 2.9% of GDP in 2025 with a primary surplus of 1.9%, gross financing needs have fallen from 18.3% of GDP in 2024 to a projected 9.1% in 2026, and a $1 billion DFC-backed debt conversion has eased costs. The IMF programme targets a further 3.5-percentage-point improvement in the primary balance and is expected to catalyse over $3.5 billion from other lenders. Continued delivery, including pension reform, offers a credible path to rating upgrades and lower borrowing costs, improving the outlook for infrastructure and private-sector financing.

Investor Risk Note

El Salvador's risk profile reflects an economy with strong growth momentum, transformed public security and a credible fiscal adjustment under an IMF programme. Growth above 5% in Q2 2026, record remittances, tourism and reserves, and a positive Moody's outlook point to improving fundamentals, while dollarisation removes currency risk for foreign investors. The principal considerations are high public debt and interest costs, the timing of IMF reviews, and governance questions: the IMF has cited judicial independence as a constraint, presidential term limits were removed in 2025, a state of exception has applied since 2022 and the Foreign Agents Law imposes a 30% tax on foreign funding of covered entities. Investors can mitigate these through robust legal due diligence, international arbitration and CAFTA-DR protections, and multilateral co-financing. Continued fiscal delivery and reform progress offer a clear path to rating upgrades and a lower cost of capital.

Market Access

El Salvador is a member of the Dominican Republic–Central America Free Trade Agreement (CAFTA-DR) with the United States and, in January 2026, became the first country in the Western Hemisphere to sign an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade with the US, which removes reciprocal tariffs on hundreds of listed originating goods. It has been a WTO member since 1995, participates in Central American economic integration and trades with the EU under the EU–Central America Association Agreement. Central America took 53.2% of exports and the US 30.6% in H1 2025, with knitted T-shirts, sugar and sweaters the leading products sold outside the region. The Acajutla and La Unión ports, a road link from the Gulf of Fonseca to Honduras's Puerto Cortés and the forthcoming Pacific Airport strengthen El Salvador's role as a regional logistics gateway.

Current International Business Channels

The American Chamber of Commerce of El Salvador (AmCham) and the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of El Salvador (CAMARASAL) are the principal business associations, alongside the Salvadoran Industrial Association (ASI) for manufacturers and the FUSADES think tank for economic analysis. INVEST organises investor outreach and sector promotion. Multilateral lenders are active: the Inter-American Development Bank provided a $500 million loan in 2025 and IDB Invest has financed private energy projects, CAF and Spain are co-financing the Pacific Airport, and the US DFC supported a $1 billion debt conversion. The IMF programme is expected to catalyse over $3.5 billion in further multilateral financing, creating co-investment opportunities.

Investor Profile Best Suited

El Salvador suits investors seeking exposure to a fast-growing, dollarised economy with close links to the US market: logistics and infrastructure investors drawn by the port and airport programmes; tourism and real estate developers building on record visitor numbers; manufacturers and service exporters using free trade zones and CAFTA-DR access; and technology, fintech and digital-asset firms benefiting from 15-year tax exemptions and the new investment banking framework. Energy investors will find openings in generation and efficiency. All investors should build governance and legal due diligence into entry planning.
