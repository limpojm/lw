The economy today

North Korea's economy has returned to steady growth despite comprehensive sanctions. The Bank of Korea estimates real GDP grew 3.5% in 2025, after 3.7% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2023, taking output above its 2017 level, with mining and manufacturing up 4.9% and construction up 6.3%; nominal gross national income was about $33.8 billion, around 1.8% of South Korea's. Trade with China, which accounts for about 98% of recorded external trade, rose 25% to $2.7 billion in 2025, back to pre-pandemic levels. Connectivity is expanding: a road bridge to Russia opened in September 2026 and the New Yalu River Bridge to China in October 2026, while Xi Jinping's June 2026 visit to Pyongyang and the five-year plan adopted at the Ninth Party Congress in February 2026 signal a focus on regional development and living standards. There is, however, no lawful foreign direct investment, and these estimates come from outside observers rather than published official statistics.

The defining structural reality for international investors is the sanctions framework. UN Security Council resolutions 1718, 2270, 2321, 2371, 2375 and 2397 prohibit joint ventures, most exports and imports, and financial support for trade, and the US, UK, EU and others add comprehensive national prohibitions. Recent growth has been linked by the Bank of Korea to cooperation with Russia, including weapons production and the deployment of around 15,000 troops, while the 11-country Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team reported in September 2026 that overseas labour earned $450–800 million in 2025 in breach of UN resolutions. For responsible investors the opportunity today is therefore limited and clearly defined: rigorous monitoring and supply-chain compliance, support for permitted humanitarian activity under UN exemptions, and long-term readiness should the Security Council ever lift sanctions.

Pyongyang

Sinuiju / Yalu River crossings (China border)

The existing Dandong–Sinuiju bridge carries over 70% of official China trade, and the four-lane New Yalu River Bridge opened on 15 October 2026 after a 12-year delay

Zone established in 1991 bordering China and Russia, with Rajin port (about 3 million tonnes capacity) and the Novichenko road bridge to Russia, opened in September 2026

How do I get in?

No lawful foreign investment route exists: UN Security Council resolution 2375 (2017) prohibits opening, maintaining or expanding joint ventures or cooperative entities with DPRK entities or individuals, and US, UK and EU measures add comprehensive national prohibitions

UN humanitarian exemptions: the 1718 Sanctions Committee grants exemptions for assistance and relief under Implementation Assistance Notice No. 7, valid for nine months and covering up to three shipments, with experienced aid organisations able to apply directly

United States: Executive Order 13722 prohibits new investment in North Korea and the export of goods and services by US persons; NGOs may use the OFAC general licence at 31 CFR 510.512 after notifying the State Department at least 30 days in advance

United Kingdom: the DPRK (Sanctions) (EU Exit) Regulations 2019, administered by OFSI and the Department for Business and Trade, prohibit fund transfers to or from the DPRK, with licences available for relief activities benefiting civilians subject to UN approval; the EU applies Council Regulation (EU) 2017/1509

Banking and repatriation: UN resolution 2321 required states to close bank offices and accounts in the DPRK except for humanitarian or diplomatic use and bars public and private financial support for trade, so no commercial payment or repatriation channel is available

Readiness route: legacy frameworks such as the Rason zone and the Kaesong precedent provide reference points for planning, applicable only if the Security Council and national authorities lift the relevant measures

Trade and market access

North Korea is not a member of the WTO or of any major trade bloc, and its external commerce is narrow: China accounted for about 98% of recorded trade in 2025, with wigs and hair products, worth $190.6 million, the largest export category permitted under UN rules. Most exports, including coal, iron, textiles, seafood and machinery, are prohibited by Security Council resolutions, and imports of refined petroleum are capped. Physical links are expanding, with the New Yalu River Bridge to Dandong opened in October 2026, direct flights and passenger trains to China resumed in 2026, and the Novichenko road bridge and an existing rail bridge connecting Rason with Russia. For international businesses, however, market access is effectively nil, and these corridors are best understood as indicators to monitor and as infrastructure relevant to any future normalisation.

The overall risk picture

North Korea is closed to lawful foreign investment, and investors should treat this as a fixed legal boundary rather than a risk to be managed. UN Security Council resolutions 1718, 2270, 2321, 2371, 2375 and 2397 prohibit joint ventures and cooperative entities with DPRK entities, most trade and financial support for trade, and remain binding on all UN members even though the UN Panel of Experts' mandate ended in April 2024 after a Russian veto. The US prohibits new investment and exports by US persons and can sanction foreign banks that facilitate DPRK trade, while the UK and EU prohibit fund transfers and related dealings. The economy has grown for three consecutive years and new bridges to China and Russia have improved connectivity, but none of this creates a lawful entry point. Investors should obtain specialist sanctions advice, screen supply chains, remote contractors and Russia-linked counterparties for DPRK exposure, support humanitarian objectives only through UN-exempted and licensed channels, and maintain a monitoring and readiness position for any future Security Council decision to ease sanctions.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the President of the State Affairs (Kim Jong Un)

Supreme decision-making authority setting economic, defence and foreign policy, including the five-year plan adopted in February 2026

Reference point for monitoring policy direction only; no engagement with designated persons

Cabinet of the DPRK (Premier Pak Thae Song)

Administers economic planning and external economic relations, and represented the DPRK at the 2026 bridge openings with China and Russia

Background for readiness analysis; any contact requires prior sanctions advice

Central Bank of the DPRK

Monetary authority operating outside the international financial system under UN and national banking restrictions

No dealings permitted; relevant only to future normalisation scenarios

UN Security Council 1718 Sanctions Committee

Oversees UN sanctions implementation and grants humanitarian exemptions under Implementation Assistance Notice No. 7

Primary reference for any permitted humanitarian activity

US Treasury (OFAC) and State Department

Administer US DPRK sanctions, the NGO general licence and the passport restriction, and can target foreign banks facilitating DPRK trade

Essential compliance reference for any business with US exposure

UK OFSI and Department for Business and Trade; EU national authorities

Administer UK and EU financial and trade sanctions and licences for relief activities

Licensing counterparts for humanitarian support from UK and EU entities

UN Resident Coordinator and UN Country Team (based in Bangkok)

Coordinate UN programmes in the DPRK, operating remotely since 2021 with a limited national staff presence

Channel for supporting permitted humanitarian programmes

Business networks

There are no conventional business channels for foreign investors in North Korea: no international chambers of commerce, business councils or development finance institutions operate there, and the World Bank, IMF and regional development banks have no programmes. The relevant channels are compliance and monitoring sources: the UN 1718 Sanctions Committee and its implementation notices, OFAC and OFSI guidance, reports of the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team established by 11 countries after the UN Panel of Experts lapsed, the Bank of Korea's annual estimates of the North Korean economy, and Chinese customs data. Humanitarian support can be channelled through UN agencies and licensed international NGOs, while specialist research institutes in Seoul, Washington and London provide continuing analysis useful for long-term readiness planning.

Who this market suits

North Korea is not open to commercial investors of any kind at present. The engagement profile is limited to humanitarian organisations and donors working under UN exemptions and national licences; compliance, risk and supply-chain teams at international companies, banks and digital-asset businesses that need to prevent indirect DPRK exposure; and long-horizon strategic investors, particularly those with Northeast Asian logistics, infrastructure or resource interests, who wish to maintain scenario planning and institutional knowledge for any future Security Council decision to ease sanctions.

Five steps from interest to engagement

  1. 1
    Obtain specialist sanctions advice and confirm the legal boundaryCommission counsel covering UN, US, UK and EU measures to confirm that no investment, trade or financial dealings with DPRK persons are permitted for your organisation.
  2. 2
    Screen supply chains, contractors and counterparties for indirect exposureApply enhanced checks for DPRK-origin goods, overseas DPRK labour, remote IT workers and digital-asset counterparties, including Russia- and China-linked logistics partners.
  3. 3
    Channel humanitarian support only through exempted and licensed routesSupport relief through UN agencies or NGOs holding 1718 Committee exemptions and the relevant OFAC, OFSI or EU licences.
  4. 4
    Establish a structured monitoring programmeTrack Security Council decisions, Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team reports, Bank of Korea estimates, Chinese customs data and border-infrastructure developments.
  5. 5
    Maintain a long-term normalisation readiness planKeep scenario analysis on logistics, resources and light manufacturing up to date, drawing on precedents such as Rason and Kaesong, without engaging DPRK counterparts unless and until sanctions are lifted.
