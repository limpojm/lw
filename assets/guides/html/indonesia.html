The economy today

Indonesia in November 2026 offers the scale and steady growth of South-East Asia's largest economy. GDP grew 5.11% in 2025, up from 5.03% in 2024, and accelerated to 5.45% in the first half of 2026 (5.61% in Q1 and 5.29% in Q2) and grew 5.47% in Q3, led by household consumption, investment, manufacturing, construction and ICT. Realised investment reached Rp1,010.6 trillion in H1 2026, about half of the Rp2,041.3 trillion annual target, with foreign investors accounting for around half, with Singapore, Hong Kong, China, Japan and the United States the leading sources and data centres emerging as the second-largest sector. Reforms to risk-based licensing, lower capital requirements for foreign companies, a reciprocal trade agreement with the United States signed in February 2026 and the forthcoming Indonesia–EU CEPA are broadening the opportunity set.

Indonesia's main structural challenge is sustaining market confidence in its currency and fiscal framework while funding an ambitious development agenda. The rupiah has weakened by about 7% in 2026, trading around Rp18,000 to the dollar in October, and Fitch moved its BBB outlook to negative in March, following Moody's, citing policy predictability and the cost of flagship programmes. Policymakers have responded decisively: Bank Indonesia raised its policy rate by 100 basis points to 5.75% in May–June and is intervening to stabilise the currency, while the 3% of GDP deficit ceiling remains in law. Buffers are substantial, with reserves of $146.3 billion covering 5.3 months of imports and a trade surplus of $7.25 billion in January–August 2026. For investors with dollar revenues or export orientation, a more competitive rupiah lowers local costs and supports entry valuations.

How do I get in?

Ministry of Investment and Downstreaming/BKPM, the national investment agency, which administers foreign-investment licensing and facilitation through the Online Single Submission (OSS) risk-based licensing system

Risk-based licensing reforms under Government Regulation 28/2025 and BKPM Regulation 5/2025, which cut the minimum paid-up capital for foreign-owned companies (PT PMA) from Rp10 billion to Rp2.5 billion

Special Economic Zones (25 locations) offering tax and customs incentives, with Rp353 trillion of cumulative realised investment by Q1 2026

Co-investment with Danantara, the sovereign investment fund, which plans around $13.1 billion of investment in 2026 across waste-to-energy, data centres, chemicals and agriculture

Bank Indonesia oversight of a floating rupiah; natural-resource exporters must retain export proceeds onshore (generally 100% for 12 months in state-owned banks), with an eased 30%/three-month option since September 2026 for qualifying mining exporters

Downstreaming and processing investment in nickel, bauxite and other minerals, where the government actively seeks foreign partners for value-added processing

Trade-agreement routes, including the US reciprocal trade agreement of February 2026 and the Indonesia–EU CEPA targeted to apply from early 2027

Trade and market access

Indonesia is a founding member of ASEAN, giving preferential access to South-East Asian markets, and a party to the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) and the WTO. A reciprocal trade agreement signed with the United States in February 2026 sets a 19% base US tariff with zero-duty treatment for 1,819 tariff lines, including palm oil, coffee, cocoa, rubber, semiconductors and aircraft parts. The Indonesia–EU Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement, under which the EU will remove duties on 98.5% of tariff lines, is targeted to apply from early 2027, and CPTPP members launched accession discussions with Indonesia in June 2026, with full membership targeted for 2027. Exports reached $193.6 billion in January–August 2026, with China, the United States and India the leading markets, while Indonesia has also joined the BRICS-led New Development Bank.

The overall risk picture

Indonesia's risk profile is that of a large, diversified and steadily growing economy managing a period of currency and market pressure. Growth has held above 5% for several years and rose to 5.45% in H1 2026 and 5.47% in Q3, realised investment reached Rp1,010.6 trillion in the first half, and reserves of $146.3 billion and a continuing trade surplus provide meaningful buffers. The main considerations for investors are rupiah volatility, rating-agency attention to fiscal and policy predictability, MSCI's review of the local equity market and export-proceeds retention rules for resource exporters. Each is being actively addressed: Bank Indonesia has tightened policy and intervened in the currency market, the 3% deficit ceiling remains in force, and retention rules have been eased for qualifying mining exporters. Investors should apply standard currency hedging, take local legal advice on licensing and sector rules, and model treasury flows around retention requirements, while recognising that a more competitive rupiah can improve entry costs for long-term investors.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the President (Prabowo Subianto)

Sets national economic priorities, including downstreaming, the free nutritious meals programme and an 8% growth target

Primary reference point for understanding Indonesia's strategic direction

Ministry of Investment and Downstreaming/BKPM

National investment agency; administers licensing through the OSS system and leads investment promotion and downstreaming policy

Standard first point of contact for market entry, licensing and incentive guidance

Bank Indonesia

Central bank; sets the BI-Rate (5.75%), manages reserves and the rupiah, and oversees export-proceeds placement

Relevant for currency, hedging and foreign-exchange regulation

Danantara (Daya Anagata Nusantara)

Sovereign investment fund leading large co-investments in downstreaming, waste-to-energy, data centres and agriculture

Key co-investment partner for large-scale and strategic projects

Ministry of Finance

Manages the budget, the 3% deficit ceiling, tax incentives and customs

Relevant for fiscal incentives, tax holidays and customs facilities

Financial Services Authority (OJK) and Indonesia Stock Exchange

Regulate banking, capital markets and listings, and are central to the market-functioning issues raised by MSCI

Relevant for financial-services entry, listings and portfolio investment

Ministry of Trade

Leads trade policy and export promotion, including IEU-CEPA implementation and CPTPP accession

Relevant for market-access planning and export strategy

Business networks

The American Chamber of Commerce in Indonesia, EuroCham Indonesia and the British Chamber of Commerce in Indonesia are the leading Western business associations, alongside the Indonesian Chamber of Commerce and Industry (KADIN) for domestic business liaison; bilateral chambers such as the German-Indonesian Chamber of Commerce (EKONID) are active on trade-agreement implementation. The Indonesia–UK Economic Growth Partnership signed in January 2026 provides a structured framework for UK engagement. BKPM and Danantara promote investment through domestic forums and international platforms, including the World Economic Forum. The World Bank, IFC and Asian Development Bank maintain large country programmes in infrastructure, energy transition and private-sector development, and can support co-financing for larger projects.

Who this market suits

Indonesia suits investors seeking long-term exposure to a large consumer market and a resource-rich, industrialising economy: mineral-processing and battery supply-chain investors building on the nickel and bauxite downstreaming programme; digital infrastructure and data-centre investors drawn by fast-growing demand; manufacturing investors targeting export markets under the new US and EU trade arrangements; and infrastructure, energy-transition and agribusiness investors able to partner with Danantara or development finance institutions. Investors with export revenues or natural currency hedges are particularly well placed, while those with local-currency revenues should build standard hedging into project economics.
