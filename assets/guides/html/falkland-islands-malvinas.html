Investor Entry Routes

  • Falkland Islands Development Corporation (FIDC), the economic development agency, which supports business development and has co-commissioned an Inward Investment Review recommending a dedicated investment framework
  • Simple, low tax regime: corporation tax of 21% on profits up to £500,000 and 26% above, with no VAT and no capital gains tax, and a double taxation agreement with the United Kingdom
  • Petroleum licensing by the Islands' government with a transparent fiscal regime: a 9% royalty and 26% corporation tax, with no signature bonuses or production-sharing contracts
  • Fisheries participation through the licensing system and joint ventures with Falklands-owned companies, which hold the controlling share of established partnerships with Spanish operators
  • Public procurement under a capital programme of about £295 million over five years, covering the new port, power generation, housing and infrastructure
  • Falkland pound at par with sterling, removing currency risk for sterling-based investors
  • Aquaculture entry subject to the Executive Council's in-principle decision on salmon farming, following public consultation closing on 30 October 2026

Market-Entry Observation

The Falkland Islands offer one of the highest levels of output per resident anywhere, prudent public finances and a growing pipeline of major projects. GDP rose 2.7% to £288 million in 2024 (3.6% in real terms), equal to £86,050 per resident, and non-resource GDP has grown by 3.5% a year in real terms over the decade to 2024. Fishing and aquaculture remain the core of the economy at 58.3% of GDP. Two investments are now reshaping the outlook: the Sea Lion oil development, where Navitas Petroleum and Rockhopper Exploration took a final investment decision in December 2025 with $1.8 billion of funding to first oil in 2028, and a £109 million replacement port due in 2028. The 2026/27 budget of £216.3 million sits within rules that bar borrowing for operating purposes and require reserves of at least 1.5 times operating expenditure. Tourism is broadening the base, with leisure visitors at their highest since 2007 and cruise arrivals forecast to rise from 65,197 in 2025/26 to more than 74,000 by 2028/29.

The Islands' main structural challenge is their reliance on fisheries, and on squid in particular. The second Loligo season closed early in 2026, the fourth consecutive year of difficulties, the Illex catch was below its ten-year average, and EU tariffs of 6–18% on fish exports cost the industry around €15 million a year. As a result, corporation tax receipts are budgeted to ease to £19.2 million in 2026/27 from an average of about £30 million. The response is a clear diversification strategy. Sea Lion royalties and tax are expected from 2028. The new port will enable fish transhipment and allow larger cruise and expedition vessels to berth alongside. A salmon farming industry is under consultation, and the Fisheries Department is preparing measures for the 2027 seasons. For investors, this creates opportunities in oil-related services, port and marine logistics, aquaculture, seafood processing, construction and nature-based tourism.

Investor Risk Note

The Falkland Islands combine a stable, rules-based administration, a currency at par with sterling, high income per resident and a transparent, low-tax fiscal regime. Their risk profile reflects concentration rather than instability: fisheries dominate output and public revenue, and the recent run of weaker squid seasons, together with EU tariffs, has tightened the budget. The government is responding with firm fiscal rules, savings measures and a capital programme focused on the infrastructure that underpins future growth. The most significant compliance consideration in 2026 concerns offshore oil. Argentina has announced sanctions against companies, shareholders, directors and suppliers involved in hydrocarbon activity around the Islands, an Argentine federal court has issued a provisional injunction against Sea Lion, and Argentina has applied to the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea. The UK government has said that Argentine domestic law does not apply within the Islands, and the operators state that their timetable is unaffected. Investors should take specialist legal advice, especially if they also operate in Argentina. Investors in tourism, fisheries services, construction and renewables face little exposure to these measures and can position for growth as the port and Sea Lion come on stream in 2028.

Market Access

The Falkland Islands' economy is export-led and closely tied to European markets. More than 90% of fish exports pass through the port of Vigo in Spain, which sources about a third of its imported calamari from the Islands, while toothfish is sold to the United States. The Falkland pound is at par with sterling, and a double taxation agreement with the United Kingdom simplifies UK-based investment. Since 2021, seafood exports to the EU have faced tariffs of 6–18%, and engagement on relief continues. Air access is provided by a weekly LATAM service from Punta Arenas in Chile, with a monthly stop in Río Gallegos, and by the South Atlantic Airbridge to RAF Brize Norton in England. Containerised sea freight links the Islands with Europe and South America. Their position makes them a natural base for South Atlantic fisheries, expedition cruising and Antarctic logistics, and the new port due in 2028 will expand capacity for larger vessels and transhipment.

Current International Business Channels

The Falkland Islands Chamber of Commerce and the Falkland Islands Development Corporation are the main local business channels, and together they commissioned the Inward Investment Review from Queenstown Strategic Advisors. The Falkland Islands Fishing Companies Association and the Loligo Producers Group represent the core fishing industry, which operates largely through joint ventures with Spanish partners based around Vigo. The Falkland Islands Tourist Board provides market data for hospitality investors. The Falkland Islands Government Office in London is the first contact for UK-based investors. Navitas Petroleum, which has teams in London, Aberdeen and Stanley, and Rockhopper Exploration hold public briefings on Sea Lion and are a practical source of supply-chain intelligence.

Investor Profile Best Suited

The Falkland Islands suit specialist investors comfortable with a small, remote and high-value market: seafood companies, processors and cold-chain operators building on one of the South Atlantic's leading fisheries; marine, port and logistics firms targeting transhipment and services around the new port; engineering and offshore supply-chain companies supporting Sea Lion, subject to legal advice on Argentine measures; aquaculture investors positioned for a decision on salmon farming; and expedition-tourism, lodge and hospitality operators serving nature and Antarctic visitors. Construction, modular-housing and renewable-energy firms can also find opportunities in the government's capital programme.
