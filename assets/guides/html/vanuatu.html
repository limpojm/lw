The economy today

Vanuatu combines a zero income tax regime with an open investment framework, a pegged currency and growing infrastructure investment, giving it a distinctive position among Pacific economies. The government estimates growth of 3.7% in 2025 and forecasts 3.9% in 2026, as reconstruction after the December 2024 Port Vila earthquake, resumed cruise calls and stronger agriculture and industry lift activity; the IMF's 2025 assessment was more cautious, at 1.7% and 2.8%. Inflation was just 0.8% in the December 2025 quarter, the Reserve Bank has held its policy rate at 2.75%, and gross reserves stood at VT82.6 billion in March 2026. The 2026 budget targets record recurrent revenue of VT46.8 billion, and the Nakamal Agreement signed with Australia in June 2026 provides A$500 million of support over ten years.

Vanuatu's main structural challenge is to broaden its revenue base and secure its links to the international financial system. Citizenship programme sales have been the largest single source of government revenue, VT10.4 billion by mid-2025, but the Reserve Bank identifies pressure on correspondent banking relationships as a major risk, and in September 2025 an Australian correspondent bank stopped processing transfers for citizenship agents. Vanuatu has been on the EU list of high-risk third countries for anti-money laundering since 2016, although the FATF removed it from its grey list in 2018. The government is strengthening the Financial Intelligence Unit and has a fiscal reform agenda supported by the IMF, while a review of investment restrictions, accession to the EU–Pacific interim EPA and infrastructure investment create openings in tourism, agribusiness and resilient construction.

How do I get in?

Vanuatu Investment Promotion Authority (VIPA), which promotes investment, provides facilitation and approves foreign investors under the Foreign Investment Act 2019

Most sectors are open: 15 activities are reserved for ni-Vanuatu citizens and 26 are restricted to conditions, with a review under way that may open mining and utilities to joint ventures

No corporate or personal income tax (a 0% rate), with a 15% value added tax as the main indirect tax

The vatu is managed by the Reserve Bank of Vanuatu under a basket peg, with reserves kept above a minimum of four months of import cover

Leasehold land access through negotiated leases with customary landowners, which should be secured with specialist legal advice

Public works and resilience projects co-financed by Australia, New Zealand, the ADB, the World Bank, Japan, the EU and the Green Climate Fund

Trade and market access

Vanuatu is a WTO member and a party to the Pacific Island Countries Trade Agreement and Melanesian Spearhead Group trade arrangements, and it ratified PACER Plus, the regional trade and development agreement with Australia and New Zealand, in October 2022. In October 2025 the EU Council approved Vanuatu's accession to the EU–Pacific interim Economic Partnership Agreement, which will give duty-free, quota-free access to the EU once both sides complete their procedures and provisional application begins. Exports are led by agricultural products such as kava, copra, beef and cocoa, with new lines such as powdered ginger emerging, while tourism, mainly from Australia and New Zealand, is the main services export. Port Vila and Luganville handle international shipping, and Bauerfield International Airport links Port Vila with Australia, New Zealand and Fiji.

The overall risk picture

Vanuatu offers investors no income tax, low inflation, a pegged currency and an investment regime open to most sectors, backed by a new ten-year A$500 million agreement with Australia and continued support from the ADB, World Bank, New Zealand, Japan and the EU. The main considerations are political instability, with frequent changes of prime minister and a no-confidence motion lodged in November 2026; reliance on citizenship sales for revenue; and pressure on correspondent banking, which the Reserve Bank describes as a major risk. Vanuatu remains on the EU list of high-risk third countries for anti-money laundering, so EU-regulated investors face enhanced due diligence, although it is not on the FATF grey list. Exposure to cyclones, earthquakes and volcanic activity is a constant planning factor and also drives a large reconstruction and resilience programme. There are no international sanctions restricting investment in Vanuatu.

Who should I talk to first?

Office of the Prime Minister (Prime Minister Jotham Napat)

Leads a five-party coalition formed in February 2025 after a snap election, and signed the Nakamal Agreement with Australia in June 2026

Primary reference point for national priorities and major projects

Vanuatu Investment Promotion Authority (VIPA)

Approves and facilitates foreign investment under the Foreign Investment Act 2019 and is reviewing the reserved and restricted lists

Standard first point of contact for market entry

Reserve Bank of Vanuatu

Key counterpart on banking, payments and financial-sector entry

Leads the budget, revenue policy, debt and development-partner financing

Relevant for tax, public-private partnerships and co-financed projects

Financial Intelligence Unit

Leads anti-money laundering supervision and screens citizenship applicants, and is being strengthened by the government

Important for compliance planning and the path off the EU high-risk list

Vanuatu Tourism Office

National tourism marketing body supporting the recovery of air and cruise arrivals

Partner for resort, cruise and aviation investments

Air Vanuatu

State-owned airline being stabilised, with fleet repairs and two new Twin Otters for domestic routes

Counterpart for aviation, tourism access and outer-island logistics

Business networks

The Vanuatu Chamber of Commerce and Industry is the main private-sector body and a channel for dialogue with government, while VIPA organises investor facilitation and promotional events. Australia, through the Nakamal Agreement, and New Zealand are the largest bilateral partners, alongside France, Japan, China and the EU, and their missions in Port Vila provide useful introductions. The Asian Development Bank, which in October 2026 launched a new five-year strategy covering Vanuatu and 11 other Pacific countries, and the World Bank finance infrastructure, resilience and private-sector programmes, and the Green Climate Fund supports adaptation projects. Regional forums such as the Pacific Islands Forum and the Melanesian Spearhead Group offer wider networks.

Who this market suits

Vanuatu suits investors comfortable with a small, island market who value a zero income tax base: resort, eco-tourism and cruise-related investors targeting Australian and New Zealand visitors; agribusiness and food processors in beef, kava, cocoa, coffee and spices; construction and engineering firms able to work on reconstruction, roads, airports and wharves funded by development partners; and renewable energy and telecommunications investors. Investors should have strong compliance systems and established banking relationships to manage correspondent-banking and AML considerations.
