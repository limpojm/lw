The economy today

Israel in October 2026 is rebounding strongly from a conflict-related dip at the start of the year. GDP grew 3.1% in 2025 (first estimate), and after a 1.5% annualised contraction in Q1 2026 during the war with Iran, output surged 15.4% annualised in Q2, led by exports, consumption and public spending; the Bank of Israel forecasts growth of 4.0% in 2026 and 5.5% in 2027. Inflation is subdued at 1.6% in September, within the 1–3% target range, allowing three consecutive rate cuts to 3.25% by September, held in October. Foreign direct investment reached a record $26.2 billion in 2025, 78% above 2024, and $14.1 billion in Q1 2026 alone, underpinned by a high-tech sector that generated about $85 billion of exports (58% of the total) and 18.3% of GDP in 2025. Foreign exchange reserves of $237 billion (34.2% of GDP) at end-September 2026 provide a substantial buffer.

Israel's main structural challenge is the regional security environment and its fiscal cost. Ceasefires with Hamas, Hezbollah and Iran have held through mid-2026, though implementation of the wider Gaza plan has been slow and the Bank of Israel continues to flag geopolitical uncertainty. Defence spending of around 6–7% of GDP has lifted government debt to 68.5% of GDP in 2025, with deficits expected to narrow from 2027. The economy has repeatedly shown its capacity to recover: the sovereign risk premium had returned to pre-October 2023 levels by July 2026, Moody's (Baa1) and S&P (A) affirmed stable outlooks, and capital continued to flow into technology at record levels. For investors, the combination of a deep innovation ecosystem, strong external buffers and a recovering growth trajectory offers attractive entry points, provided security and compliance considerations are built into planning.

How do I get in?

Invest in Israel, the national investment promotion agency within the Ministry of Economy and Industry, acting as a one-stop shop with sector project managers, regulatory navigation and after-care support

Law for the Encouragement of Capital Investments, offering reduced corporate tax of 16% for Preferred Enterprises and 12% for Preferred Technological Enterprises, falling to 7.5% in Development Area A, against a standard rate of 23%

Israel Innovation Authority programmes, including R&D funding and technology incubators for early-stage companies operating in Israel

Acquisition of, or minority investment in, Israeli technology companies, the route used by the record Wiz ($32 billion) and CyberArk ($25 billion) transactions

Establishment of a multinational R&D centre, a well-trodden model with more than 400 such centres in Israel, nearly two-thirds of them US-owned

Bank of Israel oversight of a freely traded shekel, with the central bank intervening only on a targeted basis to maintain orderly market functioning

Energy-sector participation alongside the Leviathan partners (Chevron, NewMed Energy and Ratio Energies) as the field's planned expansion and Egypt export contract progress

Trade and market access

Israel is a WTO member and OECD member with a long-established free trade network, anchored by the US–Israel Free Trade Agreement (1985) and the EU–Israel Association Agreement (in force since 2000). The EU is Israel's largest trading partner, accounting for 31.7% of its goods trade and 29.4% of its exports in 2025, and Israel's participation in the EU Open Skies agreement and a pharmaceuticals conformity agreement (ACAA) eases access further. The United States is Israel's closest economic partner, with two-way goods and services trade exceeding $50 billion in 2024 and US firms owning nearly two-thirds of multinational R&D centres in Israel. Exports are dominated by high-tech goods and services, while offshore gas links Israel to Egypt under a $35 billion supply contract running to 2040. Deeper regional economic ties offer longer-term upside as regional stability consolidates.

The overall risk picture

Israel's risk profile combines exceptional innovation strength and robust external buffers with a regional security environment that requires active management. The economy contracted during the early-2026 conflict with Iran and then rebounded sharply, inflation is within target, and record reserves of around $237 billion and investment-grade ratings with stable outlooks from Moody's and S&P underline its resilience. Ceasefires with Hamas, Hezbollah and Iran remain the key variable, and coalition talks after the inconclusive 27 October 2026 election will shape fiscal and institutional policy. Investors should also note clear legal parameters: under EU law, Israeli settlements in territories under Israeli administration since June 1967 are not part of Israel for trade purposes, so settlement goods are excluded from EU tariff preferences and must be labelled with their true origin; the EU has sanctioned violent settlers; Ireland and Spain have adopted national restrictions on settlement goods; and the EU is considering further trade options. Investors should take specialist legal and sanctions advice, conduct supply-chain and location due diligence, and focus on activities within Israel's pre-1967 lines, which provide full access to Israel's trade agreements and its world-class technology economy.

Who should I talk to first?

Prime Minister's Office

Sets national economic, security and foreign policy; coalition talks are under way after the 27 October 2026 election, in which no bloc won a majority

Primary reference point for strategic direction and major cross-ministerial projects

Invest in Israel (Ministry of Economy and Industry)

National investment promotion agency acting as a one-stop shop, with sector project managers and after-care services

Standard first point of contact for market entry, incentives and regulatory navigation

Bank of Israel

Independent central bank; sets the policy rate (3.25%), manages $237 billion of reserves and supervises banks

Relevant for monetary outlook, currency and banking-sector matters

Ministry of Finance (Accountant General, Chief Economist, Israel Tax Authority)

Manages the budget, sovereign debt issuance and the tax incentive regime, and tracks foreign investment flows

Key counterpart for tax rulings, Capital Investments Law benefits and public-private partnerships

Israel Innovation Authority

Government body funding R&D and incubators and publishing authoritative data on the high-tech sector

Essential partner for technology investors seeking co-funding, partnerships and market intelligence

Ministry of Energy and Infrastructure

Leads natural gas and power-sector policy; the government approved the Leviathan–Egypt export deal in December 2025

Key counterpart for energy, gas and power-sector investment

Ministry of Foreign Affairs

Manages relations with the EU, the US and regional partners, including EU trade dialogue

Relevant for understanding regional positioning and EU-related compliance developments

Business networks

The Israel-America Chamber of Commerce (AmCham Israel), the Israel-British Chamber of Commerce and the German-Israeli Chamber of Commerce are leading bilateral business bodies, alongside the Federation of Israeli Chambers of Commerce and the Manufacturers' Association of Israel for domestic liaison. Invest in Israel organises delegations, conferences and meetings with officials, while the Israel Innovation Authority is a hub for technology partnerships and sector data. With more than 400 multinational R&D centres and an active venture-capital community that raised nearly $15 billion in 2025, established foreign investors and local funds are a valuable source of deal flow and practical market intelligence.

Who this market suits

Israel best suits investors seeking access to world-class innovation: strategic acquirers and corporate venture arms in cybersecurity, AI, software, semiconductors and life sciences; multinationals establishing R&D centres; and venture and growth funds participating in a market that produced around $84 billion of exits in 2025. Energy and infrastructure investors can benefit from offshore gas expansion and regional export contracts, while manufacturers can use reduced tax rates in Development Area A. All investors should build security contingency planning into operations and apply careful compliance screening, particularly regarding EU differentiation rules for settlement-linked activity.
