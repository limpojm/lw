The economy today

Liberia in October 2026 is one of West Africa's steadier growth stories, anchored by a mining expansion and a disciplined IMF-supported reform programme. Real GDP grew 5.1% in 2025, up from 4.0% in 2024, as mining output rose 17% and exports climbed 31.5%; the IMF projects 5.5% growth in 2026. Inflation fell to 4% by December 2025 and is estimated at 4.5% in Q3 2026, allowing the Central Bank of Liberia to trim its policy rate to 16%, while gross international reserves now cover 3.3 months of imports, above the ECOWAS benchmark. The IMF completed its fourth review under the Extended Credit Facility on 28 September 2026, alongside a new $265 million Resilience and Sustainability Facility. Investment momentum is strong: ArcelorMittal's agreement has been extended to 2050 with a $1.8 billion expansion to 20 million tonnes a year, Ivanhoe Atlantic has secured a $1.8 billion rail-and-port access deal, and TotalEnergies and Oranto have taken up eight offshore blocks. Inward FDI reached $744.7 million in 2023 (17.1% of GDP), much of it in mining.

Liberia's main structural challenge is infrastructure. Electricity access is around 40% and only about one-fifth of the road network is paved, which raises costs for business; the end of major US aid in 2025 has also reduced grant funding. The government is responding with a clear programme. More than 63,000 new electricity connections were made in 2025, with 100,000 targeted in 2026; a 250 MW gas-to-power plant, a 200 MW solar plant with storage and a St. John River hydropower project are planned to take capacity above 700 MW; and a second Millennium Challenge Corporation compact focused on energy is in development. Roads are being paved through public-private concessions, and record domestic revenue of $847.7 million in 2025, a primary surplus and a planned move to VAT in 2027 are strengthening public finances. For investors, the infrastructure gap is itself a substantial opportunity in power generation, grids, roads, ports and logistics.

Where are the opportunities?

Monrovia and the Freeport of Monrovia

Capital and main commercial port, now operating around the clock after night navigation was restored in 2026; home to the Monrovia Freezone and Monrovia Industrial Park

Nimba County (Yekepa / Tokadeh)

Iron ore heartland, where ArcelorMittal's Tokadeh concentrator is lifting shipments from about 5 to 20 million tonnes a year in 2026

Buchanan (Grand Bassa County)

Yekepa–Buchanan rail corridor

Multi-user corridor being upgraded to carry up to 30 million tonnes a year, with Ivanhoe Atlantic set to ship Guinean ore from 2027 and an independent rail operator planned from 2030

Margibi County / Roberts International Airport

How do I get in?

National Investment Commission (NIC), the investment promotion agency administering the Investment Act of 2010, screening projects and granting incentives such as customs duty relief under the 2021 Tax Amendment Act

Concession and investment agreements reviewed by the Inter-Ministerial Concession Committee; contracts of $10 million or more are ratified by the Legislature and approved by the President, giving them the force of law (e.g. the ArcelorMittal agreement, extended to 2050)

Liberia Special Economic Zones Authority (operational since 2024) overseeing five zones, including the Buchanan Special Agro-Processing Zone, Monrovia Freezone, Monrovia Industrial Park and Roberts International Special Zone

Dual-currency system in which the US dollar and Liberian dollar are both legal tender, with 100% repatriation of funds and no currency exchange restrictions

Additional incentives for projects in economically deprived regions, those creating more than 100 direct jobs and those using at least 60% local materials, with priority sectors including agro-processing, energy, manufacturing, tourism, ICT and housing

Wholly foreign-owned companies permitted with minimum capital of $500,000 (or $300,000 with at least 25% Liberian ownership); land is held through leases, typically of 25 to 50 years

Petroleum entry through production-sharing contracts awarded by the Liberia Petroleum Regulatory Agency, supported by ICSID, New York Convention and MIGA membership for dispute resolution and risk cover

Trade and market access

Liberia is a member of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS), applying its Common External Tariff and giving preferential access to the wider West African market, and of the Mano River Union with Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire. It ratified the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA) in 2023 and has launched a national implementation strategy for 2025–2030, and it has been a WTO member since 14 July 2016. Liberia qualifies for duty-free US access under AGOA, which was renewed in 2026, and the United States is its largest export market (about 38% of exports), followed by Europe and Ghana. Iron ore moves by rail to Buchanan, and the Yekepa–Buchanan corridor is becoming a regional gateway for Guinean ore. Liberia also operates the world's largest ship registry, approaching 300 million gross tons and around 17% of global shipping capacity, which gives it unusual global maritime reach.

The overall risk picture

Liberia's risk profile is that of a fast-improving frontier market with strengthening macroeconomic anchors. Growth has accelerated to above 5%, inflation has fallen to mid-single digits, reserves have risen to 3.3 months of import cover and the IMF programme has stayed on track through four reviews, with a new Resilience and Sustainability Facility adding support. Long-dated, legislatively ratified agreements such as ArcelorMittal's to 2050 and Ivanhoe Atlantic's 25-year rail access give major investors a clear legal basis, backed by ICSID and New York Convention membership. The main practical considerations are infrastructure costs, land due diligence, contract enforcement and a still-developing banking sector, each of which can be managed through captive power, careful structuring, international arbitration clauses and DFI co-finance. The widespread use of the US dollar and full freedom to repatriate funds reduce currency risk for investors, and Liberia's peaceful democratic transition in 2023 and its election to the UN Security Council for 2026–2027 underline its growing stability and international standing.

Primary reference point for strategic priorities and flagship projects

National Investment Commission (NIC)

Investment promotion agency administering the Investment Act and incentives, now chaired by Morley P. Kamara

Standard first point of contact for market entry, incentives and investor facilitation

Central Bank of Liberia (Governor Henry F. Saamoi)

Sets monetary policy, manages reserves and supervises banks in a dual-currency system

Relevant for currency, banking and financial-services matters

Ministry of Finance and Development Planning (Minister Augustine Kpehe Ngafuan)

Manages the budget, the IMF programme, tax reform and the second MCC compact

Key counterpart for fiscal incentives, PPPs and donor-aligned projects

Ministry of Mines and Energy

Oversees mineral rights and the power sector, including the generation pipeline and mining concessions

Essential partner for mining, mining-services and energy investment

Liberia Petroleum Regulatory Agency

Licenses and regulates upstream oil and gas, including the 2024 direct-negotiation round

National Port Authority and Liberia Special Economic Zones Authority

Relevant for logistics, port services and zone-based manufacturing or agro-processing

Business networks

The Liberia Chamber of Commerce is the main domestic business association and an active voice on local content, while the National Investment Commission leads investor outreach. The EU–Liberia Business Forum held in Brussels in February 2026 showcased around €1.5 billion of opportunities in agriculture, energy, infrastructure, mining, fisheries and tourism under the EU's Global Gateway strategy, and Liberia sent its first-ever delegation to the World Economic Forum in Davos in January 2026. Development finance is an important channel: the US International Development Finance Corporation has more than $90 million in active commitments, the World Bank signed $125 million of infrastructure and electricity financing in June 2026, the AfDB financed the Buchanan agro-processing zone, and the Millennium Challenge Corporation is preparing an energy-focused second compact. Major operators such as ArcelorMittal and Ivanhoe Atlantic are also valuable sources of practical insight on mining logistics.

Who this market suits

Liberia suits investors with a frontier-market appetite and a long-term horizon: mining and mining-services firms positioned around the iron ore and gold expansion and the multi-user rail corridor; power developers and infrastructure investors targeting generation, grid and road concessions, often alongside DFIs and the MCC; agribusiness and agro-processing investors in rubber, palm oil and cocoa, supported by the new rubber processing policy and the Buchanan zone; logistics and port operators; and oil and gas services firms preparing for offshore exploration. The use of the US dollar and full repatriation freedom make the market particularly accessible for dollar-based investors.
