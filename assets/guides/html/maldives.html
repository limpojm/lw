Investor Entry Routes

  • Invest Maldives, under the Ministry of Economic Development and Trade, is the government's investment promotion and facilitation channel and handles foreign investment registration
  • Foreign Investment Act, ratified in September 2024 to replace the 1979 law, with Foreign Investment Entry Requirements classifying sectors as fully open, joint venture with local partners, or reserved, and transition periods of one to seven years for existing investors
  • Special Economic Zones Act, amended in November 2025: projects of at least $500 million, income tax of 5% for the first 10 years and 10% for the following decade, and import-duty exemption on capital goods
  • Maldives International Financial Centre, a planned financial free zone in Malé offering zero corporate tax, tax-free inheritance and no residency requirements
  • Resort and island leasing for tourism development, the country's principal and most established foreign-investment route
  • Maldives Monetary Authority oversight of the rufiyaa, pegged at MVR 15.42 per US dollar; since 1 September 2026, Category A tourism businesses must convert 40% of monthly gross foreign-currency sales through licensed banks

Market-Entry Observation

The Maldives in October 2026 is one of Asia's most successful tourism economies, with a record of more than 2 million visitor arrivals in 2025 and tourism earnings of about $5.6 billion. GDP grew an estimated 5.4% in 2025 (ADB), and the government has turned its attention to diversifying the investor base. The new Foreign Investment Act gives clearer sector rules; the amended Special Economic Zones Act offers 5% income tax for a decade on large integrated projects; and the planned $8.8 billion Maldives International Financial Centre aims to build a financial-services sector in Malé. The $585 million Velana airport terminal, opened in July 2025, has multiplied passenger capacity fivefold and supports the next phase of tourism growth.

The main structural challenge is external financing. Public debt stood at about 130% of GDP at the end of 2025, and the IMF and World Bank both judge the risk of debt distress to be high. The government has shown its commitment to meeting obligations: it repaid a $500 million sukuk in full on 2 April 2026, and Fitch upgraded the sovereign rating in June 2026. Partner support remains strong, with India rolling over its ₹30 billion currency swap from 23 October 2026. The Middle East conflict has slowed tourism, and growth is projected at about 1% in 2026 before recovering to 3% in 2027. Gross reserves were $686.8 million at the end of June 2026, while usable reserves were 23% higher than a year earlier. The IMF has noted improved resilience and easing FX shortages. For investors, the government's need for foreign currency and private capital creates openings in tourism, energy and infrastructure, provided projects are structured to manage currency rules.

Investor Risk Note

The Maldives offers a world-class tourism franchise, strong partner support and a government actively opening new investment channels through the Foreign Investment Act, the amended Special Economic Zones regime and the planned Maldives International Financial Centre. The country is not subject to international sanctions. The main considerations are financial: public debt of about 130% of GDP, thin reserve buffers and tight foreign-exchange availability. The authorities have responded with FX regulations, including the 40% conversion requirement for resorts in force since 1 September 2026, which investors must build into cash-flow planning. The full repayment of the $500 million sukuk in April 2026, Fitch's subsequent upgrade, India's continuing swap support and IMF recognition of improved resilience all show a commitment to meeting obligations. Investors with strong dollar revenues, sound local advice and prudent FX structuring are well placed to benefit as tourism recovers from the 2026 slowdown.

Market Access

The Maldives is a member of the World Trade Organization and of the South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC), and draws on the SAARC currency swap framework with India. Its free trade agreement with China has been in force since January 2025, and talks on a trade agreement with India have been reported. The economy is primarily an exporter of tourism services, with China, Russia and the United Kingdom the three largest visitor markets in 2025, alongside fish and seafood exports. The expanded Velana International Airport, with capacity for 7.5 million passengers a year, is the country's main gateway, and the Maldives' position on major Indian Ocean shipping lanes between the Gulf and East Asia supports its ambitions for a financial and logistics hub.

Current International Business Channels

The Maldives Association of Tourism Industry (MATI), representing most of the country's resorts, is the leading private-sector voice and a valuable source of insight on the tourism regulatory environment. Invest Maldives provides investor outreach and sector guidance. The Asian Development Bank, the World Bank and the IMF engage closely with the government on growth, debt and reform, and Gulf development funds from Abu Dhabi, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, together with the OPEC Fund, co-financed the new Velana airport terminal. India remains a principal financial partner through its currency swap support, and international hotel groups operating in the Maldives offer practical market intelligence.

Investor Profile Best Suited

The Maldives is best suited to investors with dollar-denominated revenues and a long-term view: international hotel groups and hospitality investors building on a world-class tourism brand; developers of large integrated resorts and real-estate projects able to meet the $500 million SEZ threshold; renewable energy and infrastructure investors addressing high fuel import costs and airport-linked logistics; and financial-services and fintech firms considering an early position in the planned MIFC. All investors should plan carefully for foreign-exchange conversion rules and work with experienced local advisers.
