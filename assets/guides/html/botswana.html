Investor Entry Routes

  • Botswana Investment and Trade Centre (BITC), the national investment and export promotion agency, with accreditation services and the Botswana One Stop Service Centre (BOSSC) for investor facilitation
  • No foreign exchange controls: profits, dividends and capital can be repatriated freely
  • International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) accreditation, offering a 15% corporate tax rate, no withholding tax on dividends, interest, royalties or management fees, and access to the double-taxation treaty network
  • Special Economic Zones Authority (SEZA), overseeing eight sector-focused zones from financial services in Gaborone to agro-processing in Pandamatenga and energy in Palapye
  • Tax incentives including a 22% standard corporate rate, a 15% rate for approved manufacturers, a negotiable tax holiday of up to ten years, duty rebates on machinery and inputs, and a 200% training rebate
  • Mining-sector participation through the Debswana partnership with De Beers and the Okavango Diamond Company, and through copper and other mineral licences in the Kalahari Copper Belt
  • National Development Plan 12 (to 2030) and the Botswana Economic Transformation Programme, which prioritise agribusiness, healthcare, renewable energy and technology

Market-Entry Observation

Botswana in October 2026 is returning to growth on the back of a diamond recovery and a broadening reform agenda. Real GDP grew 8.7% year-on-year in Q2 2026, the fastest pace since 2021, after 3.5% in Q1 and contractions of 2.8% in 2024 and 0.4% in 2025; diamond output rose 94.7% in the quarter, and services such as professional activities, hospitality and trade grew by more than 3%. The BITC facilitated P14.9 billion of investment in the 2025 financial year, including P5.52 billion of FDI against a P5 billion target, led by tourism and energy, and MMG's $936 million Khoemacau expansion underlines interest in copper. UNCTAD records net FDI of minus $654 million in 2025, while announced greenfield projects rose to 11 worth $938 million. Inflation eased to 7.8% in September 2026 from 10.7% in June after the Bank of Botswana raised its policy rate to 5.5% in April, and S&P affirmed Botswana's investment-grade rating (BBB-) in September 2026.

Botswana's main structural challenge is its reliance on diamonds, which provide about 70% of exports, a third of fiscal revenue and a quarter of GDP, at a time of soft global demand and competition from lab-grown stones. The country is responding from a position of institutional strength: foreign reserves have recovered to P63.2 billion (about $4.4 billion) in July 2026, their highest level since July 2024, with 7.3 months of import cover in Q1 2026, and the 2026/27 budget deficit has been revised down to 3.1% of GDP from the 8.9% forecast in February. The agreement in principle with De Beers extends Debswana's licences to 2054, raises the state-owned Okavango Diamond Company's share of output from 30% to 50% over ten years and creates a Diamonds for Development fund of up to $750 million, while Botswana is reported to be close to raising its 15% stake in De Beers to about 25% as Anglo American sells the company. Together with copper growth and the diversification priorities of NDP 12, these measures give investors a widening set of entry points beyond diamonds.

Investor Risk Note

Botswana's risk profile is that of a well-governed, investment-grade economy working through a cyclical downturn in its main export. S&P cites a strong institutional framework, prudent management of natural-resource wealth and a comparatively low government debt burden as core strengths, and the economy has returned to growth in 2026 as diamond production has recovered. The main risks are continued weakness in diamond prices and slower-than-planned fiscal consolidation, which are reflected in the negative outlooks from S&P and Moody's. These are being addressed through a sharply lower 2026/27 deficit estimate, rebuilt foreign reserves, a long-term diamond agreement with De Beers and a diversification programme under NDP 12. Investors should build diamond-price and interest-rate scenarios into planning and take advice on citizen-reserved activities, while the absence of exchange controls and Botswana's record of stable democratic transitions, including the change of government in 2024, provide a solid foundation for long-term investment.

Market Access

Botswana is a member of the Southern African Customs Union (SACU), giving duty-free access to South Africa, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini, and of the Southern African Development Community (SADC), and has been a WTO member since 1995. Its double-taxation treaty network covers South Africa, the UK, France, Sweden, Mauritius, Namibia, Zimbabwe and Russia. Diamonds, sold through Debswana's partnership with De Beers and through the Okavango Diamond Company, account for about 70% of exports, while copper is a growing second export stream. As a landlocked country, Botswana reaches the Atlantic through the Trans-Kalahari Corridor to Walvis Bay in Namibia, connects to Gauteng and Maputo by road and rail through South Africa, and links north to Zambia via the Kazungula Bridge, positioning Gaborone and Francistown as regional logistics and distribution hubs.

Current International Business Channels

BITC is the main facilitation channel and runs the Global Expo Botswana trade and investment fair, the Botswana Exporter Promotion Programme and the Botswana Trade Portal. Business Botswana, the national private-sector organisation, provides domestic business liaison. Major mining groups, including De Beers through Debswana, MMG at Khoemacau and Sandfire at Motheo, maintain a substantial in-country presence and are a useful source of practical intelligence on suppliers, skills and logistics. The IMF and World Bank are engaged on fiscal, financial-sector and business-environment reform, and De Beers' Diamonds for Development fund, of up to $750 million over ten years, is designed to support enterprise development outside diamond mining.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Botswana suits long-term investors who value governance, an open capital account and investment-grade credentials: mining and mining-services companies targeting copper in the Kalahari Copper Belt and the diamond value chain; renewable-energy, power and water developers supporting new mines and SEZs; agribusiness and food-processing investors using the Pandamatenga and Lobatse zones and SACU market access; financial-services and business-process firms using IFSC incentives in Gaborone; and tourism investors in the sector that led FDI facilitated by BITC in 2025. Investors should factor in diamond-cycle exposure, local borrowing costs and citizen-participation rules in certain sectors.
