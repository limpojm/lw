The economy today

Cabo Verde in October 2026 is one of Africa's most stable and steadily improving investment destinations. GDP grew 6.3% in 2025 after 7% in 2024, and momentum has continued into 2026, with growth of 6.4% in the first quarter led by investment (+15.9%) and services exports. Inflation is moderate at 2.7% in September 2026, the policy rate stands at 2.5%, and reserves cover about nine months of imports. Tourism welcomed a record 1.25 million hotel guests in 2025, up 6%, and contributed 4.1 percentage points of growth. CVTI recorded $1.9 billion of approved projects and memoranda in 2024, with tourism and tourist real estate accounting for around 90% of foreign investment. S&P upgraded the sovereign to B+ with a positive outlook in February 2026 and Fitch moved its B rating to a positive outlook in April 2026, citing fiscal consolidation and stronger external buffers. A new PAICV government led by Prime Minister Francisco Carvalho took office in June 2026 following an orderly democratic transition.

Cabo Verde's main structural challenge is its public debt, at around 100% of GDP in 2025, together with contingent liabilities in state-owned enterprises in transport and energy. The trajectory, however, is clearly positive: debt has fallen from a peak of 147% of GDP in 2021, the government ran a fiscal surplus of around 1% of GDP in 2025, and Fitch projects debt to fall to about 85% of GDP by 2027. Most of the debt is concessional, long-term and low-cost, and the escudo's euro peg, backed by Portugal, anchors macroeconomic stability. Reform of state-owned enterprises, airport and port concessions and a target of 50% renewable electricity by 2030 are opening space for private capital, supported by a €378 million EU Global Gateway package and a planned €100 million Portuguese credit line for companies investing in the archipelago.

How do I get in?

Cabo Verde TradeInvest (CVTI), the national investment and export promotion agency established under Law No. 41/2016, acting as a one-stop shop for investors

Investment Law (2012) giving equal treatment to foreign and domestic investors, protection against expropriation with market-value compensation, and the right to hold foreign currency accounts

Code of Fiscal Benefits for qualifying projects, including dividends free of tax in a company's first five years and a flat 10% rate for the following 15 years

Special Economic Zones: the Special Maritime Economic Zone of São Vicente (Decree-Law No. 17/2024), the Maio Special Economic Zone and the Special Economic Zone for Technology in Praia and São Vicente

Escudo pegged at about 110.27 escudos per euro under a credit facility agreement with Portugal; foreign exchange operations liberalised in 2018, with repatriation of profits and capital processed by Banco de Cabo Verde

Concession and public-private partnership route, illustrated by VINCI Airports' 40-year concession for the country's seven airports and the 2026 international tender for port modernisation

Investor-state protection through ICSID membership (2011), the New York Convention (2018) and bilateral investment treaties with 14 countries, including Portugal, Germany, Italy and China

Trade and market access

Cabo Verde is a member of the Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS), giving it a bridge to a regional market of West African economies, although it has postponed implementing the ECOWAS common external tariff. It has been a WTO member since 2008 and enjoys a Special Partnership with the European Union, whose GSP+ scheme provides preferential access for Cabo Verdean exports; the EU fisheries agreement was renewed in February 2025 for five years. Europe is the dominant market for its main export, tourism services, with the UK, Portugal, Germany and France the leading source markets, while Portugal supplies around half of goods imports. The euro peg simplifies trade and investment with the eurozone, and the country's mid-Atlantic position between Europe, Africa and the Americas supports its ambitions as a hub for maritime services, air connectivity and digital infrastructure.

The overall risk picture

Cabo Verde's risk profile is that of a small, well-governed island economy with strong institutions, a credible currency anchor and steadily improving public finances. The escudo's peg to the euro, low inflation, reserves of around nine months of imports and positive outlooks from both S&P and Fitch in 2026 provide a stable platform for investment. Public debt remains high at around 100% of GDP, but it is largely concessional and on a firm downward path, and the IMF has encouraged the new government to keep it so while addressing fiscal risks in state-owned enterprises. The economy's reliance on European tourism and imported energy is real, and is being addressed through diversification into the blue economy, digital services and renewable energy. The orderly change of government in June 2026 underlines the strength of the country's democratic institutions; investors should follow the policy priorities of the new administration and the presidential election on 15 November 2026 as part of routine planning.

Business networks

Cabo Verde TradeInvest is the central business channel, running regular investment forums, including the fourth Cabo Verde Investment Forum on Sal in June 2025 and an edition in Milan aimed at Italian and European capital, as well as the Cabo Verde–Portugal economic forum held with Portugal's AICEP in July 2026. The Chambers of Commerce of Sotavento (Praia) and Barlavento (Mindelo) are the main domestic business associations. Portugal is the leading identified source of new investment, and the Portuguese government is preparing a €100 million credit line for companies investing in the archipelago. The African Development Bank, the World Bank and IFC, Proparco and DEG are active in infrastructure, energy and private-sector finance, and the EU's €378 million Global Gateway package supports the blue economy, digital modernisation and the energy transition.

Who this market suits

Cabo Verde suits investors seeking a stable, euro-anchored entry point to the Atlantic and West Africa: hospitality and tourist real-estate investors building on record visitor numbers and dedicated tourism zones; renewable energy, storage and water investors supporting the 50% renewables target for 2030; ports, logistics, fisheries and maritime-services investors drawn to the Special Maritime Economic Zone in Mindelo; and technology and business-services investors using the technology zone and TechparkCV. Infrastructure investors and DFI-backed sponsors are well placed to participate in concessions and SOE reform, and diaspora-linked and Portuguese-speaking investors benefit from strong cultural and commercial ties.
