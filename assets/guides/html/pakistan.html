Investor Entry Routes

  • Special Investment Facilitation Council (SIFC), a civil-military apex forum including the Prime Minister and Chief of Army Staff, providing single-window facilitation and policy continuity in defence, agriculture, minerals, IT and energy
  • Board of Investment (BOI), the national investment promotion agency, which facilitates investors, runs the SIFC's day-to-day operations and leads the Special Economic Zone programme
  • Foreign Private Investment (Promotion and Protection) Act 1976 and Furtherance and Protection of Economic Reforms Act 1992, guaranteeing tax parity with domestic investors; most sectors carry no foreign-ownership cap
  • Special Economic Zones (44 notified) for export-oriented manufacturing, with BOI support on utilities and land-lease arrangements
  • State Bank of Pakistan foreign-exchange framework: profits and dividends are repatriable, with foreign investors remitting $2.31 billion in FY26; portfolio investors use Special Convertible Rupee Accounts
  • SECP Virtual One Stop Shop for company registration, and the Pakistan Stock Exchange for listed-equity and capital-markets entry
  • Sector-entry routes through power and renewables (the largest FDI recipient at $958 million in FY26), financial services ($806 million) and minerals partnerships with federal and provincial state entities

Market-Entry Observation

Pakistan in October 2026 is consolidating a hard-won macroeconomic stabilisation. GDP grew 3.7% in FY26, the fastest pace in four years, with manufacturing up 6.6% and large-scale manufacturing up 6.1%, while per capita income rose to $1,901. Fiscal discipline has been notable: a primary surplus of 3.2% of GDP in July–March FY26 and public debt falling to 68.5% of GDP from 75% in 2023. The IMF completed the third review of the $7 billion programme in May 2026, releasing more than $1.2 billion, and S&P has upgraded the sovereign to "B". Workers' remittances reached a record $41.6 billion in FY26, up 8.6%, and total liquid reserves stood at $26.9 billion in early October 2026. FDI of $1.64 billion in FY26 was lower than the previous year, but inflows into power and financial services remained robust and foreign firms repatriated $2.31 billion in profits, underlining an open and functioning repatriation channel.

Pakistan's main structural challenge is its exposure to imported energy and external financing, which the Middle East conflict of 2026 has tested. Higher global oil prices lifted CPI inflation to 10.26% in September 2026, after an average of 6.2% in July–April FY26. The State Bank has responded with steady policy, holding the rate at 11.5% against a medium-term target of 5–7%, and the external account has held up well: FY26 ended with only a small current account deficit, SBP reserves of $21.5 billion exceed the $18 billion end-June target, and Gulf-based remittances (Saudi Arabia $9.8 billion, UAE $8.8 billion) continue to provide a deep cushion. The same pressures underscore the opportunity in domestic energy, renewables, minerals and export manufacturing, all priority areas for the SIFC, where investment directly strengthens Pakistan's external resilience.

Investor Risk Note

Pakistan's risk profile reflects an economy that has moved from crisis management to stabilisation, with a primary fiscal surplus, falling debt ratios, rebuilt reserves, completed IMF reviews and a sovereign rating upgrade. The 2026 rise in inflation was driven largely by external oil prices linked to the Middle East conflict, and the State Bank's steady 11.5% policy rate and the depth of remittance inflows have kept the external account broadly balanced. Security conditions in parts of Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and the closure of the Afghan border since October 2025, are the principal location-specific considerations; they are concentrated geographically and can be managed through specialist security advice, careful site selection and phased project structures. Profit repatriation of $2.31 billion in FY26 shows that foreign investors can access their returns. Investors should plan for elevated local interest rates in the near term, while taking comfort from Pakistan's improving fiscal credibility and its sustained policy focus on investment facilitation.

Market Access

Pakistan has been a WTO member since 1995 and enjoys preferential access to the European Union under the GSP+ scheme, supporting its textile and apparel exports to major EU markets. It is a party to the South Asian Free Trade Area (SAFTA) and has a free trade agreement with China, which, through the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC), links Gwadar and Pakistan's road and rail network to western China. The United States is Pakistan's largest export market, taking around 20% of exports and generating a $2.86 billion trade surplus, followed by China (9%) and Germany, Spain and the Netherlands (around 5% each). Karachi and Port Qasim handle most seaborne trade, while overland links to Afghanistan and Central Asia offer longer-term upside once border trade normalises.

Current International Business Channels

The Overseas Investors Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OICCI), representing multinational investors, and the American Business Council of Pakistan are the leading foreign business associations, alongside the Federation of Pakistan Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FPCCI) and the Karachi and Lahore chambers for domestic liaison. The SIFC and BOI host investor conferences and country-specific business dialogues, including a China B2B investment conference in September 2025 that produced more than 160 MoUs and joint ventures. The IMF, World Bank, Asian Development Bank and IFC are active in Pakistan's energy, infrastructure and mining sectors, and the large Pakistani diaspora in the Gulf and the United Kingdom is an increasingly important source of investment and business networks.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Pakistan suits investors with a medium- to long-term horizon seeking exposure to a large, young consumer market and a stabilising economy: power and renewables investors, building on the sector's position as the largest FDI recipient; financial-services and fintech investors, with financial services attracting rising inflows; export manufacturers in textiles, apparel and sports goods using GSP+ access to the EU and Pakistan's leading position in the US market; IT and digital-services firms drawn by IT exports above $3.8 billion; and minerals investors prepared to work with state partners and specialist security planning. Gulf, Chinese and UK-linked investors with established ties to Pakistan are particularly well placed.
