The economy today

Luxembourg in November 2026 remains one of Europe's most stable and wealthy investment locations, combining strong public finances, euro-area and EU membership and the continent's largest investment-fund industry. Net assets of Luxembourg funds reached €6.44 trillion at the end of April 2026, up 14.6% in a year. Public debt is projected at 29.3% of GDP at the end of 2026, among the lowest in the euro area, and the government plans record public investment of €4.5 billion, 4.4% of GDP, in 2027. The draft 2027 budget presented by Finance Minister Gilles Roth on 7 October 2026 would cut corporate income tax again, to 15%, abolish the time limit on carrying forward losses and raise investment tax credits. Inflation eased to 2.7% in October 2026, well below many euro-area peers.

Luxembourg's main structural challenge is sustaining growth in a small, finance-led economy while diversifying and keeping costs competitive. GDP grew by 0.6% in 2025, and output rose 1.3% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026 despite a quarterly dip as investment and financial services slowed. High housing costs and reliance on cross-border workers add pressure, and defence spending is rising towards 2.3% of national income by 2029. The government's answer is a pro-business agenda: lower corporate taxes, record investment in infrastructure and housing, and support for new sectors such as space, data, health technology and sustainable finance. With low debt, a strong fund and banking base and the European Investment Bank on its doorstep, Luxembourg offers a secure platform for financial and technology investors.

How do I get in?

Ministry of the Economy and Luxinnovation, the national innovation agency, provide investor support, R&D grants and access to state-planned business parks

Corporate income tax of 16% in 2026 (an aggregate rate of 23.87% in Luxembourg City), which the draft 2027 budget would cut to 15% and 22.8%

Investment tax credits for digital and ecological or energy-transition investment, which the 2027 budget proposes to raise to 21% and 9%

CSSF authorisation for funds, management companies, banks and payment firms, with EU passporting from Europe's largest investment-fund centre

Euro-area membership: the euro is the national currency, with free movement of capital under EU rules

EU single-market and Schengen membership, with a highly international, multilingual workforce drawn from neighbouring countries

Luxembourg's space-resources law and national space agency, offering a legal framework for commercial space activity

Trade and market access

As a founding member of the EU, Luxembourg gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and to all the EU's free trade agreements, with WTO membership and the EU's common trade policy governing trade with third countries. Its financial regulator's authorisations carry EU passporting rights, allowing funds and financial firms based in Luxembourg to serve investors across Europe and beyond. Luxembourg is also part of the Benelux economic union with Belgium and the Netherlands and sits at the heart of the Schengen area, bordering France, Belgium and Germany. Findel airport is one of Europe's leading air-cargo hubs, and rail and road links connect the country with the ports of Antwerp and Rotterdam and with the wider European market.

The overall risk picture

Luxembourg's risk profile is among the lowest in Europe: a stable parliamentary monarchy and EU founding member with public debt of about 29% of GDP, a long record of policy continuity and a deep financial sector. The main considerations are economic rather than political: slow growth, with GDP up 0.6% in 2025, dependence on financial services and high housing costs. These are being addressed through tax cuts, record public investment of €4.5 billion in 2027 and support for new sectors. The CSV–DP coalition led by Prime Minister Luc Frieden has a clear pro-business agenda, and defence spending is rising steadily within NATO commitments. Inflation of 2.7% in October 2026 and an ECB deposit rate of 2.50% are moderate. There are no sanctions or legal restrictions specific to Luxembourg beyond those applying across the EU, which financial firms must observe in their client and fund operations.

Business networks

The Luxembourg Chamber of Commerce, the Luxembourg Business Federation (FEDIL), the Luxembourg Bankers' Association (ABBL) and the Association of the Luxembourg Fund Industry (ALFI) are the main business networks, with regular dialogue with government on tax, regulation and skills. The American Chamber of Commerce in Luxembourg and the British Chamber of Commerce for Luxembourg support international firms. Luxembourg for Finance and Luxinnovation run trade missions and investor events, and the European Investment Bank, headquartered in Luxembourg, is a major lender for infrastructure, innovation and climate projects across Europe. Embassies of major partners provide further introductions.

Who this market suits

Luxembourg suits investors seeking a stable, low-debt and highly international base at the centre of the EU: asset managers, private-equity and fund sponsors using Europe's largest fund centre and EU passporting; banks, insurers and payment firms serving the euro area; fintech, data and technology companies; space and satellite businesses using the national space framework; and research-led firms in health technology and materials around Belval. Industrial and logistics investors can also use state business parks and the Findel and Bettembourg hubs.
