Investor Entry Routes

  • Antigua and Barbuda Investment Authority (ABIA), established under the Investment Authority Act 2006, provides facilitation on licences and permits and processes duty and tax concessions for qualifying projects
  • Tourism and Business Special Incentives Act 2013 and small-business legislation offering duty, tax and customs relief for qualifying hotel, tourism and enterprise investments
  • Special Economic Zone Act 2015, providing for zones with dedicated customs regimes and exemptions from income, corporate and import duties
  • Citizenship by Investment Programme, with routes from a $230,000 National Development Fund contribution, $300,000 in approved real estate or $1.5 million in business investment, now with a 30-day physical residency requirement and regional regulatory oversight
  • Public-private partnerships for infrastructure, as demonstrated by the 30-year cruise port concession with Global Ports Holding
  • Eastern Caribbean dollar under the Eastern Caribbean Central Bank, held at a fixed peg to the US dollar and backed by reserves of 97.6% of demand liabilities

Market-Entry Observation

Antigua and Barbuda in October 2026 offers a stable, tourism-led economy with improving public finances and strong investor appetite. The IMF estimates real GDP growth of 3.0% in 2025 and projects 2.8% in 2026, while the government's 2026 Budget targets 5%. Inflation fell to an estimated 1.2% in 2025 from 6.2% in 2024, and public debt has dropped from 101% of GDP in 2020 to 68.3% in 2025, supported by a primary surplus of 4.9% of GDP, with the regional 60% target in view before 2035. Foreign direct investment reached about $282 million in 2025, or 12.4% of GDP, taking the inward FDI stock to $2.9 billion. Stayover arrivals rose 6.7% in the first quarter of 2026, with March the highest month on record, and a new cruise terminal, airport upgrades and the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in November 2026 add momentum. The Antigua and Barbuda Labour Party's re-election in April 2026, with 15 of 17 seats, gives clear policy continuity.

The country's main structural consideration is its reliance on tourism, which generates receipts of around 45% of GDP, and on Citizenship by Investment inflows, which reached 2.6% of GDP in 2025. Since 1 January 2026, US restrictions on new visitor, student and immigrant visas for Antiguan nationals, linked by Washington to Citizenship by Investment, have added a policy dimension, although visas issued before that date remain valid. The government has responded constructively: introducing a 30-day physical residency requirement, cooperating with US authorities on biometrics, joining a new five-country regional Citizenship by Investment regulator and seeking a formal review. Exposure to hurricanes is managed through adaptation investment estimated at around 1.6% of GDP a year and access to climate finance, including a $20 million Green Climate Fund resilience programme. This creates opportunities in diversified tourism, resilient infrastructure and renewable energy, where the country's 86% renewable electricity target (conditional on international support) points to substantial investment demand.

Investor Risk Note

Antigua and Barbuda's risk profile is that of a well-established, investor-friendly small island economy with improving fundamentals. Public debt has fallen by around a third of GDP since 2020, inflation is low, the banking sector reports non-performing loans of 3.4% and capital adequacy of 21.2%, and the Eastern Caribbean dollar's long-standing peg provides currency stability. The main considerations are the US visa restrictions linked to Citizenship by Investment, which the government is addressing through tighter residency rules, biometric cooperation and a new regional regulator, and the concentration of activity in tourism and exposure to hurricanes common to the Caribbean. These are mitigated by strong tourism demand, FDI-financed investment, climate-finance partnerships and a clear political mandate following the April 2026 election. Investors should monitor the US review of visa measures, build climate resilience into project design and plan for clearance of legacy arrears, while taking confidence from the country's track record of attracting high-end tourism investment.

Market Access

Antigua and Barbuda is a member of CARICOM and of the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States, whose Economic Union provides for free movement of goods, people and capital across member states, and of the Eastern Caribbean Currency Union. It has been a WTO member since 1995 and, through CARIFORUM, has Economic Partnership Agreements with the EU and the UK, which give duty-free, quota-free access for goods exports. UK–Antigua trade totalled £175 million in 2025, up 8% on 2024. Tourism is the main export: the US accounts for 46% of stayover visitors, Europe 34% and Canada 12%. Connectivity is expanding through new regional and African air links, the new Burton-Nibbs International Airport on Barbuda and a growing cruise schedule, with almost 895,000 cruise passengers forecast for 2026.

Current International Business Channels

The Commonwealth Business Forum, organised by the Commonwealth Enterprise and Investment Council with the government on 2–4 November 2026 during the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting, offers a timely platform for meeting ministers and investors around resilience, the blue economy, technology, finance and sustainable tourism. The Antigua and Barbuda Chamber of Commerce and Industry and the Antigua and Barbuda Hotels and Tourism Association provide local business liaison, and the High Commission in London supports UK-based investors. The Caribbean Development Bank, which administers UK Caribbean Infrastructure Fund grants, the Green Climate Fund and the UAE–Caribbean Renewable Energy Partnership Fund are active in infrastructure and resilience financing.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Antigua and Barbuda is best suited to hospitality and luxury real-estate investors, including branded-resort and residence developers drawn to Antigua's coastline and Barbuda's emerging ultra-premium market; marine and yachting businesses; renewable energy and resilient-infrastructure investors aligned with the 86% renewable electricity target and climate-finance programmes; and education, health and technology investors supporting diversification. Developers of Citizenship by Investment-approved projects benefit from a strengthened regulatory framework, and should build current US visa policy and full due diligence into their planning.
