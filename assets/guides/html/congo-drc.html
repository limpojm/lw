The economy today

The Democratic Republic of the Congo in October 2026 combines strong growth with markedly improved macroeconomic stability. The IMF reports real GDP growth above 5.5% in 2025, with construction, services and agriculture now broadening growth beyond mining, and the central bank forecasts 5.7% for 2026. Inflation has fallen to around 3.4% year-on-year (September 2026), well below the 7% target, allowing the Banque Centrale du Congo to cut its policy rate from 17.5% in December 2025 to 12.5% in August 2026. International reserves reached $8.18 billion (about 3.1 months of imports) in mid-2026, and the IMF completed its third Extended Credit Facility review in June 2026, bringing disbursements to nearly $1.03 billion. The country's inaugural Eurobond and new public-finance reforms mark its entry into international capital markets. FDI inflows eased to $1.87 billion in 2025 from $3.11 billion in 2024, while the FDI stock rose to $38.6 billion.

The DRC's main structural challenge is insecurity in the eastern provinces, where the AFC/M23 rebellion holds Goma and Bukavu, having briefly taken Uvira in December 2025 before withdrawing in January 2026. The conflict is geographically distant from the southern Copperbelt, where most large-scale mining investment is located, and diplomacy has advanced: the US-brokered Washington Accord between the DRC and Rwanda was signed in June 2025, a Regional Economic Integration Framework was agreed in November 2025, and the DRC and M23 signed a Doha framework agreement on 15 November 2025. Investors can mitigate exposure through site selection, security planning and supply-chain due diligence. As the peace frameworks are implemented, they open a sizeable opportunity in regional energy, infrastructure and responsibly sourced minerals, while new export routes such as the Lobito Corridor and the Banana deep-water port strengthen access to world markets.

Where are the opportunities?

Kinshasa

Capital and largest city; seat of government, the central bank and the main commercial, banking and consumer market

Financial services, construction, consumer goods, telecoms

Lualaba Province (Kolwezi)

Heart of the world's leading cobalt-producing region and Africa's largest copper output; venue of the central bank's October 2026 policy meeting

Copper and cobalt mining, processing, mining services

Haut-Katanga Province (Lubumbashi)

Second commercial hub of the southern Copperbelt and a key node on the rail link towards Zambia

Mining services, logistics, manufacturing, agro-processing

Lobito Corridor (Dilolo–Kolwezi–Lubumbashi)

Rail route linking the Copperbelt to Angola's Atlantic port of Lobito, backed by the US, the EU's Global Gateway and the G7 partnership

Rail and logistics, mineral exports, corridor infrastructure

Kongo Central (Banana and Matadi)

Atlantic gateway where DP World is building the country's first deep-water port, a $1.2 billion project targeting more than 450,000 containers a year

Ports, logistics, trade services

Inga hydropower site (lower Congo River)

Inga I and II have 1,775 MW installed, while the wider Grand Inga site has an estimated potential of around 39 GW

Hydropower, transmission, energy-intensive industry

Tanganyika Province (Manono)

Hosts globally significant lithium pegmatite resources and a historic tin and coltan mining district

Lithium, tin, battery-mineral exploration

How do I get in?

ANAPI (National Agency for Investment Promotion) provides facilitation and guidance for foreign investors and administers Investment Code incentives, recording 386 projects worth $13.56 billion in 2019–2023

Investment Code incentives, including tax breaks and duty exemptions linked to location, sector, jobs, training and exports, negotiated within a streamlined period of about 30 days

Guichet Unique (one-stop shop) bringing together the government bodies involved in company registration

Mining Code (2018) framework, requiring at least 10% Congolese ownership of mining companies, with ARECOMS administering cobalt export quotas of 96,600 tonnes a year for 2026 and 2027

Joint-venture and local-partner routes in sectors with national-ownership rules, including retail (reserved for nationals), agribusiness (49% foreign ownership cap), telecoms (25% national ownership) and subcontracting

Banque Centrale du Congo oversight of a largely dollarised banking system, with the Congolese franc broadly stable since late 2025 and reserves of about $7.8 billion at end-September 2026

US-brokered Washington Accord (June 2025) and its Regional Economic Integration Framework covering energy, infrastructure, mining, national parks, tourism and public health, with scope for partnership with US investors

Trade and market access

The DRC is a member of the Southern African Development Community (SADC), the Common Market for Eastern and Southern Africa (COMESA), the Economic Community of Central African States (ECCAS) and, since July 2022, the East African Community (EAC), giving it an unusually wide set of regional trade links, and it is a signatory to the African Continental Free Trade Area and a WTO member. China is the dominant export market, taking about 69% of exports in 2023, followed by the United Arab Emirates at about 7%. Copper and cobalt move south and east by rail and road through Zambia, and increasingly west along the Lobito Corridor, which links Kolwezi and Lubumbashi to the Atlantic and delivered the first DRC copper shipment to the United States in August 2024. The DP World deep-water port at Banana, targeted for completion in early 2027, will add a direct Atlantic gateway for the west of the country.

The overall risk picture

The DRC's risk profile reflects an economy with world-class mineral resources, strong growth and a clear improvement in macroeconomic management, anchored by an IMF programme, single-digit inflation, falling interest rates and rising reserves. Insecurity in the east is the principal risk and should be stated plainly: armed groups, including AFC/M23, control parts of North and South Kivu, and the peace frameworks agreed in Washington and Doha in 2025 are still being implemented. Investors should also note that the US, the UN and other jurisdictions operate targeted, list-based DRC sanctions on specific individuals, armed groups and some entities, so counterparty screening, sanctions counsel and OECD-standard minerals due diligence are essential, particularly for eastern tin, tantalum, tungsten and gold supply chains. These measures are not a general trade embargo, and the large-scale copper and cobalt operations of the southern Copperbelt remain open to lawful international investment. With sound compliance, early engagement with regulators and careful site selection, investors can position for a market whose role in global energy-transition supply chains continues to grow.

Primary reference point for the country's strategic direction

Lead investment promotion agency; administers Investment Code incentives

Standard first point of contact for market entry and incentive negotiation

Monetary authority; has cut the policy rate to 12.5% as inflation has fallen

Relevant for currency, banking and foreign-exchange matters

Relevant for public-investment, PPP and fiscal-incentive matters

Ministry of Foreign Affairs

Relevant for understanding the peace process and regional integration opportunities

Business networks

The Fédération des Entreprises du Congo (FEC) is the main national business federation and a practical source of local partners, and the annual DRC Mining Week in Lubumbashi is the leading meeting point for the Copperbelt mining community. ANAPI provides investor facilitation and incentive guidance. The IMF and World Bank are closely engaged on reforms, while the Lobito Corridor brings together the US, the EU's Global Gateway, the G7 Partnership for Global Infrastructure and the Africa Finance Corporation, creating a natural platform for infrastructure, logistics and mining investors. Major international mining companies operating in Lualaba and Haut-Katanga are a useful source of practical intelligence on logistics, power and regulatory processes.

Who this market suits

The DRC best suits investors with experience in frontier and resource markets and a strong compliance culture: mining and processing companies seeking long-term exposure to copper, cobalt and battery minerals; infrastructure, rail and port investors aligned with the Lobito Corridor and the Banana deep-water port; energy investors in hydropower, off-grid and transmission projects addressing the large power gap; and development finance institutions and impact investors able to support growth in agriculture, construction and services. Investors with exposure to eastern supply chains should apply OECD-standard due diligence and sanctions screening.
