The economy today

Romania in November 2026 combines EU and Schengen membership, a large domestic market and some of Europe's most significant energy projects with an investment boom driven by EU funds. Gross fixed capital formation rose 12.7% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026 and construction output 15.3%, as EU inflows support public investment of about 8.5% of GDP. Foreign direct investment inflows rebounded to €8.1 billion in 2025, according to EY, with 109 announced projects. The €4 billion Neptun Deep project, holding about 100 billion cubic metres of gas, is on track for first gas in the first half of 2027, with plateau output of about 8 billion cubic metres a year. Romania also signed a €16.68 billion SAFE defence loan in May 2026, and is in the final stage of joining the OECD.

Romania's main structural challenge is correcting the largest budget deficit in the EU. The deficit fell from 9.3% of GDP in 2024 to 7.9% in 2025, and the 2026 target is 6.25%, under an EU excessive deficit procedure. Tax rises, including VAT at 21% from August 2025, pushed inflation up and squeezed consumption, so GDP fell 0.4% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, although inflation has since eased to 6.6% in September. Politics have now stabilised: after the government of Ilie Bolojan fell in May 2026, Parliament approved Prime Minister Luca Niculescu's government on 20 October with 280 votes. S&P affirmed Romania's investment-grade BBB- rating in October 2026 and expects growth of 2.25% in 2027 as consolidation continues. For investors, this is a period to secure positions in energy, defence and EU-funded infrastructure ahead of the recovery.

How do I get in?

InvestRomania, the government's investment promotion agency, provides information and facilitation for foreign investors

State-aid schemes run by the Ministry of Finance, including a proposed 5.3 billion lei (about €1.05 billion) programme for new production facilities with investments of at least 50 million lei

A 16% corporate income tax and a 1% turnover tax for microenterprises with revenue up to €100,000; standard VAT is 21% and dividend tax 16%

EU single-market and Schengen membership since January 2025, with free movement of capital under EU rules; the leu is managed by the National Bank of Romania

EU co-financing through cohesion funds and the Recovery and Resilience Facility, with EU inflows expected to peak at about 3.5% of GDP in 2026

Defence-sector entry through the €16.68 billion EU SAFE loan, the second-largest allocation in the EU

Energy-sector partnerships with state and private champions such as Romgaz and OMV Petrom

Trade and market access

As an EU member, Romania gives investors tariff-free access to the EU single market and to all EU free trade agreements, with trade with third countries governed by the EU's common trade policy and WTO rules. Full Schengen membership since January 2025 ended border checks with Hungary and Bulgaria, cutting freight times to Central Europe. The port of Constanța connects the Black Sea with the Danube and Central Europe. From 2027, Neptun Deep gas will strengthen Romania's position as a regional energy supplier.

The overall risk picture

Romania's risk profile is that of an EU and NATO member with a fast-rising investment base and a clear need for fiscal correction. The deficit has fallen from 9.3% of GDP in 2024 to 7.9% in 2025 and is targeted at 6.25% in 2026 under an EU excessive deficit procedure, but S&P and Fitch keep negative outlooks on their BBB- ratings and S&P cites a current account deficit of about 7.5% of GDP. Consolidation has slowed growth and raised inflation, while the political crisis that followed the fall of the Bolojan government in May 2026 ended with the approval of the Niculescu government in October. Romania is not on the FATF grey list and has been a full Schengen member since January 2025. It borders Ukraine and Moldova, and EU sanctions on Russia apply in full. Investors who plan for tax changes and work with EU-funded and energy projects can benefit from one of the EU's strongest investment cycles.

Counterpart for defence manufacturers and suppliers

Business networks

The American Chamber of Commerce in Romania, the British Romanian Chamber of Commerce, the German-Romanian Chamber of Industry and Commerce and the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Romania are the main business associations, with regular dialogue with government on tax and regulation. The Foreign Investors Council represents major international companies. The European Bank for Reconstruction and Development, the European Investment Bank and the World Bank are active in energy, infrastructure and private-sector finance, and EU-funded programmes offer co-financing for qualifying projects. Embassies of major EU partners and the United States maintain commercial sections that are a useful source of introductions.

Who this market suits

Romania suits investors seeking an EU base with strong growth potential and lower costs: energy companies and service providers targeting Neptun Deep, renewables and grids; defence manufacturers positioned for SAFE-funded procurement; construction and engineering groups bidding for EU-funded transport and infrastructure; manufacturers in Timișoara, Brașov and other western and central clusters; and IT and services firms drawing on talent in Bucharest, Cluj-Napoca and Iași. Investors should plan for further tax and spending changes during fiscal consolidation.
