Investor Entry Routes

  • Tanzania Investment and Special Economic Zones Authority (TISEZA), launched on 1 July 2025 under the Tanzania Investment and Special Economic Zones Act 2025, merging the Tanzania Investment Centre and the Export Processing Zones Authority into a single digital one-stop facilitation centre
  • Certificate of Incentives and strategic investor status under the investment regulations, with a minimum initial capital of $500,000 for foreign-owned projects and joint ventures and relief on import duty for capital goods
  • Special Economic Zones and Export Processing Zones, now administered by TISEZA, offering tax and customs incentives for qualifying export-oriented and industrial projects
  • Bank of Tanzania oversight of a flexible shilling; profits, dividends and capital can be transferred through authorised dealer banks, while rules issued in March 2025 require domestic transactions to be settled in shillings
  • Sector-entry routes through production-sharing arrangements with the Tanzania Petroleum Development Corporation (TPDC) for oil and gas, and licensing under the Ministry of Minerals for mining
  • Public-private partnerships under the PPP framework, amended in 2023 to allow international arbitration for PPP projects in transport, energy and social infrastructure
  • Zanzibar Investment Promotion Authority (ZIPA) as the separate entry point for investment in Zanzibar's tourism, blue-economy and property sectors

Market-Entry Observation

Tanzania in October 2026 is one of Africa's most consistent growth stories, now underpinned by major new infrastructure. Real GDP grew 5.9% in 2025, up from 5.6% in 2024, lifting the economy to $91.8 billion on the rebased 2019 series, with financial services (+15.7%), electricity and gas (+11.8%) and mining (+9.4%) leading. Growth held at around 6% in the first half of 2026 and the government targets 6.3% for the year. Inflation was 4.3% in August, within the 3–5% target, and the Bank of Tanzania held its policy rate at 6.25% for Q4. Exports of goods and services rose 16.5% to $20 billion in the year to July 2026, led by gold ($5.67 billion) and tourism ($4.29 billion). FDI inflows rose 28.3% to $1.72 billion in 2024, and TISEZA registered a record 915 projects worth $10.95 billion in 2025. The 2,115 MW Julius Nyerere Hydropower Plant, inaugurated in August 2026, and a record year at Dar es Salaam port strengthen the platform for industry and regional trade.

Tanzania's main structural challenge in 2026 is rebuilding confidence after the October 2025 election period, when unrest was met with a heavy security response; a government commission recorded at least 518 deaths, and Western governments and NGOs have called for accountability. Economic activity reopened within weeks, and the macroeconomic framework has stayed on track: Tanzania reached staff-level agreement on the final reviews of its IMF programme in May 2026, domestic revenue rose to 16.8% of GDP in 2025/26, and reserves of more than $6 billion cover 4.3 months of imports. The Middle East conflict has added a second test through higher fuel prices, which the central bank has met with a timely rate rise while high gold prices keep the current account deficit near 2.5% of GDP. For investors, the opportunity lies in a large, fast-growing market whose energy, logistics and resource projects are moving towards delivery, approached with careful governance and reputational due diligence.

Investor Risk Note

Tanzania's risk profile combines strong and steady macroeconomic fundamentals with a political environment that requires closer attention than before 2025. Growth of around 6%, inflation within target, a current account deficit below 3% of GDP, completion of the IMF programme and moderate public debt of about 49% of GDP give the economy genuine resilience, and record gold and tourism earnings have cushioned the 2026 oil-price shock. The events surrounding the October 2025 election have led some risk assessors to revise their view of political stability, and Western governments have criticised the handling of the unrest; there are no international sanctions restricting investment, but investors should treat human-rights, reputational and ESG due diligence as a core part of entry. Practical mitigants include business-continuity planning, clear currency and payment terms, international arbitration provisions and structuring through TISEZA. With these in place, investors can access one of Africa's most dependable growth markets at a time when transformative energy and logistics projects are approaching delivery.

Market Access

Tanzania is a member of both the East African Community (EAC) and the Southern African Development Community (SADC), and a participant in the African Continental Free Trade Area, giving it a unique bridge between eastern and southern African markets. It has been a WTO member since 1995. The country's main export markets are South Africa, India and the United Arab Emirates, followed by China, Kenya and regional neighbours, with gold accounting for close to half of goods export earnings. Dar es Salaam port is a gateway for landlocked neighbours across East and Central Africa, with transit cargo rising strongly and inland links now strengthened by the SGR. The completion of EACOP to Tanga, surplus power from new hydropower capacity and growing transit volumes are deepening Tanzania's role as a regional trade and energy hub.

Current International Business Channels

The Tanzania Private Sector Foundation (TPSF) is the apex body for private-sector dialogue with government, alongside the Tanzania Chamber of Commerce, Industry and Agriculture (TCCIA) and the Tanzania National Business Council, which brings government and business leaders together on investment-climate reform. TISEZA runs regular investor forums and sector outreach, and in Zanzibar ZIPA plays a similar role. The World Bank, IMF, African Development Bank and IFC maintain substantial programmes in energy, transport and private-sector development, and international operators such as DP World and the LNG and EACOP partners provide valuable practical insight into large-project delivery.

Investor Profile Best Suited

Tanzania suits investors seeking long-term exposure to a large, consistently growing East African market: mining and mineral-processing investors building on record gold exports; energy investors positioned for LNG, EACOP-linked services and power trade from new hydropower capacity; logistics and infrastructure investors drawn by Dar es Salaam's record port volumes, the SGR and PPP opportunities; tourism and hospitality investors in the northern circuit and Zanzibar; and agribusiness and financial-services investors serving fast-growing domestic demand. Investors comfortable with emerging-market governance considerations and committed to strong ESG standards are best placed to benefit.
